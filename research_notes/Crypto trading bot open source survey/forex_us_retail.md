# Automated forex (spot FX and FX futures) trading for a US retail individual — state as of November 2026

Scope: US resident, small taxable account, fully automated bot. General information, not legal or tax advice.
Conventions: "RT" = round trip. "Not re-verified" = from researcher background knowledge, not confirmed against a source in this session; treat as a lead, not a fact. Pages that blocked direct fetch (IBKR, Schwab, eCFR, Essex repository) are cited from search-engine extracts of those pages and flagged.

## Key question 1: After realistic retail costs, which FX strategy families, if any, have evidence of positive expected return in the last 10 years, and how large is the edge relative to costs?

### Takeaway
No strategy family has clean, peer-reviewed evidence of a positive net-of-retail-cost return in G10 spot FX over roughly 2016-2026. The published record shows simple technical rules stopped working in major pairs by the early 1990s, currency anomalies (carry, momentum, value) lose much of their profit after publication, and diversified trend following had one strong year (2022) surrounded by flat years and its second-worst drawdown on record (May 2024 to May 2025). Intraday patterns (local-hours depreciation, fixing reversals) are real in academic data but nobody has shown them surviving a 1.2 to 1.5 bp retail round-trip cost.

### Cited Findings

#### Technical trading rules
- Neely, Weller and Ulrich (2009), "The Adaptive Markets Hypothesis: Evidence from the Foreign Exchange Market", JFQA 44(2):467-488. Out-of-sample tests of previously published rules: excess returns of the 1970s-80s were genuine, but profit opportunities "had disappeared by the early 1990s for filter and moving average rules"; returns to less-studied rules declined but "probably not completely disappeared"; high volatility prevents precise estimates. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:44:y:2009:i:02:p:467-488_09); [St. Louis Fed WP 2006-046](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Hsu, Taylor and Wang (2016), "Technical trading: Is it still beating the foreign exchange market?", Journal of International Economics 102:188-208. More than 21,000 rules, 30 developed and emerging currencies, 45 years of daily data, stepwise data-snooping test; finds "substantial predictability and excess profitability in both developed and emerging currencies", robust out of sample, but varying across subperiods and country groups; authors attribute it to temporary irrationality and market immaturity. The paper tests whether transaction costs eliminate profitability; exact net-of-cost figures were not retrieved. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/inecon/v102y2016icp188-208.html); [HKU repository full text](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/229640/1/Content.pdf?accept=1)
- Zarrabi, Snaith and Coakley (2017), "FX technical trading rules can be profitable sometimes!", International Review of Financial Analysis 49:113-127. 7,650 rules, six USD currency pairs, March 1994 to December 2014, false-discovery-rate control (Barras-Scaillet-Wermers), transaction costs accounted for. Headline numbers not retrieved (repository returned 503). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finana:v:49:y:2017:i:c:p:113-127); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/18805/)
- A PhD thesis by the same first author concludes profitability of technical rules is "purely due to luck" (thesis, not the journal article). — [Essex thesis](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/18630/1/Thesis.pdf)

#### Carry
- Lustig, Roussanov and Verdelhan (2011), "Common Risk Factors in Currency Markets", RFS: a carry factor (HML_FX) built from forward-discount-sorted portfolios explains most cross-sectional variation in currency excess returns; returns are computed net of bid-ask spreads on spot and forwards. Carry is framed as compensation for risk, not free alpha. — [NBER w14082](https://nber.org/papers/w14082)
- Burnside, Eichenbaum, Kleshchelski and Rebelo (2011), "Do Peso Problems Explain the Returns to the Carry Trade?", RFS: carry payoffs are large on average and uncorrelated with standard risk factors; a hedged version (options) still earns positive payoffs. — [NBER w14054](https://img.nber.org/papers/w14054)
- Daniel, Hodrick and Lu (NBER w20433): dollar-neutral G10 carry has insignificant abnormal returns; the dollar-exposure component carries the significant returns; hedging reduces but does not eliminate Sharpe ratios. — [NBER w20433](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20433/w20433.pdf)
- A 2025 University of Bayreuth dissertation reports the G10 carry risk premium "has been unexpectedly low since the global financial crisis". — [Hertrich dissertation 2025](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/id/eprint/8176/1/Hertrich%2C%20Daniel_Dissertation_2025.pdf)
- August 2024 yen carry unwind: BIS Bulletin 90 (27 Aug 2024) puts yen-funded carry at roughly JPY 40 trillion (about USD 250 billion), "probably" an underestimate; trigger was a weak US labour report on 2 August plus tighter yen funding; TOPIX posted double-digit losses on Monday 5 August; markets "erased losses within days". BIS warns leveraged carry positions force stronger reactions to shocks. — [BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.htm); [BIS Quarterly Review Sept 2024](https://bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409a.htm)
- 2025-2026 colour (no index series found): the yen was among the weakest majors against USD in 2025, attributed partly to continued attractiveness of USD/JPY carry (FTSE Russell); Commerzbank called Q1 2026 "a very good one on paper" for G10 carry traders; ING (May 2026) headlines "surprisingly low volatility keeps carry trade dominant". — [LSEG/FTSE Russell](https://www.lseg.com/en/insights/ftse-russell/fx-dynamics-shaped-global-government-bond-outcomes-in-2025); [Commerzbank via TMGM](https://www.tmgm.com/id/analysis/market-news/article/carry-trade-war-driven-gains-question-durability-commerzbank-202604161838); [ING Think](https://think.ing.com/articles/fx-daily-surprisingly-low-volatility-keeps-carry-trade-dominant/)

#### Momentum, value and post-publication decay
- Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling and Schrimpf (2012), "Currency momentum strategies", JFE 106(3):660-684: cross-sectional momentum excess returns "of up to 10% per annum"; momentum portfolios are skewed towards high-transaction-cost currencies; costs do not fully explain returns, but "effective limits to arbitrage" prevent easy exploitation. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:106:y:2012:i:3:p:660-684); [BIS WP 366](https://www.bis.org/publ/work366.pdf)
- Bartram, Djuranovik, Garratt and Xu, "Mispricing and Risk Premia in Currency Markets" (JFQA, online 11 Dec 2023; earlier SSRN title "Currency Anomalies", SSRN 3194107): currency anomalies are profitable in and out of sample before publication, before and after transaction costs, but "trading profits decrease substantially after the publication of the underlying academic research"; decline is larger for strategies with larger in-sample profits and lower arbitrage costs; signal ranks and alphas decay quickly. — [JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/mispricing-and-risk-premia-in-currency-markets/CB12427D463F25A14B5A4F20A1BB3FB4); [SSRN 3194107](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3194107)

#### Trend following (CTA proxies; multi-asset, not FX-only)
- SG Trend Index: 2015 flat (after -2.08% in December); 2022 +27.4%; drawdown of 20.4% from May 2024 through May 2025, "second largest drawdown since inception in 2000"; 16 drawdowns over 10% since 2000, roughly every 18-24 months; 2025 full year +2.39%. — [Cambridge Associates, 19 Aug 2025](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=19140); [Top Traders Unplugged (2015 and 2025 figures, from search extract)](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=20381)
- SG CTA Index 2022: +20.13% (record year). — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/flat-december-sees-ctas-complete-record-year-2022/)

#### Intraday patterns
- Breedon and Ranaldo (2013), "Intraday patterns in FX returns and order flow", JMCB 45(5):953-965: about 10 years of high-frequency data; currencies tend to depreciate during their own local trading hours; for EUR/USD this "can form a simple, profitable trading strategy". — [SNB WP 2011-04](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jmoncb:v:45:y:2013:i:5:p:953-965)
- Krohn, Mueller and Whelan (2024), "Foreign Exchange Fixings and Returns Around the Clock", Journal of Finance 79(1):541-578: USD appreciates before FX fixes and depreciates after, a W-shaped pattern, across the top nine currencies over 21 years; explained by dealer inventory risk. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:79:y:2024:i:1:p:541-578); [Bank of Canada SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2021/10/staff-working-paper-2021-48/)
- Elaut, Frömmel and Lampaert (2018), "Intraday momentum in FX markets", Journal of Financial Markets 37:35-51: first-half-hour return predicts last-half-hour return in RUB/USD (2005-2014), a market with explicit trading hours; driven by liquidity providers' overnight risk aversion. Not a major pair and not accessible at US retail dealers. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v37y2018icp35-51.html)

#### Market structure context
- BIS Triennial Survey 2025: OTC FX turnover averaged USD 9.6 trillion per day in April 2025 (preliminary; USD 9.5 trillion in the December 2025 Quarterly Review), up 27-28% from USD 7.5 trillion in 2022; FX swaps about USD 4 trillion; inter-dealer trading USD 4.4 trillion (46%). - EUR/USD 3-month realized volatility fell to 4.53% annualized, near a five-year low (Commerzbank, autumn 2026); ING had 3-month implied at 5.7% in May 2026. — [FXStreet/Commerzbank](https://fxstreet.com/news/euro-policy-risks-could-revive-swings-against-us-dollar-commerzbank-202611020632); [ING Think](https://think.ing.com/articles/fx-daily-surprisingly-low-volatility-keeps-carry-trade-dominant/)

### Inferences
- Edge versus cost arithmetic (researcher calculation): at 4.5-5.5% annualized volatility, EUR/USD daily standard deviation is about 0.28-0.35% (28-35 bp). A retail RT cost of 1.2-1.5 bp (see key question 3) is about 4-5% of one day's standard deviation. A daily-turnover strategy therefore needs a per-trade expected edge above about 1.3 bp, which is about 3.3% a year on notional at 250 trades just to break even. None of the cited papers documents a G10 edge of that size after 2015.
- Lower-frequency families (carry, trend, cross-sectional momentum) trade rarely, so spread cost is small relative to the premium; the binding problems are (a) retail financing mark-ups on overnight positions (rollover) eating the carry, (b) crash risk (August 2024, January 2015), and (c) the premium being a risk premium that is low or negative for years.
- The most defensible reading of the literature: any residual FX predictability is concentrated in emerging and less liquid currencies and in institutional-cost settings; US retail dealers offer mainly majors and minors at wider spreads, which is the worst overlap.
- Grid and martingale EAs convert a small negative expectancy into many small wins and one account-ending loss; the SNB episode shows the terminal loss can exceed the deposit.

### Gaps
- Net-of-cost headline numbers for Hsu-Taylor-Wang (2016) and Zarrabi-Snaith-Coakley (2017) were not retrieved. Not re-verified: both are generally read as showing declining profitability in developed currencies in recent decades and little persistence of individual rules out of sample.
- Park and Irwin (2007, Journal of Economic Surveys) and Brunnermeier, Nagel and Pedersen (2008, NBER Macroeconomics Annual, carry and crash risk) were not searched this session. Not re-verified: Park-Irwin report 56 of 95 modern studies with positive technical-rule results and flag data snooping; Brunnermeier et al. show carry returns are negatively skewed and unwind when funding liquidity tightens.
- No year-by-year G10 carry index returns for 2020-2026 and no FX-sector attribution for CTA returns were found; no SG Trend annual figures for 2016-2021, 2023, 2024.
- No academic study of news-trading or range-trading profitability at retail cost was found; no study of FX value at retail scale was retrieved.
- Whether intraday patterns (Breedon-Ranaldo, Krohn et al.) survive retail spreads after 2015 is untested in the sources found.

## Key question 2: What share of US retail forex accounts are profitable in a typical quarter, per the mandated broker disclosures?

### Takeaway
At the four dealer-model brokers that publish clean figures, 31% to 39% of active non-discretionary accounts were profitable in each of the last four reported quarters, so roughly two in three lose in any given quarter. Interactive Brokers reports 44-47%, and multi-year studies show the cumulative loser share is far higher (89% over four years in the French AMF study).

#### Academic and regulator evidence on retail FX traders
- Heimer and Simsek (2019), "Should Retail Investors' Leverage Be Limited?", JFE 132(3):1-21: the 2010 US leverage cap cut trading volume 23%, reduced high-leverage traders' losses 40%, and cut brokers' operating capital 25%, without changing relative bid-ask prices. — [NBER w24176](https://nber.org/papers/w24176); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:132:y:2019:i:3:p:1-21)
- Ben-David, Birru and Prokopenya (2018), "Uninformative Feedback and Risk Taking: Evidence from Retail Forex Trading", Review of Finance 22(6):2009-2036: past performance does not predict future success, yet traders raise trade size, size variability and trade count after gains; stronger for novices. — [NBER w22146](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22146)
- AMF (France, October 2014): 89% of 14,799 retail FX/CFD clients lost money over 2009-2012; average loss nearly EUR 10,900, median loss EUR 1,843; one report says transaction costs explained only 14.2% of losses. — [AMF newsletter Oct 2014](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/lettre_ou_cahier/lettre_observatoire/La%20lettre%20de%20l%27Observatoire%20de%20l%27epargne%20de%20l%27AMF%20-%20ndeg%2010%20-%20Octobre%202014.pdf); [LeapRate](https://leaprate.com/news/french-fx-and-cfd-traders-lost-more-than-eur-161m-during-2009-2013)
- ESMA (2018): national regulator studies show 74-89% of retail CFD accounts lose money, average losses EUR 1,600 to EUR 29,000 per client. — [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)

#### US rules that shape these outcomes and constrain strategy design
- Leverage: security deposit of 2% of notional for major currencies (50:1) and 5% for others (20:1); NFA can raise these (it set GBP at 3% at one point after 2015). — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html); [17 CFR 5.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-5/section-5.9); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/regulatory/nfa-changes-required-minimum-security-deposits-for-fx-transactions/)
- FIFO / no hedging: an FDM "may not carry offsetting positions in a customer account and must offset them on a first-in, first-out basis"; a customer may direct offset of same-size trades only against the oldest of that size. Does not apply to eligible contract participants. Known as NFA Compliance Rule 2-43(b), effective 2009. — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html); [NFA Q&A reprint](https://hedgefundlawblog.com/?p=2143); [OANDA on FIFO and EAs](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trade-tap-blog/trading-knowledge/the-nfas-fifo-rule-challenges-solutions-forex-expert-advisors)
- OANDA's own note: scaling in and out, averaging entry prices and hedging are all "vulnerable to the FIFO rule"; sub-accounts are the usual workaround. — [OANDA](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trade-tap-blog/trading-knowledge/the-nfas-fifo-rule-challenges-solutions-forex-expert-advisors)
- Capital: an FDM needs adjusted net capital of the greater of USD 20,000,000 or USD 20M plus 5% of customer liabilities above USD 10M (plus further affiliate charges). OANDA Corporation showed net capital of USD 32.8M and excess net capital of USD 5.6M at 30 Sep 2026. — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html); [OANDA disclosures](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/legal/regulatory-public-disclosures)
- No segregation claim: members "may not represent that forex funds deposited with them are 'segregated' or given special protection under the bankruptcy laws". Credit-card funding is prohibited. Dealers may not adjust or cancel executed customer orders except in narrow cases. — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)
- Offshore brokers: CFTC reports rising complaints about unregistered offshore forex dealers where deposits could not be withdrawn; it maintains the RED list of foreign entities that appear to need registration to serve US residents; registration is checked via NFA BASIC. — [CFTC advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/CustomerAdvisory_MustKnowForex.html); [CFTC release 8566-22](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8566-22); [CFTC check/RED list](https://www.cftc.gov/check)

### Inferences
- A sensible headline: "about one third profitable per quarter" at dealer-model brokers (range 31-39% over Q3 2025 to Q3 2026), with IBKR an outlier at 44-47%. IBKR's population is probably different (securities clients doing conversions and hedges, commission pricing) so it is not evidence that IBKR clients trade FX better.
- A quarterly win rate overstates long-run success: a one-third quarterly rate with little persistence (Ben-David et al.) is consistent with the roughly 9 in 10 cumulative loser share AMF found over four years.
- The FIFO rule rules out, at a single US account, any EA that holds simultaneous long and short tickets in one pair or closes tickets out of order (most grid, hedge-recovery and many MT4 marketplace EAs). A US bot must be written as a net-position system.
- The USD 20M capital rule and a retail pool of only about USD 470M explain why only six dealers serve US retail FX.

### Gaps
- Q3 2026 figures for FOREX.com and Schwab, and any 2025-2026 figures for Trading.com, were not found.
- No source was found confirming or denying a US negative-balance-protection requirement. FOREX.com's risk warning says a customer may "sustain a total loss of initial margin funds" and may need to deposit more ([FOREX.com](https://www.forex.com/en-us/terms-and-policies/disclosures-and-risk-warning)). Not re-verified: US rules contain no mandatory negative-balance protection (unlike ESMA/FCA), and several US dealers offer it only as discretionary policy.
- The exact text of the CFTC Regulation 5.5 risk disclosure (eCFR blocked) and of NFA Rule 2-43(b) was not fetched; the legal basis for the registration requirement (Commodity Exchange Act section 2(c)(2), as amended by Dodd-Frank) is stated from background knowledge.
- UK FCA loss statistics (commonly cited as about 82% of CFD clients losing) were not verified this session.

## Key question 3: What is the cheapest compliant way for a US retail trader to trade FX systematically (IBKR IDEALPRO versus OANDA versus CME micro futures), and which has the best API for unattended operation?

### Takeaway
For a small non-ECP US account, IBKR IDEALPRO is the cheapest quote but IBKR does not let US non-ECP clients open leveraged FX positions, so it is effectively a cash-conversion venue, not a place to run a long/short FX bot. That leaves two workable routes: OANDA (about 1.2 bp RT on EUR/USD spread-only, units of 1, the simplest unattended REST API) and CME micro FX futures through a futures broker (about 1.3 to 1.5 bp RT crossing a one-tick spread on M6E by researcher estimate, exchange-traded, Section 1256 tax treatment by default). Futures are the better structure once the account can carry 12,500-unit contracts; OANDA is better for very small size and the easiest automation.

#### US federal tax treatment (general information)
- Default: spot forex falls under Section 988, ordinary gain or loss. — [26 USC 988](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/26/988); [TaxAct (secondary)](https://www.taxact.com/support/14244/irc-section-988-cash-forex-foreign-currency-transactions)
- Election out (Section 988(a)(1)(B), Treas. Reg. 1.988-3(b)): available for forward, futures and option contracts that are capital assets and not part of a straddle; the taxpayer must clearly identify the transaction on its books and records "on the date the transaction is entered into"; a verification statement listing each election must be attached to the return; alternatively, a separate account with an unrelated broker whose statements carry the legend "Each transaction identified in this account is subject to the election set forth in section 988(a)(1)(B)" gives independent verification. Effect: capital gain or loss; if the contract is a Section 1256 contract, Section 1256 governs. — [26 CFR 1.988-3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.988-3)
- Practitioner view (secondary): the election is "internal" and contemporaneous, documented in the trader's own records before trading; with it, a trader can claim 60/40 treatment on major pairs if the trader "doesn't take or make delivery of the underlying currency"; Section 988 ordinary losses are not subject to the USD 3,000 capital loss limit. — [Green Trader Tax](https://greentradertax.com/trader-tax-center/tax-treatment/forex/); [TaxAct](https://www.taxact.com/support/14244/irc-section-988-cash-forex-foreign-currency-transactions)
- Form 6781: if an election is made under 988(a)(1)(B), Section 1256 contracts that are also 988 transactions are reported on Form 6781; 40% of the net is short-term (Schedule D line 4) and 60% long-term (line 11). — [IRS Form 6781 (2024)](https://irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/f6781--2024.pdf)
- OTC forex options are not Section 1256 contracts per IRS Notice 2007-71 (forum citation). — [Intuit community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/investments-and-rental-properties/discussion/re-spot-forex-trade-taxes-by-default-retail-forex-traders-f/01/2765123)
- A forum warning: some traders write an opt-out at the start of the year and discard it if they lose; the opt-out is expected to draw more scrutiny. — [Intuit community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/taxes/discussion/re-hello-i-just-open-a-forex-account-and-i-have-gain-and-loss-small-amounts-of-money-what-secti/01/2239219)

### Inferences
- Cost conversion (researcher arithmetic; assumes EUR/USD near 1.15, USD/JPY near 150, GBP/USD near 1.30, which were not verified for November 2026): 1 pip is about 0.87 bp on EUR/USD, 0.67 bp on USD/JPY, 0.77 bp on GBP/USD. So OANDA spread-only is about 1.2 bp RT on EUR/USD, 0.9 bp on USD/JPY and 1.5 bp on GBP/USD. Schwab and Trading.com at 1.2-1.35 pips are about 1.0-1.2 bp on EUR/USD.
- Commission accounts: USD 7 per 100k per side is 0.7 bp per side, 1.4 bp RT before any raw spread, so OANDA core and FOREX.com RAW are not cheaper than spread-only on EUR/USD unless volume rebates apply. If OANDA's rate is really USD 5 per side (the conflicting figure), core is about 1.0 bp plus raw spread.
- IBKR spot: 0.2 bp per side only applies above USD 100,000 notional; the USD 2 minimum makes a USD 25,000 ticket cost 0.8 bp per side (1.6 bp RT) and a USD 10,000 ticket 2 bp per side (4 bp RT) plus the interbank spread. For small tickets IBKR is not the cheapest, and the ECP rule means a non-ECP US client cannot short a currency or lever a position there.
- M6E estimate: about USD 14,400 notional at 1.15. Commission USD 0.15 plus exchange and regulatory fees (about USD 0.24-0.26, not verified) is roughly USD 0.80 RT, or 0.55 bp; crossing a one-tick spread adds USD 1.25, or 0.87 bp; total about 1.4 bp RT as a taker, about 0.55 bp if filled passively. That is comparable to OANDA as a taker and cheaper with limit orders, with no dealer financing mark-up (carry is embedded in the futures price) and quarterly rolls as the extra cost.
- Tax drives the structure: CME FX futures get 60/40 mark-to-market treatment by default with no election paperwork; spot forex is ordinary income by default, which is better for net losers (losses offset ordinary income) and worse for net winners. A bot designer should decide the venue with this in mind before the first trade, because the spot election must be contemporaneous.
- Best API for unattended running: OANDA v20 (plain HTTPS token, streaming prices, no desktop terminal, fractional units). IBKR needs TWS or IB Gateway kept logged in. MT5-based dealers need a Windows terminal kept alive. FOREX.com's REST API needs a manual access request.
- Spot FX has no consolidated tape, so every free history is one dealer's or one ECN's quotes. A backtest on Dukascopy or HistData ticks does not reproduce OANDA's or FOREX.com's spreads, weekend gaps or rollover-hour widening; the only honest test is against the execution venue's own candles plus its published spread.

## Key question 4: Is a retail-sized FX bot more or less promising than a retail-sized crypto bot, on the evidence?

### Takeaway
On the FX side of the comparison, the evidence is discouraging: FX is the deepest and most professionally arbitraged market (USD 9.5 trillion a day), the documented anomalies decayed decades ago or after publication, retail costs are 4-5% of a day's price standard deviation, and the vendor ecosystem (EAs, signal sellers, prop challenges) is mostly a fee-extraction business. FX offers better regulatory structure and tax treatment (via CME futures) than crypto, but less raw volatility and less evidence of exploitable inefficiency for a small systematic trader. This note covers forex only; the crypto half of the comparison must come from the companion research.

### Cited Findings

#### Volatility, hours, leverage, protections
- EUR/USD realized volatility about 4.5% annualized (3-month, autumn 2026), implied about 5.7% in May 2026; CME noted FX volatility at one-year lows across pairs in January 2026. — [FXStreet/Commerzbank](https://fxstreet.com/news/euro-policy-risks-could-revive-swings-against-us-dollar-commerzbank-202611020632); [ING Think](https://think.ing.com/articles/fx-daily-surprisingly-low-volatility-keeps-carry-trade-dominant/); [CME options report, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.cmegroup.com/newsletters/excell-with-options-report-from-rich-excell/excell-with-options-report-from-rich-excell-2026-01-28.html)
- Bitcoin 30-day implied volatility (Volmex BVIV): 35.59 on 10 August 2026 (lowest since September 2025), about 37-38 in late September 2026, after a spike to nearly 100 in February 2026 when bitcoin fell to about USD 60,000. — [FXStreet, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-volatility-is-in-meltdown-but-downside-protection-still-commands-a-premium-202608101104); [Equiti, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.equiti.com/sc-en/news/crypto-hub/is-bitcoin-volatility-remaining-contained-despite-market-uncertainty/)

- Leverage at US dealers is capped at 50:1 on majors and 20:1 on others; dealers may not describe customer funds as segregated or bankruptcy-protected. — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)

### Inferences
- Cost as a fraction of daily movement (researcher arithmetic): EUR/USD daily standard deviation about 0.3% (5% divided by the square root of 252); a 1.2 bp RT cost is about 4% of it. Bitcoin at 37% annualized has a daily standard deviation of about 1.9% (using 365 days); the same 4% ratio corresponds to a crypto RT cost of about 7-8 bp. Crypto is the relatively cheaper market only if the bot's all-in RT cost is below that; the companion crypto research should supply actual fee tiers.
- Because FX volatility is low, any FX edge is tiny in absolute terms and only becomes a meaningful dollar return through leverage; leverage is exactly what converts tail events (SNB 2015, August 2024) into account-ending losses. The US 50:1 cap and the Heimer-Simsek result suggest less leverage is the retail-optimal setting.
- Prop challenges are a poor route for an automated strategy: the US platform base is unstable (MetaQuotes 2024, cTrader 2026), most firms restrict or ban EAs and copy trading in their rules (not verified here), the trader is a customer paying fees to a mostly unregulated counterparty, and only about 7% of accounts ever see a payout.
- Structural advantages of FX over crypto for a US retail bot: registered counterparties with public capital and profitability disclosures, a cleared exchange alternative (CME), and automatic 60/40 tax treatment on futures. Structural disadvantages: FIFO and no-hedging rules, no evidence of durable inefficiency in the pairs retail can trade, five-day trading week with weekend gap risk, and a commercial ecosystem saturated with unverifiable EA and signal marketing.
- Overall judgement on the FX leg: a retail FX bot is best treated as an engineering exercise or a low-leverage risk-premium harvester (carry or trend via micro futures) with an expected return near zero after costs, not as a source of alpha.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was gathered on fake or manipulated Myfxbook track records, signal sellers, or the MQL5 EA marketplace; these are widely discussed but were not verified this session, and no regulator document specific to "forex robots" was retrieved beyond general CFTC fraud advisories.
- Whether the CFTC appealed the My Forex Funds dismissal, and the firm's operating status in late 2026, were not confirmed.
- Realized (not implied) bitcoin volatility for late 2026, crypto fee schedules, crypto leverage limits and crypto custody protections were out of scope and are not sourced here. Not re-verified: neither retail forex accounts nor crypto accounts are covered by SIPC.
- No study directly compares net-of-cost performance of retail systematic traders in FX versus crypto.
