## 1. Which backtesting libraries model fees, slippage, funding payments, partial fills and intrabar stop/target ambiguity correctly, and which are known to produce over-optimistic results?

### Takeaway
Only the order-book / event-driven engines (hftbacktest, NautilusTrader, and to a lesser extent LEAN) model queue position, latency, partial fills and (Nautilus) perpetual funding from data; the bar-based research libraries (backtesting.py, open-source vectorbt, bt, Backtrader, Freqtrade's default mode) fill at the requested price inside the bar's range with zero slippage by default and resolve stop-versus-target collisions by a fixed convention, not by data. Freqtrade and backtesting.py pick the conservative convention (stop first); the optimistic defaults that remain are zero slippage, guaranteed fills on touch, and (backtesting.py, open-source vectorbt) no funding model at all.

#### 1b. Execution-model facts by library

**backtesting.py**
- Market orders fill at the next bar's open, or at the current close with `trade_on_close=True`; `commission` is applied on entry and exit (rate, `(fixed, relative)` tuple or callable); `spread` is a constant relative bid-ask spread; `margin` is a single ratio with no separate maintenance margin; a `Backtest` takes one OHLC DataFrame; partial fills, funding and slippage are not described in the API docs — [backtesting.py API docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)
- The source contains no occurrence of the words "funding" or "slippage" (grep of `backtesting/backtesting.py`, master, 2026-11-23) — [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kernc/backtesting.py/master/backtesting/backtesting.py)
- Stop-loss orders are queued ahead of take-profit orders ("Ensure SL orders are processed first"), i.e. when both lie in one bar the stop wins; for stop-limit entries the code says "When stop and limit are hit within the same bar, we pessimistically assume limit was hit before the stop"; a gapped-through stop fills at the worse price, not the stop price — [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kernc/backtesting.py/master/backtesting/backtesting.py)
- The engine emits a warning when a contingent SL/TP would trigger in the same bar its parent order filled: "Since we can't assert the precise intra-candle price movement, the affected SL/TP order will instead be executed on the next (matching) price/bar, making the result (of this trade) somewhat dubious", referencing issue #119 — [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kernc/backtesting.py/master/backtesting/backtesting.py)

**vectorbt (open source)**
- `Portfolio.from_signals` / `from_orders` take `fees` (percentage), `fixed_fees`, `slippage` (percentage of price), `max_size` (orders "will be partially filled if exceeded"), `size_granularity`, `allow_partial`; `open/high/low` are "used solely for stop signals" — [vectorbt portfolio/base.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/portfolio/base.py)
- The file contains no occurrence of "funding" (grep 2026-11-23) — [vectorbt portfolio/base.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/portfolio/base.py)
- Stop handling note in the source: "We can execute only one signal per asset and bar... Stop signal cannot be processed at the same bar as the entry signal... we assume that any stop signal comes before any other signal in time. Thus, make sure to always execute ordinary signals using the closing price when using stop orders. Otherwise, you're looking into the future." `StopExitPrice.StopLimit` applies **no** user slippage; `StopMarket` applies it — [vectorbt portfolio/enums.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/portfolio/enums.py)
- The README states limit orders, leverage and futures contract multipliers are VectorBT PRO features — [vectorbt README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/README.md)

**Freqtrade backtesting**
- Documented assumptions: entries at the candle open; "All orders are filled at the requested price (no slippage) as long as the price is within the candle's high/low range"; stoploss exits happen exactly at the stoploss price "even if the low went lower"; "Low happens before high for stoploss, protecting capital first"; evaluation order within a candle is exit signal, stoploss, ROI, trailing stop; "backtesting will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode" — [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- `--timeframe-detail` replays lower-timeframe candles inside candles with active signals/trades to reduce intrabar ambiguity, at higher memory and runtime cost — [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- Futures: "Funding fees are either added or subtracted from the total amount of a trade"; where historical funding rates are missing a `futures_funding_rate` constant can be set, and "setting a false funding rate will mean backtesting results will be inaccurate"; "Freqtrade does not track liquidation fees, so liquidations will result in inaccurate profit/loss results" — [Freqtrade leverage docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/leverage.md)

**Backtrader**
- Slippage is off by default (`slip_perc=0.0`, `slip_fixed=0.0`); `slip_open=False` means next-open market fills are not slipped unless enabled; `slip_match=True` caps slipped prices at the bar high/low; `Close` orders get no slippage; the page says backtesting "cannot guarantee real market conditions" — [Backtrader slippage docs](https://www.backtrader.com/docu/slippage/slippage/)

**NautilusTrader**
- Bar data is expanded to four synthetic price points with bar volume split evenly; default intrabar path is `Open -> High -> Low -> Close`; with `bar_adaptive_high_low_ordering=True` the extreme nearer the open is visited first; "The adaptive path is a **deterministic heuristic**, not a reconstruction of the actual trade sequence... does not establish a general accuracy rate"; "the path matters when a protective stop and a profit target lie inside the same bar" — [Nautilus bar-execution docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/bar-execution.md)
- Note the default non-adaptive path (high before low) is the **optimistic** ordering for a long position with a stop and a target in the same bar (target reached first) — derived from the path definition in [Nautilus bar-execution docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/bar-execution.md)
- Fill model: `prob_fill_on_limit` (default 1.0: a touched limit order is fill-eligible) and `prob_slippage` (default 0.0: no one-tick slippage on L1); with L2/L3 books "the book itself determines price impact" — [Nautilus fill-models docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/fill-models.md)
- Queue position: `queue_position=True` with `trade_execution=True` tracks displayed quantity ahead of each resting order; "Only trade volume beyond the cleared queue is available to fill on that tick" — [Nautilus trade-execution docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/trade-execution.md)
- Latency: a `StaticLatencyModel` delays order arrival; without it an order submitted from `on_bar(N)` is processed after bar N's OHLC sequence — [Nautilus bar-execution docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/bar-execution.md)
- Funding: "Backtests settle perpetual funding from `FundingRateUpdate` data at funding boundaries"; "A positive funding rate debits long positions" — [Nautilus accounts-and-margin docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/accounts-and-margin.md)

**hftbacktest**
- "It focuses on accounting for both feed and order latencies, as well as the order queue position for order fill simulation"; features: tick-by-tick simulation, full order-book reconstruction from L2 market-by-price and L3 market-by-order feeds, feed and order latency models, queue-position fill models, live bot deployment for Binance Futures and Bybit (Rust-only) — [hftbacktest README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nkaz001/hftbacktest/master/README.rst)
- The README itself tells users to validate the backtest against live results before trusting it ("validated that your backtesting can accurately reproduce live trading results, then you can proceed to deeper research") — [hftbacktest README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nkaz001/hftbacktest/master/README.rst)

**LEAN**
- Pluggable per-security reality models: fill, slippage, fee, brokerage, buying power, settlement, short availability (borrow), margin interest rate, plus portfolio-level margin-call model; the docs say default models "assume you trade highly liquid assets" and high-volume or illiquid trading needs custom models — [QuantConnect reality-modeling docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/key-concepts)

**Jesse**
- README claims (maintainer statements, not independently verified): multi-timeframe/multi-symbol backtests "without look-ahead bias", partial fills (entering/exiting in multiple orders), leverage and short-selling, Monte Carlo analysis by trade-order shuffling and candle-based resampling, and "Rule Significance Testing" — [Jesse README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jesse-ai/jesse/master/README.md)

### Inferences
- Ranking by execution realism (highest first), from the documented mechanics: hftbacktest and NautilusTrader with L2/L3 data > NautilusTrader/LEAN on bars with configured models > Freqtrade with `--timeframe-detail` and real funding data > Freqtrade default / Backtrader / Jesse > backtesting.py and open-source vectorbt. This is an inference from documentation, not from a head-to-head benchmark.
- Libraries whose **defaults** bias results upward: any engine with zero default slippage and fill-on-touch for limit orders (Freqtrade, Backtrader, Nautilus `prob_fill_on_limit=1.0`, backtesting.py, vectorbt). Passive-order and scalping strategies are the most over-stated, because queue priority and adverse selection are absent.
- For crypto perpetuals specifically, backtesting.py and open-source vectorbt cannot be made correct without adding funding externally; a multi-day leveraged perp strategy tested there omits a cost that can exceed fees.
- backtesting.py's `trade_on_close=True` and vectorbt's default of executing signals at the close of the signal bar both assume the close is tradable at the instant the signal is known; this is the "unrealistic fill at the close" error and it is a user choice, not a bug.
- Nautilus's default (non-adaptive) OHLC path favours longs with bracket orders; anyone running bar data there should either enable adaptive ordering or use lower-granularity data.

### Gaps
- No independent, published head-to-head comparison of the same strategy across these engines against live fills was found.
- Jesse's fee, funding-rate and slippage handling was not verified (docs repo path not reachable in session).
- LEAN's crypto-futures funding handling, default slippage value and partial-fill behaviour were not on the page fetched.
- Zipline-reloaded, bt, QSTrader and PyBroker execution models were not inspected at source level.
- vectorbt PRO's pricing and its limit-order / leverage / funding modelling could not be verified (paid, private).
- Star counts are rounded shields.io values from one day; not cross-checked on github.com.

## 2. What are the common backtest errors (look-ahead bias, survivorship, repainting indicators, data-snooping from parameter search, unrealistic fills at the close, ignoring funding and borrow) and which tools help prevent them?

### Takeaway
The errors are well catalogued and each has at least one open-source mitigation: Freqtrade's `lookahead-analysis` and `recursive-analysis` for look-ahead and unstable/repainting indicators, point-in-time data layers (Qlib PIT database, Zipline bundles, TradingAgents' 2026 point-in-time fixes) for leakage, delisted-inclusive universes (FINSABER) for survivorship, and CPCV / PBO / Deflated Sharpe implementations (skfolio, pypbo, purgedcv, RiskLabAI, mlfinpy) for data-snooping. None of these is on by default, and the multiple-testing literature shows that an uncorrected best-of-many backtest is expected to fail out of sample.

### Cited Findings

**Look-ahead bias**
- Freqtrade: "Backtesting initializes all timestamps (loads the whole dataframe into memory) and calculates all indicators at once. This means that if your indicators or entry/exit signals look into future candles, this will falsify your backtest." The `lookahead-analysis` command "internally chains backtests and pokes at the strategy to provoke it to show lookahead bias" by comparing indicator values and moved entries/exits against the full backtest; it also supports FreqAI strategies — [Freqtrade lookahead-analysis docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/lookahead-analysis.md)
- vectorbt warns that with stop orders, executing ordinary signals at anything other than the close means "you're looking into the future" — [vectorbt enums.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/portfolio/enums.py)
- backtesting.py reveals `data` gradually inside `next()` so the last value is the most recent bar; the docs do not warn about `trade_on_close=True` — [backtesting.py API docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)
- FreqAI: targets are set once per backtest window to avoid look-ahead, "However, the definition of the features in `feature_engineering_*()` is performed once on the entire training timerange" (a documented leakage surface for user-written features) — [FreqAI running docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/freqai-running.md)
- LLM-agent specific: TradingAgents' own 2026 changelog lists repeated look-ahead fixes: v0.3.1 (Jul 2026) "Alpha Vantage look-ahead filtering"; v0.4.0 (Aug 2026) "look-ahead / point-in-time fixes across FRED macro, social sentiment, and the decision-log memory"; v0.5.0 (Sep 2026) "point-in-time integrity across every dated path, SEC EDGAR fundamentals served as filed"; v0.5.2 (Sep 2026) "backtests that see only data published by each analysis date"; v0.7.1 (Oct 2026) "a fix for model dates that could read filings made after the run date" — [TradingAgents README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/main/README.md)
- Qlib ships a point-in-time database (released 2022-03-10) — [Qlib README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/qlib/main/README.md)

**Repainting / recursive indicators**
- Freqtrade `recursive-analysis`: in backtesting the bot sees the full history, "But in a dry/live run, the bot will be limited by the amount of data each exchanges gives", so recursive indicators (EMA-type) can take different values live than in backtest; the command quantifies the discrepancy — [Freqtrade recursive-analysis docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/recursive-analysis.md)
- backtesting.py: rolling indicators are NaN-padded and the backtest starts only once all indicators are non-NaN, so results differ across parameter sets because of warm-up — [backtesting.py API docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)

**Survivorship and narrow universes**
- FINSABER (Li, Kim, Cucuringu, Ma; arXiv:2505.07078; listed as accepted to KDD 2026) uses historical S&P 500 constituent lists including delisted symbols over 2004–2024 and argues that testing only popular names such as TSLA and AMZN embeds survivorship bias — [FINSABER paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v4)
- Freqtrade: with dynamic pairlists "reproducibility of backtesting-results cannot be guaranteed" (today's pairlist applied to the past) — [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)

**Data-snooping / multiple testing**
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu, ". . . and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", NBER WP 20592 (Oct 2014), published Review of Financial Studies 29(1):5–68 (2016): a new factor needs "a t-ratio greater than 3.0" and "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" — [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 2017 (SSRN 2326253), propose combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) to estimate the probability that the in-sample best configuration underperforms the median out of sample — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [eScholarship copy](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- Bailey and López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality" (Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014) adjusts the Sharpe ratio for the number of trials, skewness and kurtosis — [eScholarship copy](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2329p290/qt2329p290.pdf)
- Harvey and Liu, "Backtesting" (Journal of Portfolio Management, 2015): a t-ratio of 3.0 is highly significant for a single test but may not be once multiple tests are accounted for; they derive a Sharpe-ratio haircut — [Harvey & Liu PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)
- Wiecki, Campbell, Lent and Stauth, "All that Glitters Is Not Gold: Comparing Backtest and Out-of-Sample Performance on a Large Cohort of Trading Algorithms" (Quantopian, March 2016; 888 algorithms): "the more backtests a user ran, the bigger the difference between IS and OOS performance"; secondary summaries report backtest Sharpe explaining only about 1–2% of out-of-sample variation (R² 0.01–0.02), versus R² 0.67 for volatility and 0.34 for max drawdown, and R² = 0.17 for a multivariate ML predictor — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies) (R² figures are from secondary summaries; the SSRN page returned HTTP 403)
- Freqtrade's own docs caution that its reported p-value assumes independent trades and that many tested strategies can produce low p-values by chance — [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)

### Inferences
- A practical minimum protocol assembled from the above: (1) run a look-ahead and recursive check on every strategy (Freqtrade commands, or shift-and-compare by hand elsewhere); (2) fill at next-bar open with non-zero slippage and real funding; (3) log every parameter set tried and report Deflated Sharpe / PBO on the full trial set; (4) use purged/embargoed or combinatorial purged CV for any ML label with overlapping horizons; (5) keep a final untouched hold-out and then forward paper-trade.
- The Wiecki et al. and Gort et al. results point the same way: selection on backtest Sharpe is close to uninformative, while risk statistics (volatility, drawdown) transfer much better. Rank candidates by robustness, not by peak return.
- LLM agents add two leakage channels classic tools do not check: (a) date-unaware data vendors returning restated or future data, as TradingAgents' fix list shows, and (b) the model's training-data cutoff overlapping the backtest window, which only live or post-cutoff evaluation removes.

### Gaps
- No open-source tool was found that automatically detects survivorship bias in a crypto pair universe; it must be handled by building a point-in-time listing/delisting table.
- The original Wiecki et al. PDF and the Deflated Sharpe SSRN page were not opened (SSRN returned 403); the R² figures are from secondary summaries.
- Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz, "A Backtesting Protocol in the Era of Machine Learning" (2019) and López de Prado's "Advances in Financial Machine Learning" (2018) are relevant standard references but were not fetched this session, so no claims are cited from them.
- Whether skfolio's `CombinatorialPurgedCV` supports embargo and overlapping-label purging as in AFML was not checked.

## 3. What does independent evidence say about ML / deep RL trading repos: do published results replicate out of sample and after costs?

### Takeaway
No independent replication was found showing that any of the popular ML/RL/LLM trading repositories produces profit after costs out of sample; the available independent evidence (FINSABER, StockBench, AI-Trader, Lu 2023, the Kaggle crypto contests, and the FinRL team's own overfitting paper) points to results that shrink or reverse under longer periods, wider universes, multiple seeds and realistic costs. #### 3b. Evidence on deep RL

- Gort, Liu, Sun, Gao, Chen, Wang, "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Cryptocurrency Trading: Practical Approach to Address Backtest Overfitting", arXiv:2209.05559 (first submitted 2022-09-12; v6 2023-01-31). Method: frame overfitting as a hypothesis test; 5 data groups with 2 validation groups give 10 combinatorial splits; 50 hyperparameter trials per agent; reject an agent if estimated probability of overfitting p >= 10% — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05559); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05559)
- Their reported PBO: PPO walk-forward 17.5% (rejected), PPO K-fold 7.9%, PPO combinatorial CV 8.0%, TD3 9.6%, SAC 21.3% (rejected). Test period 2022-05-01 to 2022-06-27 on 10 cryptocurrencies, 5-minute bars, 0.3% transaction cost, slippage explicitly ignored — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05559)
- **Every strategy lost money in the test window**: S&P BDM index −50.78%, equal-weight −47.78%, PPO walk-forward −49.39%, PPO K-fold −55.54%, PPO combinatorial CV −34.96%, TD3 −59.08%, SAC −59.48%. The "less overfitted agents have a higher return" claim means "lost less" over one 58-day crash window — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05559)
- Internal inconsistency within that paper: TD3 passed the overfitting test (9.6% < 10%) yet returned −59.08%, worse than the rejected PPO walk-forward agent (−49.39%) — figures from [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05559)
- The FinRL_Crypto README advertises the approach as "proven to be more profitable than the competition" — [FinRL_Crypto README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AI4Finance-Foundation/FinRL_Crypto/master/README.md)
- Lu, C. I., "Evaluation of Deep Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Portfolio Optimisation", arXiv:2307.07694 (2023, v3 Aug 2025): on **simulated** correlated-GBM prices where the optimal (Kelly) policy is known, off-policy DDPG, TD3 and SAC "performed poorly" because of noisy rewards; PPO and A2C got close to optimal but needed more than 2 million steps, which the author equates to roughly 8,000 years of daily data, concluding sample efficiency is too low for real data — [arXiv 2307.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694)

### Inferences
- The best-in-class correlation for short-horizon crypto return prediction in a professionally run contest is on the order of 0.13 (DRW winner, single model). A correlation that small can be valuable to a low-latency, low-fee market maker but is unlikely to cover retail taker fees and slippage; neither contest scored after-cost PnL, so these results do not demonstrate a tradable edge for a retail bot.
- In both Kaggle contests gradient-boosted trees or small MLPs with heavy feature engineering won; nothing indicates that deep RL or LLMs are competitive on pure price/order-book forecasting.
- The Gort et al. paper is frequently cited as evidence that FinRL-style agents "work" in crypto. Read carefully, it shows the opposite of a profitable result: a 35% loss in two months for the best agent, with slippage ignored, on one window. Its durable contribution is the methodology (estimate PBO, reject agents), not the returns.
- Star counts are not evidence of efficacy: the three most-starred LLM trading repos (TradingAgents, ai-hedge-fund, FinGPT) all disclaim real trading, and the only independent long-horizon test (FINSABER) could not even include TradingAgents because no code was available at the time.
- For a crypto bot project, the defensible uses of these repos are: FreqAI or Qlib-style supervised models with purged CV as one signal among several; ML4T (3rd ed.) as a process template; FinRL/TensorTrade/gym-anytrading for learning only.

- No independent, multi-seed replication of the FinRL tutorial results (Dow 30 ensemble etc.) was found.
- No independent replication of RD-Agent(Q)'s "2× ARR" claim or of Qlib's benchmark table was found.
- No after-cost, out-of-sample evaluation of FinGPT-Forecaster, FinRobot or ai-hedge-fund was found.
- FreqAI: no systematic evidence either way on live profitability; only documentation was reviewed.
- G-Research final private scores and the full DRW final leaderboard/shake-up were not retrieved (Kaggle pages not opened directly).
- The TradingAgents star count (124k) comes from a single shields.io read and should be re-checked on GitHub.

## 4. What actually happened in live LLM / AI agent trading contests in 2025-2026: returns, drawdowns, fees paid, and what conclusions organisers and independent analysts drew?

### Takeaway
In the real-money contests the median LLM lost money: Alpha Arena Season 1 (crypto perps on Hyperliquid, Oct–Nov 2025) ended with two of six models up and the field down roughly 30% in 17 days, and Season 1.5 (US equities, Nov–Dec 2025) saw only 6 of 32 result sets positive with the combined book down about one-third. Organisers and independent reviewers agree the runs are too short and noisy to rank models, that overtrading and fees dominated outcomes, and, in the Nof1 founder's words, that LLMs "can't really make money by themselves".

### Cited Findings

**Alpha Arena Season 1 (Nof1)**
- Format: six models (Qwen3 Max, DeepSeek V3.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, GPT-5), USD 10,000 real money each, crypto perpetuals on Hyperliquid (BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, DOGE, XRP), no human intervention, decisions roughly every 2–3 minutes, 18 October to 3 November 2025 — [Moby guide](https://moby.win/learn/alpha-arena/); [GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/)

**Academic live / contamination-free benchmarks**
- StockBench (Chen et al., arXiv:2510.02209, Oct 2025; v2 Mar 2026): "most models struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline", while some show potential for higher returns and better risk management; strength on static financial QA does not translate into trading — [arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209)
- AI-Trader (Fan et al., HKUDS, arXiv:2512.10971, Dec 2025): six LLMs across US stocks, A-shares and crypto; "general intelligence does not automatically translate to effective trading capability"; most agents show poor returns and weak risk management; risk control determines cross-market robustness — [arXiv 2512.10971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10971)
- LiveTradeBench (Yu, Li, You, UIUC, arXiv:2511.03628, Nov 2025): 21 LLMs, 50-day live evaluation on US stocks and Polymarket; high LMArena scores do not imply better trading outcomes — [arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)

### Inferences
- The consistent failure mode is behavioural, not predictive: too many trades, poor sizing, and leverage. In Season 1.5 the winning model traded about one-ninth as often as the most active one under an identical prompt. Any LLM-driven bot needs hard trade-frequency, sizing and leverage limits outside the model.
- Fees and funding on perps scale with turnover and leverage; with decisions every 2–3 minutes and leveraged notional, the cost drag reported by Nof1 is what theory predicts. An LLM loop is best run at low frequency (hours to days) if used at all.
- Seventeen-day and two-week windows with six to eight contestants cannot distinguish skill from luck; the Season 1 winner (Qwen) was among the heavy-leverage, overtrading models in Season 1.5 per Briefs. Season rankings should not drive model choice.
- Evidence versus marketing: balances reported by news sites and Nof1's own qualitative write-up count as weak evidence; third-party "fee" and "Sharpe" tables that contradict each other, and platform self-promotion (TradeRank, NOFX), do not.

- StockBench, AI-Trader and LiveTradeBench numeric return tables were not extracted (abstracts only).
- "Agent Market Arena" and other benchmarks named in secondary lists were not located.

## 5. Which libraries are fast enough for large parameter sweeps and intraday data (polars / numba / rust based)?

### Takeaway
For brute-force parameter sweeps on bar data, vectorbt (NumPy + Numba, with an optional Rust engine since April 2026) is the purpose-built tool; for tick/order-book data the Rust-core engines (NautilusTrader, hftbacktest) are the realistic choices; Jesse, PyBroker and Freqtrade sit in the middle. No independent 2026 benchmark comparing them was found, so speed claims below are architectural facts plus maintainer statements.

### Cited Findings
- vectorbt: "NumPy arrays, Numba acceleration, and an optional Rust engine let you run large experiments in a few lines of Python"; install via `pip install -U "vectorbt[rust]"`; parallelisation, random search and conditional parameters are listed as PRO features — [vectorbt README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/README.md)
- `vectorbt-rust` first appeared on PyPI on 2026-04-22 (1.0.0); latest 1.2.1 on 2026-11-11; licence "Apache-2.0 WITH Commons-Clause" — [PyPI vectorbt-rust](https://pypi.org/project/vectorbt-rust/)
- hftbacktest: Python API JIT-compiled with Numba plus a Rust implementation; tick-by-tick with L2/L3 reconstruction; the README notes "sometimes fully accurate backtesting is not practical due to the time it requires" — [hftbacktest README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nkaz001/hftbacktest/master/README.rst)
- NautilusTrader: "Rust-native engine"; "Fast: Rust core with the mimalloc allocator and asynchronous networking using tokio"; backtests over quote ticks, trade ticks, bars, order book and custom data "with nanosecond resolution"; Python bindings via PyO3; strategies can also be written entirely in Rust — [NautilusTrader README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/README.md)
- Jesse: optimisation with Optuna and parallel processing via Ray; "Rust-Powered Indicators: Native Rust implementations make indicator-heavy strategies and large research runs substantially faster" (maintainer claim) — [Jesse README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jesse-ai/jesse/master/README.md)
- PyBroker: "A super-fast backtesting engine built in NumPy and accelerated with Numba"; caching of data, indicators and models — [PyBroker README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/edtechre/pybroker/master/README.md)
- backtesting.py advertises "Blazing fast execution"; `optimize()` supports exhaustive or randomised grid and model-based SAMBO search with heatmaps — [backtesting.py README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kernc/backtesting.py/master/README.md); [API docs](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html)
- Freqtrade: `--timeframe-detail` increases memory and runtime; FreqAI backtests train one model per window per pair (a 10-day range with `backtest_period_days=0.1` means 100 models per pair) — [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/); [FreqAI running docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/freqai-running.md)
- LEAN: C# engine with local Docker-based `lean backtest` and `lean optimize` commands — [LEAN README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QuantConnect/Lean/master/readme.md)
- Indicators: TA-Lib Python wrapper 0.8.3.post1 (2026-11-21) over the C core — [PyPI TA-Lib](https://pypi.org/project/TA-Lib/); `polars-talib` 0.2.1 on 2026-10-18 — [PyPI polars-talib](https://pypi.org/project/polars-talib/); pandas-ta-classic claims optional Numba compilation makes recursive indicators "6–200× faster on 100,000-bar series" (self-reported) — [pandas-ta-classic README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xgboosted/pandas-ta-classic/main/README.md)
- Polars itself is at 2.1.0 (2026-10-25) — [PyPI polars](https://pypi.org/project/polars/)
- Backtrader (pure Python, last release April 2023) — [PyPI backtrader](https://pypi.org/project/backtrader/)

### Inferences
- A sensible two-stage workflow: sweep thousands of parameter sets in vectorbt (or polars + numba by hand) with pessimistic fees/slippage to discard ideas, then re-test the few survivors in an event-driven engine with realistic fills (NautilusTrader, or Freqtrade with `--timeframe-detail`). Fast sweeps multiply the number of trials, so they must be paired with the Deflated Sharpe / PBO corrections from section 2.
- None of the surveyed backtesters is polars-native; polars is used for data preparation and indicators (polars-talib), with Numba or Rust doing the simulation loop.
- Pure-Python event loops (Backtrader, QSTrader, PyAlgoTrade, bt) are the slowest class and are a poor fit for 1-minute multi-year sweeps.

### Gaps
- No independent, current benchmark (bars per second, memory) across vectorbt, Nautilus, hftbacktest, Jesse, backtesting.py and Freqtrade was found; all speed statements above are qualitative.
- vectorbt-rust's speed-up over the Numba path was not quantified from a primary source.
- Whether any maintained library is built directly on polars for backtesting was not established.

## 6. Which licences restrict private or commercial use?

### Takeaway
None of the surveyed licences forbids private use of an unmodified or modified copy for your own trading. The restrictions that matter are: Commons Clause (vectorbt, PyBroker) which bars selling the software or a service built substantially on it; AGPL-3.0 (backtesting.py, NOFX, pypbo) which forces source release if you distribute or offer it as a network service; GPL-3.0 (Backtrader, Freqtrade, Lumibot) which forces source release on distribution; and proprietary terms (mlfinlab, vectorbt PRO, current pandas-ta unclear).

### Inferences
- For a privately run trading bot that is never distributed or offered as a service, every open licence here (including GPL and AGPL) is usable; the AGPL/GPL obligations bite only if the bot or a derived tool is shipped to others or hosted for third parties. The note in the brief about backtesting.py's AGPL matters mainly if a web dashboard or SaaS is ever built on it.
- The Commons Clause tools (vectorbt, PyBroker) are safe for internal research but rule out selling a backtesting product or paid hosted service built substantially on them.
- pandas-ta should be replaced by pandas-ta-classic or TA-Lib in any new dependency list until the original's current licence text is read; older MIT-licensed 0.3.x copies remain MIT for whoever already holds them, but they are no longer listed on PyPI.
- This is a licence summary, not legal advice; mixed-licence stacks (e.g. GPL Freqtrade importing AGPL or Commons Clause components) should be checked before any redistribution.
