# Operational, security, regulatory and tax risks of running an unattended crypto trading bot as a US retail individual (status: 23 November 2026)

General information only, not legal or tax advice. Items marked CONSULT are where a CPA / tax attorney / derivatives lawyer should be consulted. "As of" dates are given per claim. Where sources conflict, both are shown.

---

## What are the most common ways retail bot users have lost their entire balance for reasons unrelated to strategy performance?

### Takeaway
The documented total-loss paths are, in rough order of frequency for small retail users: (1) running a trojanised "bot" repo or dependency that exfiltrates the private key / .env file, (2) deploying a YouTube "MEV/arbitrage bot" contract that is itself a drainer, (3) handing keys to a custodial Telegram bot or third-party bot platform that is then breached, (4) leaked exchange API keys abused through trade-only permissions, and (5) venue failure, hack, auto-deleveraging or depeg. Strategy-independent software faults (runaway order loops, duplicate orders, silently dead stop-losses) are the residual category.

### Cited Findings

**A. Trojanised bot repositories and packages (private-key / API-key theft)**
- July 2025: SlowMist analysed the GitHub repo `zldp2002/solana-pumpfun-bot`, a fake Pump.fun trading bot with inflated stars and forks; a malicious Node.js dependency (fake npm package) read the wallet key and POSTed it to an attacker server. A victim reported on 2 July 2025 that funds were stolen the day after running it. — [Cointelegraph, Jul 2025](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam); [CryptoTimes, 4 Jul 2025](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2025/07/04/crypto-stealing-solana-trading-bot-on-github-exposed/)
- SlowMist's Q3 2025 stolen-funds report says private-key leakage was the top cause of stolen assets that quarter and describes a second repo, `pumpfun-pumpswap-sniper-copy-trading-bot`, that hid code in a config file to read a `.env` file containing `PRIVATE_KEY`. — [SlowMist Q3 2025 MistTrack analysis](https://slowmist.medium.com/slowmist-2025-q3-misttrack-stolen-funds-analysis-639cbcefdf6f)
- February 2025: Kaspersky's "GitVenom" campaign: 200+ fake GitHub repos (Telegram bots, Bitcoin wallet managers, game cheats) in Python, JS, C, C++, C#; payloads were a Node.js stealer (credentials, wallet data, browser data sent via Telegram), AsyncRAT, Quasar and a clipboard address hijacker; one attacker wallet received about 5 BTC. AI-generated READMEs were used to look legitimate; oldest repo about two years old. Valuation differs by outlet ($485,000 in Kaspersky's press release title, $456K in The Hacker News). — [Kaspersky press release](https://www.kaspersky.com/about/press-releases/kaspersky-exposes-hidden-malware-on-github-stealing-personal-data-and-485000-in-bitcoin); [Kaspersky blog](https://kaspersky.com/blog/malicious-code-in-github/53085); [The Hacker News, Feb 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/gitvenom-malware-steals-456k-in-bitcoin.html?m=0)
- Fake GitHub stars: Carnegie Mellon's StarScout study (accepted ICSE 2026) found about 6 million suspected fake stars and 18,617 repos with fake-star campaigns (Jul 2019 to Dec 2024), rising sharply in 2024; the majority of campaigns promoted short-lived phishing or malware repos posing as pirated software, game cheats or crypto bots. Results are "suspected" (false positives possible). — [arXiv 2412.13459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459); [CMU news, 2025](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2025/fake-github-stars)
- Socket (April 2025): one npm and two PyPI packages built to exfiltrate seed phrases and private keys. — [Socket blog](https://socket.dev/blog/malicious-npm-and-pypi-packages-steal-wallet-credentials)
- Socket 2025 Blockchain and Cryptocurrency Threat Report (June 2025): many stealers abuse lifecycle hooks (npm `postinstall`, PyPI `setup.py`) so theft triggers on install, even if the package is never imported. — [Socket threat report](https://socket.dev/blog/2025-blockchain-and-cryptocurrency-threat-report)
- September 2025: four npm packages impersonating Ethereum crypto utilities and Flashbots (MEV) tooling exfiltrated private keys and mnemonics to an attacker's Telegram bot. — [The Hacker News, Sep 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/09/malicious-npm-packages-impersonate.html?m=1)
- Checkmarx documented the PyPI package `cryptoaitools`, which shipped a seemingly functional DEX trading bot, supported by a fake website and GitHub repo (report date not confirmed in this session). — [Checkmarx blog](https://checkmarx.com/blog/cryptocurrency-enthusiasts-targeted-in-multi-vector-supply-chain-attack/)
- June 2025 roundup citing Checkmarx, ReversingLabs, Safety and Socket: PyPI packages stealing Solana private keys and npm packages draining Ethereum wallets. — [The Hacker News, Jun 2025](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/malicious-pypi-npm-and-ruby-packages.html?m=1)
- February 2026: The Hacker News reported compromised dYdX npm and PyPI client packages (legitimate exchange-client libraries turned malicious; wallet-emptying payload per syndicated headline). — [The Hacker News, Feb 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/compromised-dydx-npm-and-pypi-packages.html?m=1)
- May 2026: "TrapDoor" campaign, 34+ packages across npm, PyPI and Crates.io targeting Solana, Sui and Aptos wallet data; Rust packages used malicious build scripts; Socket did not identify victims or stolen amounts. — [CoinDesk, 29 May 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/05/29/solana-sui-and-aptos-wallet-data-targeted-in-trapdoor-package-attack)

**B. YouTube "ChatGPT / AI MEV bot" smart-contract scams**
- SentinelLABS (August 2025): YouTube tutorials instruct viewers to deploy a "trading bot" contract in Remix; the contract hides the attacker's address by obfuscation and sets it as a second owner; when the victim funds the contract and clicks "start", the ETH goes to the scammer. Over $900k stolen; the most successful video (@Jazz_Braze) had 387,000+ views; aged YouTube accounts were used. — [SentinelLABS](https://www.sentinelone.com/labs/smart-contract-scams-ethereum-drainers-pose-as-trading-bots-to-steal-crypto/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/fake-ethereum-trading-bots-on-youtube-help-scammers-steal-over-900k/)
- SlowMist: the older "Uniswap Arbitrage MEV Bot" scam was rebranded "ChatGPT Arbitrage MEV Bot"; ChatGPT is mentioned only as a credibility hook. — [SlowMist](https://slowmist.medium.com/a-new-twist-on-old-scams-the-arbitrage-mev-bot-deception-8a9118b4645a)
- CFTC customer advisory (25 Jan 2024, Release 8854-24) "AI Won't Turn Trading Bots into Money Machines": fraudsters tout automated algorithms and crypto schemes with unreasonably high returns; "AI technology can't predict the future or sudden market changes"; be wary of social-media influencers and strangers online. No update since found. — [CFTC advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnandProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html); [CFTC release 8854-24](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8854-24)

- Maestro (Oct 2023): router contract exploit, about 280 ETH (about $500k); combined Maestro + Unibot loss about $1.1M per CertiK; Maestro compensated users. — [CertiK](https://www.certik.com/blog/maestro-and-unibot)
- Banana Gun (19 Sep 2024): 563 ETH (about $1.9M) drained from user wallets; team blamed a vulnerability in its Telegram message oracle and promised refunds. Figures conflict: about $1.9M / "nearly $2M from at least 36 victims" (Cointelegraph) versus "$3 million to 11 users" (The Block). — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/telegram-bot-banana-gun-users-drained-500-ether); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/318074/banana-gun-exploit-refund); [Rekt](https://rekt.news/bananagun-rekt)
- Solareum (March 2024): Solana Telegram bot drained of about $520k and shut down; BONKbot users were reported affected; US prosecutors later tied the exploit to a North Korean developer. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/telegram-trading-bot-solareum-shutters-after-520k-exploit); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/solareum-telegram-trading-app-shuts-down-following-dollar523k-exploit-bonkbot-users-affected); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/regulation/how-a-dprk-developer-tricked-solareum-and-stole-14m)
- DEXX: Helius's Solana incident history lists a $30M DEXX loss attributed to private-key management (date not confirmed in this session). — [Helius](https://helius.dev/blog/solana-hacks)
- Bloom (26 Oct 2026): about 9,408 SOL (about $947k to $953k) taken from 48 to 49 wallets of nine users via a remote-code-execution flaw in a third-party component; users reimbursed same day. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bloom-bot-hackers-953k-faster-popcorn); [parameter.io](https://parameter.io/bloom-trading-bot-suffers-9408-sol-hack-all-users-refunded-within-hours/)
- 3Commas (Oct to Dec 2022): API keys users had given to the bot platform leaked; about 100,000 keys reported (not an official count); CEO confirmed on 28 Dec 2022; loss estimates $14.9M (HAPI) to $22M; attackers used trade permissions to pump illiquid tokens (e.g. DMG on FTX) and wash-trade balances away; Binance and 3Commas declined to reimburse one user. — [ForkLog / HAPI analysis](https://forklog.com/en/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/3commas-ceo-confirms-api-key-leak-following-warning-from-cz); [SiliconANGLE, 29 Dec 2022](https://siliconangle.com/2022/12/29/crypto-trading-service-3commas-confirms-massive-api-key-leak-hack)

**D. API keys abused without withdrawal rights**
- CyberNews research: stolen API keys for sale essentially never had withdrawal rights; attackers instead drain balances by placing extremely unfavourable orders against their own counter-orders. — [CyberNews](https://cybernews.com/security/report-how-cybercriminals-abuse-api-keys-to-steal-millions/)
- Lighter (reported 17 Nov 2026): over $10M (10.39M USDC traced) drained from a decentralised perp exchange using compromised API keys to move funds into a public pool and redeem via another account; about $5M returned to the affected trader; fixes: wallet signature for pool transfers, API-key signatures no longer public, IP allowlists and per-key permissions planned. Lighter's post-mortem: "A key that can trade can still lose you money if someone else holds it." — [Cointelegraph, 17 Nov 2026](https://cointelegraph.com/news/lighter-incident-exposes-risks-beyond-withdrawal-controls)

- Bybit (21 Feb 2025): about 401,000 ETH (about $1.4B to $1.5B), largest crypto theft on record, attributed to North Korea. Chainalysis: $3.4B stolen in 2025 with attacks on individual wallets rising. — [The Block, 2025 top hacks](https://www.theblock.co/post/380992/biggest-crypto-hacks-2025); [The Block / Chainalysis](https://www.theblock.co/post/382477/crypto-hack-2025-chainalysis)
- Coinbase (disclosed May 2025): bribed support contractors leaked data on 69,461 customers (under 1% of monthly transacting users), used for impersonation scams; Coinbase estimated $180M to $400M in remediation and voluntary reimbursement; keys and funds not directly compromised. — [Fortune, 16 May 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/05/16/coinbase-data-breach-ransom-sec-investigation/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/21/coinbase_confirms_insider_breach_affects/)

- 10 to 11 Oct 2025: over $19B liquidated in about 24 hours after a tariff announcement, the largest notional deleveraging on record; auto-deleveraging (ADL) triggered on multiple venues; USDe traded to about $0.65 on Binance while near $1 elsewhere (Binance used its own spot book for collateral pricing). - Hyperliquid JELLY (26 Mar 2025): validators delisted the JELLY perp and settled 392M tokens at $0.0095 (the attacker's short entry price) after the HLP vault faced about $12M to $13.5M unrealised loss; critics called this proof of centralised discretion ("validator put"). — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2025-03-26-hyperliquid-delists-jellyjelly-memecoin-amid-whale-manipulation-fiasco-348314); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/hyperliquid-decentralization-validator-put)

- Regulated-futures caveat: customer positions in US-listed perpetuals sit in the futures account class, which a law-firm analysis says exposes customers to "double default" (FCM plus fellow-customer) risk. — [Cahill via Columbia CLS Blue Sky, 15 Jun 2026](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/06/15/cahill-discusses-how-digital-asset-perpetual-contracts-came-onshore/)

**F. Software and operational failures**
- Knight Capital (1 Aug 2012): in the first 45 minutes its router sent more than 4 million orders while trying to fill 212 customer orders, because discontinued code remained on a server; loss over $460M; 97 automated warning emails before the open were not treated as alerts; SEC penalty $12M (16 Oct 2013), first action under Market Access Rule 15c3-5. — [SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)
- "Runaway bot" on Lighter: a bot swept a thin order book and pushed HYPE from about $47 to about $98 within a minute; the faulty account's loss was reported at about $482k. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/346371/runaway-bot-sends-hype-token-to-98-in-one-minute-on-lighter-dex); [AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/a-482k-trading-bot-failure-put-lighter-to-the-test-why-was-the-damage-limited/)
- Individual developer post-mortem (anecdotal, low authority): two execution paths fired simultaneously and opened a BTC position at double size on Hyperliquid; on OKX a stop-loss silently stopped existing; fix was a periodic check that stop orders are still live on the exchange. — [dev.to post-mortem](https://dev.to/xqliu/two-losses-two-systemic-bugs-automated-trading-post-mortem-from-okx-and-hyperliquid-427l)

### Inferences
- For a small retail account, credential theft (categories A to D) is the dominant total-loss path; it is binary and immediate, whereas venue failure is rarer but unrecoverable and uninsured.
- "Withdrawals disabled" is necessary but not sufficient: 3Commas (2022), CyberNews and Lighter (2026) all show value extraction through trading or internal-transfer permissions alone. Limiting which markets the account can reach (liquid majors only, no internal transfer or pool rights) matters as much as the withdrawal flag.
- Any bot that needs a raw private key in a `.env` file (all on-chain sniper / MEV / DEX bots) has a strictly worse threat model than a CEX bot with a scoped API key: one malicious dependency means total loss, with no exchange-side control to stop it.
- Star counts, fork counts and polished READMEs are not evidence of legitimacy (StarScout; GitVenom's AI-written READMEs).
- Custodial Telegram bots have been breached roughly yearly since 2023 through varied vectors (router contracts, message oracle, third-party component, insider); refunds have been discretionary, not guaranteed.

### Gaps
- No reliable aggregate statistic on how many retail bot users lost funds by each cause; ranking above is qualitative.
- Could not find security-firm reports specifically from ReversingLabs, Phylum or Scam Sniffer on fake trading bots in this session (ReversingLabs appears only in a roundup).
- No sourced documentation of social-media threads that promote bot toolkits to sell courses or drain wallets, beyond the CFTC's general advisory and the YouTube cases.
- Trojan (Solana Telegram bot) and BONKbot: no confirmed direct breach of their own infrastructure found; BONKbot exposure was reported only via the Solareum incident.
- DEXX date and details, and 2026 incidents listed by Comparitech (e.g. Drift $285M), were not verified.
- No 2025 to 2026 primary source on exchange withdrawal freezes at US venues was found; the USDC 2023 conversion pause is the cleanest US example.

## What is the minimum security and operational setup that makes unattended operation acceptable?

### Takeaway
Sources converge on: run only vetted, pinned code in an isolated environment; give the bot a scoped trade-only key with IP allow-listing on a dedicated sub-account holding only risk capital; enforce hard limits and independent monitoring outside the strategy code; and paper-trade first. No setup removes venue risk, so balance kept on the venue is the final control.

### Cited Findings
- Exchange defaults and recommendations: withdrawal permission disabled by default; IP whitelisting recommended; Binance restricts keys without IP restriction to read-only by default and requires IP restriction for withdrawals; do not store keys in plain files or disclose them. — [CyberNews](https://cybernews.com/security/report-how-cybercriminals-abuse-api-keys-to-steal-millions/); [Binance security blog](https://www.binance.com/cs/blog/security/5461902802472279002)
- Post-Lighter lessons (Nov 2026): each credential needs an explicit mandate (accessible accounts, permitted markets, order sizes, price tolerances, aggregate exposure); moving capital into shared pools should require separate authorisation; a second signature only helps if its approval independently evaluates the transaction's financial effect. — [Cointelegraph, 17 Nov 2026](https://cointelegraph.com/news/lighter-incident-exposes-risks-beyond-withdrawal-controls)
- Hyperliquid's developer docs: a master account approves "API wallets" (agent wallets) that "are only used to sign" on behalf of the master or sub-accounts; recommends separate API wallets per sub-account and not reusing agent addresses; API wallets can expire or be pruned. The page does not state whether an agent wallet can withdraw. — [Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/nonces-and-api-wallets)
- Install-time execution: npm `postinstall` and PyPI `setup.py` hooks can steal credentials at install, before any code is imported. — [Socket threat report](https://socket.dev/blog/2025-blockchain-and-cryptocurrency-threat-report)
- SlowMist: be highly vigilant with unknown GitHub projects involving wallet or private-key operations; LLMs can be used to review scam contract code for malicious behaviour. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam); [SlowMist](https://slowmist.medium.com/a-new-twist-on-old-scams-the-arbitrage-mev-bot-deception-8a9118b4645a)
- Freqtrade (largest open-source bot) itself states: "This software is for educational purposes only", "Do not risk money which you are afraid to lose", and "Always start by running a trading bot in Dry-run and do not engage money before you understand how it works"; it offers Telegram / web UI start-stop and monitoring and market blacklists. — [Freqtrade docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/)
- Knight Capital lessons per SEC: inadequate controls for code deployment and testing, no effective pre-trade limits, alerts not routed as alerts. — [SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)
- Practitioner guidance (low authority): per-hour order caps and hard position limits before any edge; verify periodically that stop-loss orders are still live on the exchange. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-trading-bot-needs-limits-before-it-needs-an-edge); [dev.to post-mortem](https://dev.to/xqliu/two-losses-two-systemic-bugs-automated-trading-post-mortem-from-okx-and-hyperliquid-427l)
- Coinbase's insurance does not cover losses from compromised customer credentials. — [Coinbase insurance page](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/insurance)

### Inferences
Repository vetting checklist (synthesised from the incidents above; not from a single source):
1. Provenance: is it the canonical repo linked from the project's own docs / PyPI page? Check account age, contributor count, multi-year commit history, issue and PR activity, and independent references. Ignore stars and forks.
2. Reject on sight: repos promoted via YouTube tutorials, Telegram/Discord DMs, X threads with "guaranteed" returns, or anything requiring you to paste Solidity into Remix and fund it.
3. Read before running: grep for network calls (`fetch`, `axios`, `requests`, `http`, Telegram bot URLs), base58/base64/hex decoding, `eval`/`exec`, obfuscated strings, and any read of `.env`, keystore or browser paths. Inspect `package.json` scripts (`preinstall`/`postinstall`), `setup.py`, `build.rs`.
4. Dependencies: check each for typosquats and recent publish dates; pin exact versions with a lockfile and hashes; install with scripts disabled (`npm ci --ignore-scripts`; `pip install --require-hashes`); do not auto-update (the dYdX Feb 2026 case shows legitimate packages can be compromised).
5. Isolation: run in a dedicated VM / container / VPS with no browser, no personal wallets, no SSH keys, and egress restricted to the exchange API hosts.
6. First run with a dummy key and no funds while watching outbound connections; then dry-run / paper trade; then minimum size.
7. Prefer bots that never need a raw private key (CEX API key bots). If an on-chain key is unavoidable, use a fresh wallet funded only with the amount you accept losing.

Minimum API-key and account setup:
- Dedicated sub-account (or separate exchange account) funded only with bot risk capital; keep the remainder off the venue.
- Key scoped to trade plus read only; withdrawals, internal transfers, earn / pool / margin-borrow permissions off; IP allow-list to the bot host's static IP; one key per bot; rotate on any suspicion.
- Restrict the tradable universe to liquid majors (the 3Commas drain depended on illiquid pairs).
- Secrets in an OS keyring / secrets manager or environment injected at runtime, never committed; host disk encrypted; 2FA with hardware key on the exchange login; withdrawal address allow-list with time lock.
- A hardware wallet cannot sign unattended, so any self-custody bot wallet is by construction a hot wallet; treat its balance as fully at risk and sweep profits out manually.

Minimum operational controls (outside strategy code where possible):
- Hard caps: max position notional, max order size, max orders per minute / hour, max daily loss, max leverage (1x for a first deployment).
- Kill switch that cancels all orders, flattens, and revokes or disables the key; test it.
- Heartbeat / dead-man's switch on an independent host that alerts (and ideally cancels orders) if the bot stops reporting.
- Startup and periodic reconciliation of local state against exchange positions and open orders; idempotent client order IDs to prevent duplicate orders after restart; handle partial fills explicitly.
- Stale-data guard (refuse to trade if last tick is older than N seconds), NTP clock sync, rate-limit back-off, explicit behaviour during exchange API outage (do not blindly retry market orders).
- Exchange-resident stop-loss orders plus periodic verification that they still exist.
- Dry-run for weeks, then smallest live size; change control (no hot code changes on the live instance); log everything and export fills daily for tax records.
- Assume ADL, liquidation and depeg can occur while unattended (10 Oct 2025): low or no leverage, and do not hold bot collateral in yield-bearing or synthetic stablecoins.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (regulator or standards-body) checklist exists for retail bot operation; the above combines vendor, exchange and practitioner guidance with inference.
- Freqtrade's main page does not document NTP requirements or stop-loss-on-exchange; those details live in sub-pages not retrieved.
- Per-venue availability of sub-accounts, IP allow-listing and per-key market restrictions on Coinbase Advanced, Kraken Pro and Gemini was not verified.

## Which venue and product choices minimise legal and tax friction for a US individual (regulated futures with 60/40 treatment versus spot)?

### Takeaway
Lawful, low-friction choices in November 2026 are (a) spot on a US-licensed exchange (simple legality, but every fill is a separately reported short-term property sale) or (b) CFTC-regulated futures through a US FCM: dated CME-style crypto futures have settled Section 1256 60/40 treatment, while US perpetual and perpetual-style contracts (Coinbase, Kraken/Bitnomial, Kalshi) are marketed and widely believed to qualify but have no IRS guidance and face a pending legal challenge. Offshore exchanges via VPN and geoblocked on-chain perps are the highest-friction choice on both legal and tax grounds.

### Cited Findings

**Venues and products lawfully available to US retail (status Nov 2026)**
- Without federal market-structure law, unleveraged spot trading and custody remain governed by state licensing regimes. — [Orrick, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation)
- April 2025: Bitnomial was the first CFTC-registered DCM to self-certify a perpetual futures contract (Reg. 40.2). — [Dechert, Jun 2026](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/6/cftc-takes-historic-steps-to-bring-digital-asset-perpetual-contr.html)

### Inferences
- Running a bot on your own account requires no federal registration under the definitions above (CTA/CPO status attaches to advising or pooling others' money for compensation). Trading for friends or family, selling signals with discretion, or pooling funds is where registration (CFTC/NFA for derivatives; potentially SEC/state adviser rules for securities) becomes a live question. CONSULT a lawyer before touching anyone else's money. No primary source states the "own account" negative directly.
- Using a VPN to reach an offshore exchange or geoblocked DEX front end is a breach of venue terms and misrepresents location; the enforcement record (OKX, KuCoin) targets venues, but the practical user risks are account closure or frozen funds when KYC or location is re-checked, no US legal recourse, no 1099-DA (self-computed tax on thousands of fills), and no Section 1256. I found no case of a retail individual prosecuted solely for VPN access, and none establishing that it is lawful.
- Tax-friction ranking for a high-turnover bot (lowest to highest): (1) dated CME-style crypto futures via a US FCM (settled 1256, one net number on Form 6781, no per-lot tracking); (2) US perpetual / perpetual-style futures (probably 1256 per brokers' reporting, but unresolved; funding-payment character unknown); (3) US spot (every fill a short-term lot on Form 8949; wash-sale rule not yet applicable but legislatively targeted); (4) offshore / DeFi perps and DEX trading (no broker reporting, no 1256, gas-fee dispositions).
- 60/40 blended federal rate (my arithmetic on the cited rates): 24% bracket, 0.6 x 15% + 0.4 x 24% = 18.6% versus 24% for spot short-term; 22% bracket, 17.8% versus 22%; taxable income inside the 0% long-term band, 0.4 x 12% = 4.8% versus 12%. Leverage, funding and futures fees can outweigh this saving.

Worked break-even illustration (my calculation; assumptions labelled):
- Let c = all-in cost per side (fee + half-spread + slippage) as a fraction of notional, N = round trips per year at 1x capital, t = combined marginal tax rate on short-term gains, R = target after-tax return on capital. Required gross edge per round trip g = 2c + R / ((1 - t) x N). Annual cost drag = 2 x c x N.
- Entry-tier taker spot (c about 0.85%: 0.80% fee + 0.05% spread/slippage assumption): each round trip costs 1.7%. At N = 100, cost drag = 170% of capital per year; not viable for any intraday strategy.
- Maker-only at entry tier (c about 0.40%): N = 100 gives 80% drag; N = 250 gives 200%.
- Low-cost case (c = 0.10%, e.g. higher volume tier or futures; assumption): N = 250 gives 50% of capital per year in costs. To net R = 10% after tax at t = 29% (24% federal + assumed 5% state), pre-tax net must be 14.1%, so gross trading profit before costs must be 64.1% of capital, i.e. g = 25.6 bps per round trip against 20 bps of cost. The strategy must capture 28% more than its cost on every trade, on average, just to net 10%.
- Same case with 60/40 at 24% federal + 5% state (t about 23.6%): pre-tax net needed 13.1% instead of 14.1%; the cost term dominates the tax term at high turnover.
- Tax asymmetry: a profitable year is taxed in full at ordinary rates, while a subsequent losing year's net capital loss offsets only $3,000 of other income per year (carryforward only). A bot that makes $20k in year 1 and loses $20k in year 2 is flat pre-tax but has paid about $4,800 federal tax (24%) and holds a $20k carryforward usable at $3k per year absent future gains.

## What has changed in US law and IRS reporting between 2024 and November 2026 that a new bot operator must know?

### Takeaway
Hard changes already in force: per-wallet basis tracking (1 Jan 2025), Form 1099-DA gross-proceeds reporting for 2025 sales and basis reporting for assets acquired and sold in the same custodial account from 1 Jan 2026, repeal of the DeFi broker rule (10 Apr 2025), the GENIUS Act (18 Jul 2025; effective by 18 Jan 2027), and CFTC onshoring of perpetuals (2025 to 2026). Not enacted as of 23 Nov 2026: the CLARITY Act (failed Senate cloture 49 to 50 on 15 Sep 2026) and the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act (H.R. 10357, cleared Ways and Means 38 to 5 on 16 Sep 2026), so the wash-sale rule still does not apply to crypto, but that is the single most likely near-term change affecting bots.

**Tax reporting and basis**
- Baseline: "For U.S. tax purposes, digital assets are considered property, not currency"; the Form 1040 digital-asset question asks whether the taxpayer received or disposed of a digital asset during the year. — [IRS digital assets page, updated 2 Sep 2026](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets); [IRS Notice 2014-21](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-14-21.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a spot bot starting in late 2026 or 2027 on one US exchange, nearly all lots will be "covered" (acquired after 2025, same custodial account), so the 1099-DA should carry basis, most likely on a FIFO default. This sharply reduces the reconciliation burden compared with 2025 if, and only if, the bot's assets never move between venues or wallets. Transfers in break covered status.
- After 31 Dec 2026 the books-and-records identification relief ends unless extended again; any non-FIFO lot method would then need to be communicated to the broker at or before each sale, which is impractical for a bot. Plan on FIFO per account.
- If the wash-sale extension in H.R. 10357 (or a successor in the 120th Congress) is enacted, a bot that repeatedly re-enters the same asset within 30 days would have most realised losses deferred into replacement-lot basis, with painful year-end effects (taxable gains recognised, offsetting losses deferred). Effective date is unknown; monitor. The proposed Section 475-style election for digital-asset traders would neutralise this for those who qualify and elect.
- On-chain bots face the heaviest burden: no broker reporting (DeFi rule repealed), each swap and each gas payment in a non-stablecoin token is a disposition, and per-wallet basis applies. The proposed $10 fee nonrecognition rule is not law and, per EY's summary, would be capped at 5,000 transactions a year, which an active bot could exceed.
- Trader tax status is a facts-and-circumstances test built around the taxpayer's own activity; whether a fully automated bot run by someone with a day job satisfies "substantial, continuous, regular" is uncertain. CONSULT before relying on TTS or filing a 475(f) election; the election for 2027 would be due by 15 Apr 2027 (unextended due date of the 2026 return).
- A high-frequency spot bot can generate thousands of Form 8949 lines; consumer tax software prices by transaction count, so the top tier (or summary reporting with attached statements) should be budgeted as a fixed cost.
