# Evidence review: which systematic trading strategies work in crypto and other liquid markets at retail scale (through November 2026)

Reading notes for the report writer:
- "Gross" means before fees, spread, slippage, funding and borrow. "Net" means the study deducted some stated cost. Each finding says which.
- Source tiers are marked: **[PR]** peer-reviewed journal or refereed conference, **[WP]** working paper or preprint (SSRN, arXiv, NBER, BIS WP), **[REG]** regulator or central bank, **[IND]** industry, vendor, analytics firm or news. Industry numbers are not audited.
- Where I could read only an abstract, a search snippet or a secondary summary, the finding says so. Numbers I could not verify are in Gaps, not in Cited Findings.
- Evidence is organised under the five key questions. The ten strategy families are sub-headings inside Key Question 1.

## Key Question 1: For a small US retail account trading intraday to multi-day horizons in BTC / ETH / SOL, which strategy families have evidence of positive expected return after realistic costs, and how large and how stable is that edge?

### Takeaway
Only two families have repeated academic support for a positive net edge that a retail account could run: slow time-series trend following on liquid coins (daily signals, volatility-sized, mostly long or flat) and short-horizon cross-sectional momentum (which needs shorting and is hard to run from the US). Both are regime-dependent and the strongest published trend-factor result was challenged in a November 2026 re-audit. Carry was the best risk-adjusted trade of 2020-2024 but is now roughly a Treasury-bill yield, and every intraday, market-making, arbitrage, MEV, memecoin, grid and indicator-rule family shows zero or negative expected net return for non-professional participants.

### Cited Findings

#### Family 1. Day trading profitability in general
- **[PR]** Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, "The cross-section of speculator skill: Evidence from day trading", Journal of Financial Markets 18 (2014) 1-24. Taiwan Stock Exchange, 1992-2006. Fewer than 1% of day traders predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees. The average day trader earns -11.5 bps per day gross and -28.9 bps per day net. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html); [author PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- **[WP]** Companion paper by the same authors on learning: experienced losing day traders keep trading at almost the same rate as winners (95.3% versus 96.4% probability of day trading again in the next 12 months). — [Odean working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- **[WP]** Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?", SSRN 3423101 (2019, revised 2020). All 19,646 Brazilians who began day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013-2015, regulator (CVM) data, net results. Of the 1,551 who persisted more than 300 days, 97% lost money, 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage (US$16 per day) and about 0.5% earned more than a starting bank teller (US$54 per day; the abstract says 0.4%, the body 0.5%). The top earner averaged US$310 per day with a daily standard deviation of US$2,560. The share with positive net profit falls with persistence: 29.8% (1 day), 15.5% (2-50 days), 8.9% (51-100), 6.8% (101-200), 5.4% (201-300), 3.0% (300+). The authors find no evidence of learning. — [Quantpedia summary with tables](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)
- **[REG]** ESMA product intervention (2018): national regulators' analyses across the EU found 74-89% of retail CFD accounts lose money, with average losses per client of EUR 1,600 to EUR 29,000. A loss means the sum of realised and unrealised net CFD profits over 12 months is negative. — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933); [ESMA notice](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma35-43-1135_notice_of_pi_decisions_on_cfds_and_binary_options.pdf)
- **[REG]** US retail FX: NFA rules require dealers to publish the share of profitable accounts each quarter. Interactive Brokers reported 46.09% profitable and 53.91% unprofitable for Q1 2026; IG US reported 32.63% profitable and 67.37% unprofitable for Q2 2023. These are one-quarter figures, so they understate multi-year loss rates. — [IBKR disclosure](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=3731); [IG US disclosure](https://www.ig.com/us/public-disclosures-and-risk-disclaimer); [NFA requirement](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/rfed-reporting.html)
- **[PR]** Heimer and Simsek, "Should retail investors' leverage be limited?", Journal of Financial Economics 132(3) (2019) 1-21, studies the 2010 US cap on retail FX leverage with a difference-in-differences design. I read the abstract only: the cap reduced trading activity and the authors conclude it improved welfare without hurting liquidity. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v132y2019i3p1-21.html)
- **[REG]** SEBI (India), September 2024: 93% of 1.13 crore (11.3 million) individual equity futures and options traders lost money over FY22-FY24, with a combined net loss of INR 1.81 lakh crore including transaction costs. In FY24 alone 91.1% lost. 76.3% of traders who lost in two consecutive years kept trading. — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html)
- **[REG]** BIS Working Paper 1049 (Auer, Cornelli, Doerr, Frost, Gambacorta; November 2022, revised July 2023), crypto exchange app data August 2015 to December 2022: an estimated 73-81% of retail investors likely lost money on their initial bitcoin investment. BIS Bulletin 69 (February 2023) restates it as "a majority of crypto app users in nearly all economies made losses" and adds that after Terra/Luna and FTX large holders sold while small retail holders bought. — [BIS WP 1049](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1049.htm); [BIS Bulletin 69](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm)
- **[PR]** Jia, Moallemi, Wang and Zeng, "Who Loses and Why? Market Microstructure of Perpetual Futures", AFT 2026 (LIPIcs vol. 395), full version SSRN 7270041. Complete on-chain Hyperliquid record for the ten most active contracts. Valuing each trade against the mid-price two hours later, net of fees, front-end (manual, mostly retail) traders lost USD 1.11 billion, algorithmic market makers gained USD 208 million and algorithmic takers roughly broke even. The loss channels are paying for immediacy, forced liquidations and fees. — [Dagstuhl DROPS](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36)
- **[PR]** Kogan, Makarov, Niessner and Schoar, "Are cryptos different? Evidence from retail trading", Journal of Financial Economics 159 (2024). About 200,000 eToro accounts, 2015-2019. The same retail traders are contrarian in stocks and gold but trend-chasing in crypto. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v159y2024ics0304405x2400120x.html)
- **[PR]** Copy trading: Apesteguia, Oechssler and Weidenholzer, "Copy Trading", Management Science 66(12) (2020) 5608-5622. Laboratory experiment: showing others' results raises risk-taking and the option to copy raises it further, lowering ex-ante welfare. This is an experiment, not live account data. — [BSE publication page](https://bse.eu/research/publications/copy-trading)
- **[WP]** eToro mirroring data: people copy mainly on popularity, which is only weakly correlated with performance. — [arXiv 2507.01817](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01817)
- **[IND, unverified]** A secondary article cites a 90-day study of Binance, Bybit and MEXC copy trading in which 97% of lead traders were profitable on their own positions but only 43.6% produced positive returns for followers. I could not find the underlying study. — [AInvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/leader-copying-losing-point-2608/)

#### Family 2. Crypto time-series momentum and trend following
- **[PR]** Liu and Tsyvinski, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", Review of Financial Studies 34(6) (2021) 2689-2727, report a strong time-series momentum effect. In the authors' VoxEU column, a one-standard-deviation rise in this week's bitcoin return is followed by a 3.16% higher return the next week (gross, in-sample; the sample pre-dates 2019). — [VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/risks-and-returns-cryptocurrencies); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:34:y:2021:i:6:p:2689-2727.)
- **[WP]** Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon, "Catching Crypto Trends: A Tactical Approach for Bitcoin and Altcoins", SSRN 5209907 / Swiss Finance Institute Research Paper 25-80 (2025). Survivorship-bias-free data on all coins since 2015, results January 2015 to mid-March 2025. Ensemble of Donchian breakouts with lookbacks of 5 to 360 days, trailing stop at the channel midpoint, 25% volatility target, long-only, top 20 liquid coins. The author page reports a Sharpe ratio above 1.5 net of fees and annualised alpha of 10.8% versus bitcoin; a secondary summary reports 18% net CAGR at 10 bps cost, with 25 and 50 bps also tested. One author works at a trading firm (Concretum). — [Barbon paper page](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/); [SFI record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html)
- **[WP]** Han, Kang and Ryu, "Time-Series and Cross-Sectional Momentum in the Cryptocurrency Market: A Comprehensive Analysis under Realistic Assumptions": with liquidation risk and costs modelled, evidence for time-series momentum is strong and evidence for cross-sectional momentum is weak, and time-series momentum profits are concentrated in rising markets. I read search snippets only; the PDF was blocked. — [AUT working paper PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)
- **[WP]** "A Decade of Evidence of Trend Following Investing in Cryptocurrencies" (arXiv 2009.12155), BTCUSD 2011-2019: when parameters were fitted on year Y and tested on year Y+1, trend following showed no predictable, attractive Sharpe ratios; the study assumed negligible costs. — [arXiv 2009.12155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155)
- **[PR]** Shen, Urquhart and Wang, "Bitcoin intraday time-series momentum", Financial Review 57(2) (2022) 319-344: the first half-hour return of a volume-defined session positively predicts the last half-hour return, strongest on high-volume or high-volatility days. I could not confirm whether the reported economic gains are net of costs. — [University of Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181)
- **[IND]** Man Group (Man AHL), "In Crypto We Trend" (data to December 2024): moving-average and breakout trend models on Coinbase data, including transaction and shorting costs (levels undisclosed). Risk-adjusted return peaks at about 10-15 coins; average pairwise correlation is about 0.6; liquidity falls sharply beyond the top 15 coins. No Sharpe numbers are stated in the text. — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)
- **[IND]** Grayscale Research (August 2023): a 50-day moving-average rule on bitcoin had a Sharpe ratio of 1.9 versus 1.3 for buy and hold over 2012 to July 2023. Cost treatment not confirmed; issuer research. — [Grayscale](https://research.grayscale.com/reports/the-trend-is-your-friend-managing-bitcoins-volatility-with-momentum-signals)
- **[WP, low credibility]** Bui and Nguyen, "AdaptiveTrend" (arXiv 2602.11708, February 2026, authors from a private trading firm): 6-hour trend signals on 150+ Binance perpetuals, out of sample January 2022 to December 2024. Reported net of 4 bps taker fee plus modelled slippage and funding: 40.5% annual return, Sharpe 2.41, maximum drawdown -12.7%. Their own benchmarks over the same window: volatility-scaled time-series momentum Sharpe 1.83, one-month time-series momentum Sharpe 0.65 (drawdown -34.8%), bitcoin buy and hold Sharpe 0.17 (drawdown -64.1%). Bear-regime Sharpe was -0.31. Parameters were re-optimised monthly, which the authors concede risks look-ahead bias. — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11708)
- **[IND]** Volatility scaling: Man Group reports that volatility-scaling a bitcoin allocation raises the Sharpe ratio by about 0.4 regardless of the half-life of the volatility estimate. — [Man Group, "Crypto: Too Hot to Handle?"](https://www.man.com/insights/crypto-too-hot-to-handle)

#### Family 3. Cross-sectional crypto factors
- **[PR]** Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", Journal of Finance 77(2) (2022) 1133-1177: a three-factor model (market, size, momentum) prices the cross-section. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:2:p:1133-1177)
- **[WP]** Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu, "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age" (arXiv 2510.14435, 2025), data December 2013 to September 2025. Weekly value-weighted quintile long-short returns, **gross, no costs**: two-week momentum +2.6% per week full sample (t = 3.89) and +2.1% per week after 2020 (t = 3.70); size (large minus small) -2.3% per week full sample (t = -2.66) but only -0.9% after 2020 (t = -1.69); value (price to new addresses) -3.5% full sample and -2.3% after 2020 (t = -2.32). Longer-horizon momentum (12 and 24 weeks) and volatility sorts earn nothing significant. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **[PR]** Fieberg, Liedtke, Poddig, Walker and Zaremba, "A Trend Factor for the Cross Section of Cryptocurrency Returns" (CTREND), Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 60(7) (2025) 3116-3153. More than 3,000 coins, April 2015 to May 2022, 28 technical signals combined by machine learning. Long-short quintile return 3.87% per week gross, annualised Sharpe 1.94. Net of 30 bps (long) and 40 bps (short) costs: 2.90% per week; at 50/60 bps: 2.35%. Turnover 68% per week; break-even cost 1.41% per trade. Largest 100 coins: 3.40% gross, 1.90-2.45% net. Returns fell in the second half of the sample to 3.26% per week. — [JFQA open-access PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0022109024000747)
- **[WP]** Wang and Pang, "The Cryptocurrency Trend Factor Revisited" (arXiv 2611.01951, 1 November 2026) audit the CTREND code and report look-ahead and accounting problems. After their comprehensive correction the weekly gross return is 0.05%, the Sharpe ratio 0.01, the alpha -1.38% per week, the largest-100 return 1.00% per week and the net return at the lowest cost assumption -0.87% per week. This is a new, unrefereed preprint and the original authors' response is not known. — [arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01951)
- **[PR]** Dobrynskaya, "Cryptocurrency Momentum and Reversal", Journal of Alternative Investments (2023), about 2,000 coins, 2014-2020: momentum at horizons up to 2-4 weeks, significant reversal beyond one month; the peak effect uses two-week formation and holding. — [HSE summary](https://www.hse.ru/en/news/research/559533243.html)
- **[PR]** "Up or down? Short-term reversal, momentum, and liquidity effects in cryptocurrency markets", International Review of Financial Analysis 78 (2021): yesterday's return predicts today's with a reversal sign in most coins, but the largest and most liquid coins show daily momentum instead. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)
- **[WP]** Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson, "Trading volume and liquidity provision in cryptocurrency markets" (Riksbank WP 413 / CERGE-EI WP 730), daily USD pairs 2017-2022: short-term reversal profits are compensation for liquidity provision and are concentrated in low-activity pairs; results hold with linear transaction costs. — [Riksbank WP 413](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2022/no.-413-trading-volume-and-liquidity-provision-in-cryptocurreny-markets.pdf)
- **[WP]** Fieberg, Günther, Poddig and Zaremba (2024), cited in Borri et al.: across 20,736 research designs for 43 sorting variables, only size and momentum stay robust. — [arXiv 2510.14435, footnote 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)

#### Family 4. Funding-rate carry and cash-and-carry basis
- **[REG/WP]** Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov, "Crypto carry", BIS Working Paper 1087 (April 2023, revised 2025): the futures-spot basis averages above 10% a year and has reached 60%; it is driven by leveraged retail trend-chasing and scarce arbitrage capital, not interest differentials. High carry predicts future crashes, and cash-and-carry positions face margin spikes and liquidations in drawdowns. — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- **[WP]** Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu (2025) rebuild the Schmeling et al. trade (long spot, short Binance bitcoin perpetual, 8-hour data, August 2020 to May 2025): annualised Sharpe 6.45 over the full sample, 4.06 from 2024, and negative in 2025. The funding leg averaged about 8% a year with 0.8% volatility. Costs and margin are not modelled. — [arXiv 2510.14435, section 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **[WP]** He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888): perpetual-spot deviations are larger than in traditional currency markets, co-move across coins and diminish over time; the implied arbitrage has high Sharpe ratios but is not risk-free. Abstract only. — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888)
- **[IND]** Funding by year, bitcoin, open-interest weighted, from Ethena's own data: 11.0% annualised in 2024, 4.9% in 2025, 2.2% for 2026 to 11 August. — [CryptoSlate, 28 August 2026](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/)
- **[IND]** Ethena risk documentation (charts to December 2024): long-run average funding 7.80% (BTC) and 9.15% (ETH); funding was negative on 15.9% of days for BTC and 17.5% for ETH; longest negative streak 13 days; longest positive streak 176 days (late 2023 to early 2024). Issuer source. — [Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/protocol-overview/risks/funding-risk)
- **[IND]** CME bitcoin futures basis: about 20% annualised in late 2024, below 10% throughout 2025 and about 8% in early September 2025 (Velo data via CoinDesk). — [Yahoo Finance / CoinDesk, 2 September 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/federal-rate-cut-could-spark-091651943.html)
- **[IND]** By April 2026 the CME basis was about 5% against a risk-free rate of about 4.5%, described as "essentially zero incentive" to run the trade. — [The Block, 9 April 2026](https://www.theblock.co/post/396722/cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand)
- **[IND]** US retail access: Coinbase launched CFTC-regulated "perpetual-style" nano bitcoin and nano ether futures on 21 July 2025, with up to 10x leverage, a five-year expiry and fees from 0.02% per contract. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/331180/coinbase-opens-perpetuals-trading-us-customers); [Coinbase Derivatives](https://www.coinbase.com/derivatives)

#### Family 5. Market making and liquidity provision
- **[WP]** Albers, Cucuringu, Howison and Shestopaloff, "The Market Maker's Dilemma" (arXiv 2502.18625, Oxford; revised November 2025). Live experiment on the Binance bitcoin perpetual: 232,897 minimum-size maker orders (12-19 February 2024) and 172,800 more (21-31 August 2024). One-second post-fill returns are negative in every queue state, from -0.06 to -1.16 bps before rebate; at five seconds about -0.8 bps, or -0.3 bps after a 0.5 bps maker rebate (the best fee tier). A naive strategy quoting at the touch lost almost 60% in about three days. Order-book-imbalance strategies lost 0.43-0.49 bps per trade as maker and 1.96 bps as taker; the taker signal was worth about +1 bp per round trip before fees. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)
- **[PR]** On Hyperliquid, algorithmic market makers as a group gained USD 208 million while manual traders lost USD 1.11 billion (Jia et al., AFT 2026). The gains accrue to professional algorithmic makers. — [Dagstuhl DROPS](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36)
- **[IND]** Hummingbot: the only material I found is the project's own 2019 liquidity-mining whitepaper and blog posts. Its documentation says computing return requires netting mining rewards against trading profit and loss, fees and capital, and the operator states it does not claim liquidity mining will be profitable. — [Hummingbot whitepaper](https://docs.hummingbot.org/liquidity-mining.pdf); [Hummingbot academy](https://docs.hummingbot.org/academy-content/a-systematic-approach-to-liquidity-mining/)
- **[WP]** Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden and Zhang, "Automated Market Making and Loss-Versus-Rebalancing" (arXiv 2208.06046): LP profit equals fees minus LVR, an adverse-selection cost paid to arbitrageurs that grows with volatility. This is a model, not a profitability survey. — [arXiv 2208.06046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06046v4)
- **[WP/IND]** Loesch, Hindman, Welch and Richardson, "Impermanent Loss in Uniswap v3" (arXiv 2111.09192, Bancor-affiliated, 2021 data): the pools studied earned USD 199.3 million in fees and suffered USD 260.1 million of impermanent loss; 49.5% of LPs had negative returns relative to holding. — [arXiv 2111.09192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09192)
- **[WP]** Heimbach, Schertenleib and Wattenhofer, "Risks and Returns of Uniswap V3 Liquidity Providers" (arXiv 2205.08904, ETH Zurich): simple strategies in low-volatility pools are profitable but earn little; larger returns need active management and more risk. They criticise the Loesch method. — [arXiv 2205.08904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08904)
- **[REG/WP]** Aquilina, Foley, Gambacorta and Krekel, BIS Working Paper 1227 (November 2024), Uniswap v3: liquidity provision is concentrated in a small group of sophisticated participants who earn higher absolute and relative profits; retail LPs capture about 10-25% of fees. A former Uniswap researcher disputed the framing. — [BIS WP 1227](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.htm); [Cointelegraph coverage](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bis-uniswap-defi-liquidity-centralization)
- **[WP]** Sadeghi, Liu, Moallemi, Wan and Zhu, "Not All LPs Are Equal" (arXiv 2609.37963, September 2026), Uniswap v2/v3/v4 on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base: in concentrated-liquidity pools passive LPs underperform pool-level averages, more so on Ethereum than on layer 2s, and do better in higher-fee pools. Abstract only. — [arXiv 2609.37963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37963)

#### Family 6. Mean reversion and intraday patterns
- **[IND]** Vojtko and Padyšák (Quantpedia), Gemini hourly bitcoin data, 9 October 2015 to 3 February 2022: the 22:00 and 23:00 UTC hours have the highest returns; holding only from 21:00 to 23:00 UTC returned 33% a year with 20.93% volatility and -22.45% maximum drawdown. **Gross: no fees or spread.** An extension reports 40.64% and a Calmar ratio of 1.79. On NYSE trading days overnight returns dominate intraday returns. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/); [Quantpedia follow-up](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/)
- **[PR/WP]** Baur, Cahill, Godfrey and Liu test time-of-day, day-of-week and month-of-year effects on seven exchanges and find time-varying effects but no consistent or persistent pattern. Search-snippet summary. — [UCD repository](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/3fe12e0f-f1c4-4226-937e-b7fc034dfcd9/content)
- **[PR]** Day of week: results conflict. Caporale and Plastun (2019) find higher Monday returns in bitcoin only; Aharon and Qadan find day-of-week effects in returns and volatility for 2010-2017; a 2013-2019 GARCH study finds no classical day-of-week effect in returns but lower risk at weekends; Yaya and Ogbonna (2019) find no significant return effect. Search-snippet summaries. — [Caporale and Plastun](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/17208/3/FullText.pdf); [Aharon and Qadan](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/bitcoin-and-the-day-of-the-week-effect/); [RIBAF 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ribaf.2019.02.003); [Yaya and Ogbonna](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/91429/)
- **[WP]** Reversal after large moves: an LMU Munich intraday bitcoin study finds larger price moves are followed by stronger percentage reversals. Costs not addressed in what I read. — [LMU repository](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/97651/)
- **[PR]** Daily cross-sectional reversal exists in small, illiquid coins but turns into daily momentum in the largest coins (see Family 3), which covers BTC, ETH and SOL. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)

#### Family 7. Arbitrage and MEV
- **[PR]** Makarov and Schoar, "Trading and arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets", Journal of Financial Economics 135(2) (2020) 293-319: 34 exchanges in 19 countries. At least USD 2 billion of potential arbitrage profit was left on the table in December 2017 to February 2018; the Korea premium reached 40% for several days; within a country, gaps between exchanges typically stay under 1%. Capital controls explain the cross-border gaps. — [Harvard Law forum summary by the authors](http://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/09/21/trading-and-arbitrage-in-cryptocurrency-markets/); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:2:p:293-319)
- **[PR]** Crépellière, Pelster and Zeisberger, "Arbitrage in the market for cryptocurrencies", Journal of Financial Markets 64 (2023): arbitrage opportunities still exist but their magnitude fell greatly from April 2018 onward. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v64y2023ics1386418123000150.html)
- **[WP]** Borri et al. (2025): across 510 bitcoin-fiat pairs on 165 exchanges, the 10th and 90th percentile price discounts versus Kraken are -1.8% and +3.7% for 2014-2025 and -2.1% and +3.5% after 2020. The gaps persist because capital controls, withdrawal limits and settlement frictions block the trade; the authors describe the returns on the investable subset as risk compensation, not arbitrage. — [arXiv 2510.14435, section 3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **[PR]** Triangular arbitrage: Muck, Schmidl and Wolf, "Wish or reality?", Finance Research Letters (2025), Binance high-frequency data. Most opportunities return 0-0.025%; after real fees the 18 evaluated opportunities yielded about USD 12-18 in total; measured latency from a European server was about 80 ms. - **[PR]** CEX-DEX arbitrage: Wu, Sui, Thiery and Pai, AFT 2025 (arXiv 2507.13023), August 2023 to March 2025. 19 searchers extracted USD 233.8 million from 7.2 million arbitrages. Three searchers took about three-quarters of volume and value overall and 90% of volume by Q1 2025. Active labelled searchers fell from 23 to 14 to 11. - **[WP]** Private-channel sandwiches on Ethereum, November 2024 to February 2025: 2,932 attacks, USD 409,236 of victim losses, USD 293,786 of attacker profit, one bot behind nearly two-thirds of private front-runs. — [arXiv 2512.17602](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17602)
- **[PR]** Solana (IMC 2026 paper, Jito data from early 2025): 521,903 sandwich attacks costing users more than USD 7.7 million. A news claim of USD 500 million over 16 months is much higher and I could not verify it. — [IMC 2026 paper](https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf)

#### Family 9. Grid, DCA and martingale bots
- **[WP]** Chen, Chen and Jang, "Dynamic Grid Trading Strategy: From Zero Expectation to Market Outperformance" (arXiv 2506.11921, June 2025), minute-level BTC and ETH data, January 2021 to July 2024: without a view on trend, the expected return of a traditional grid is essentially zero before fees, so fees make it negative. #### Family 10. Technical analysis rules with data-snooping controls
- **[PR]** Hudson and Urquhart, "Technical trading and cryptocurrencies", Annals of Operations Research (published online 2019): almost 15,000 rules from five classes on several coins, with multiple-hypothesis corrections. Many rules stay significant in-sample and break-even costs exceed typical crypto costs, **but there is no predictability for bitcoin in the out-of-sample period**; predictability remains in other coins. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1)
- **[PR]** Anghel, "A reality check on trading rule performance in the cryptocurrency market: Machine learning vs. technical analysis", Finance Research Letters 39 (2021), using White's Reality Check framework: trading rules do not provide benefits in crypto beyond those in traditional markets. Abstract only. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v39y2021ics1544612320304414.html)
- **[PR]** Detzel, Liu, Strauss, Zhou and Zhu, "Learning and predictability via technical analysis: Evidence from bitcoin and stocks with hard-to-value fundamentals", Financial Management 50(1) (2021) 107-137, is the main peer-reviewed support for moving-average rules on bitcoin. I could not retrieve its numbers. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finmgt/v50y2021i1p107-137.html)
- **[PR, contested]** The CTREND factor is built from 28 technical indicators and was reported to survive costs (JFQA 2025), but see the Wang and Pang re-audit under Family 3. — [JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0022109024000747); [arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01951)

### Inferences
- Ranking by weight of evidence for a US retail BTC / ETH / SOL account: (1) daily-frequency trend following, long or flat, volatility-sized: positive net evidence at 10 bps cost, but most of it comes from working papers and practitioner sources, and returns are concentrated in bull regimes. (2) Two-week cross-sectional momentum: robust gross through 2025, but it needs a broad coin universe and a short leg, with 30-60 bps costs per side and no practical way for a US retail account to short small coins, so it is at best a long-only tilt. (3) Carry: mechanically implementable on Coinbase or CME, but the yield is now near the risk-free rate.
- Families 5 to 10 (maker bots, DEX LP, hour-of-day rules, arbitrage, MEV, memecoin sniping, grids, indicator rules) have no credible evidence of positive net expectancy for a non-professional. Where money is made in those families it is made by a handful of vertically integrated or insider participants.
- Trend following in BTC and ETH is largely timed market exposure. Liquid large coins show momentum at daily to two-week horizons while reversal lives in illiquid small coins, so mean-reversion bots on BTC, ETH and SOL are trading against the documented sign of the effect.
- "Sharpe above 1.5" claims for crypto trend come from samples that include 2015-2021. The independent out-of-sample comparators (for example a one-month time-series momentum Sharpe of 0.65 over 2022-2024 in the AdaptiveTrend benchmarks) suggest a plain rule earns far less in recent data.

### Gaps
- I found no SOL-specific academic evidence for any family; SOL appears only as one constituent of multi-coin samples.
- I could not verify the Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown or the 25 and 50 bps results in Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon (SSRN blocked; summaries are partial). A secondary summary says short lookbacks have weakened recently while long ones remain robust; I could not trace that to the paper.
- I could not confirm whether Shen, Urquhart and Wang's intraday momentum gains are net of costs, or retrieve Detzel et al.'s moving-average Sharpe ratios.
- I found no peer-reviewed study of Bollinger-band or RSI mean reversion on BTC or ETH with costs; only blogs, scripts and master's theses.
- I found no independent study of Hummingbot or other retail market-making bot profitability, and none of Meteora DLMM or Solana memecoin LP outcomes (only protocol documentation and vendor research).
- I found no academic study of martingale or DCA "recovery" bots (3Commas-style) and no documented loss-rate statistics for them.
- AQR: I found no AQR publication covering crypto trend following.
- A dedicated academic paper on volatility-managed crypto portfolios did not surface; only Man Group and blog material.
- The AMF (France) retail CFD/FX loss study was not retrieved.

## Key Question 2: How have the edges decayed as crypto markets matured (ETFs in 2024, institutional market makers, tighter basis)?

### Takeaway
Decay is clearest and largest in carry and arbitrage: the perpetual carry Sharpe went from about 6 to negative in 2025 and the CME basis from about 20% to about 5%. Cross-sectional momentum has weakened only modestly, the size effect has mostly gone, and several technical-rule results already failed out of sample for bitcoin before the ETF era.

### Cited Findings
- **Carry.** Annualised Sharpe of the long-spot, short-perpetual bitcoin trade: 6.45 for August 2020 to May 2025, 4.06 from 2024, negative in 2025 (gross). The authors call this a "sharp compression" and question the sustainability of funding-harvesting products. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **Funding.** Bitcoin open-interest-weighted funding: 11.0% (2024), 4.9% (2025), 2.2% (2026 to 11 August), Ethena's data. — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/)
- **Ethena.** USDe supply reached about USD 14 billion in September 2025 (peak near USD 15 billion) and fell to about USD 4 billion by August 2026; Ethena began adding equity perpetuals (funding about 14-17.5%) to its backing because crypto funding yields shrank. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/)
- **Ethena yield.** sUSDe yielded above 20% in earlier euphoric periods and about 3.5-4.5% in 2026 (search-summary figures from Blockworks and Forklog; one outlet cites a different peak). — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/newsletter/the-breakdown/issue/post_999b16ee-0fe0-4631-a1d0-2aa15a1d18a7)
- **CME basis.** About 20% in late 2024; below 10% all of 2025; about 8% in September 2025; about 5% in April 2026 versus about 4.5% risk-free. — [Yahoo / CoinDesk](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/federal-rate-cut-could-spark-091651943.html); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/396722/cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand)
- **Basis-trade unwind.** CME bitcoin futures average daily open interest fell to USD 7.2 billion in early April 2026, the lowest since February 2024, after five straight monthly declines, and March 2026 volume was down nearly 50% from the January 2025 peak; hedge funds cut net shorts by 89% from the December 2024 peak; Binance overtook CME as the largest bitcoin futures venue. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/396722/cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand); [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/hedge-funds-unwind-bitcoin-basis-trades-as-cme-short-positions-fall-89)
- **Cross-exchange arbitrage.** Magnitudes fell greatly from April 2018 (Crépellière et al., JFM 2023). Cross-country price gaps are unchanged after 2020 but are not tradable. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v64y2023ics1386418123000150.html); [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **Momentum and size.** Two-week momentum long-short: 2.6% per week full sample versus 2.1% after 2020 (gross). Size: -2.3% versus -0.9% per week, the latter significant only at 10%. — [arXiv 2510.14435, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **Trend factor.** The CTREND authors note "an inevitable decline in profitability over time" within 2015-2022 (3.26% per week in the later half versus 3.87% overall). The November 2026 re-audit puts the corrected premium near zero. — [JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0022109024000747); [arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01951)
- **Technical rules.** Bitcoin showed no out-of-sample predictability in Hudson and Urquhart's test even though other coins did. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1)
- **MEV.** Ethereum sandwich extraction fell about 75% in a year (USD 10 million to USD 2.5 million a month) and CEX-DEX arbitrage consolidated from 23 to 11 active searchers. — [Cointelegraph Research](https://cointelegraph.com/research/exclusive-data-from-eigenphi-reveals-that-sandwich-attacks-on-ethereum-have-waned); [arXiv 2507.13023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13023)
- **Market structure.** Weekend share of bitcoin volume fell from 28% (2019) to about 16% in the first half of 2024, which Kaiko attributes partly to spot ETFs; CME reports weekend volatility at about 75% of weekday levels. — [The Block on Kaiko](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2024-06-30-bitcoin-sees-all-time-low-weekend-trading-levels-following-etf-approvals-kaiko-302705); [CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/2026/aligning-cryptocurrency-derivatives-with-spot-markets-measuring-the-247-trading-opportunity.html)
- **Asset behaviour.** Bitcoin weekly volatility fell from 11.28% before 2020 to 8.75% after; the coin market's correlation with global equities rose from 0.01 to 0.39; maximum weekly-data drawdown -83.6% full sample and -74.9% after 2020. — [arXiv 2510.14435, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)

### Inferences
- The order of decay matches the order of capacity and ease of entry: riskless-looking trades (basis, funding, same-country arbitrage) were competed away first once ETF-era institutional capital and synthetic-dollar products could warehouse them. Risk-bearing trades (momentum, trend) have decayed less.
- Carry is now cyclical, not structural: it pays in leveraged bull phases (funding near 30% annualised in early October 2025 per FTI) and pays nothing or goes negative otherwise. A bot that holds the trade constantly earns about the Treasury-bill rate with tail risk attached.
- Lower volatility and higher equity correlation after 2020 mean smaller trends to capture and less diversification value, which should lower trend-following Sharpe ratios versus 2015-2021 backtests.
- Thinner weekends concentrate gap and liquidation risk in hours when ETF and CME participants are absent.

### Gaps
- Year-by-year average funding for 2020-2023 was not found from a neutral source (only Ethena's long-run 7.8-9.15% averages).
- No study measures bitcoin time-series momentum or hour-of-day effects specifically before and after the January 2024 ETF launch.
- The BitMEX nine-year funding study and the CME basis explainer could not be opened.
- All 2026 funding and Ethena figures are issuer-sourced and not independently audited.

- Automation does not change the population result: the Hyperliquid data show algorithmic takers as a group roughly break even and only algorithmic makers profit, and those makers are professional firms.
- Realised-only memecoin statistics (CoinGecko's 73% profitable in April 2026) are biased upward because wallets holding dead tokens are not counted as losers.

### Gaps

- Hyperliquid's share of wallets that lost (as opposed to aggregate dollars) is in the SSRN full text, which I did not retrieve.
- No regulator publishes loss rates for US retail crypto futures accounts comparable to the NFA forex disclosures.

## Key Question 4: What cost level (fees plus spread, in basis points per round trip) kills the typical intraday crypto signal?

### Takeaway
Measured intraday edges in BTC are about 1 bp per round trip at second-to-minute horizons and roughly 8-9 bps per trade for the best published hour-of-day rule, so any retail cost above a few basis points per round trip erases them. Only multi-day to multi-week signals, with break-even costs in the tens to low hundreds of basis points, have room for retail fees.

### Cited Findings
- Order-book-imbalance taker signal on the Binance bitcoin perpetual: about +1 bp per round trip gross, -1.96 bps net at a 1.5 bps taker fee (the best tier). Maker versions net -0.43 to -0.49 bps per trade even with a 0.5 bps rebate. Post-fill adverse selection is -0.06 to -1.16 bps within one second. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)
- Triangular arbitrage on Binance: gross opportunities are mostly 0-2.5 bps and are eliminated by standard fees. — [Muck, Schmidl and Wolf](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/bitstreams/8b9ae900-017a-4bed-94b9-609c16e89945/download)
- Hour-of-day rule (hold bitcoin 21:00-23:00 UTC daily): 33% a year gross from one round trip per day, with no costs deducted in the source. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)
- Taiwan day traders: costs turned -11.5 bps per day gross into -28.9 bps per day net, a drag of about 17 bps per day. — [Barber et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)
- Six-hour trend system on perpetuals (low-credibility preprint): Sharpe 2.87 at 0 bps, 2.41 at 4 bps, 2.01 at 8 bps and 1.62 at 12 bps per trade. The same system on one-hour bars at 4 bps had Sharpe 1.54 with 847 trades a month, versus 2.41 with 142 trades a month on six-hour bars. — [arXiv 2602.11708, Tables 4 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11708)
- Weekly cross-sectional trend factor: turnover 68% per week; break-even cost 141 bps per trade for all coins and 125 bps for the largest 100; academic cost assumptions for crypto cross-sections are 30-40 bps per side, with 50-60 bps as a stress case. The effect is no longer significant if rebalanced less often than about monthly. — [JFQA, Table 9](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0022109024000747)
- Daily Donchian trend ensemble: tested at 10, 25 and 50 bps per trade, with headline results at 10 bps. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)
- Grid trading: expected return is about zero before fees, so any positive fee makes it negative. — [arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)
- Fee levels: Coinbase's US perpetual-style futures start at 0.02% (2 bps) per contract side; the best Binance perpetual tier assumed by Albers et al. is a 1.5 bps taker fee and a 0.5 bps maker rebate. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/331180/coinbase-opens-perpetuals-trading-us-customers); [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)
- Liquidity can vanish: on 10 October 2025 bitcoin top-of-book depth fell more than 90% and spreads widened from single-digit basis points to double-digit percentages. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on the Quantpedia rule: 33% a year over 365 round trips is about 8-9 bps per trade gross (about 7.8 bps on a log basis). A round-trip cost of 8 bps or more removes the whole edge, and typical retail spot taker fees are well above that. This is my calculation, not a figure in the source.
- Rule of thumb from the evidence: sub-hour signals in BTC are worth about 0-2 bps per round trip and are untradeable for anyone paying taker fees; hourly-to-daily seasonal signals are worth under 10 bps; daily trend signals survive 10-25 bps; weekly cross-sectional signals survive 30-60 bps in the published version. Holding period, not signal cleverness, determines whether retail costs are bearable.
- For a US retail account the practical floor is about 4 bps per round trip on Coinbase perpetual-style futures (2 bps per side) plus spread and funding, which already exceeds every measured sub-hour edge.
- The AdaptiveTrend cost table implies roughly 0.1 Sharpe lost per extra basis point per trade at about 140 trades a month; a strategy trading six times as often (hourly bars) loses proportionally more.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve current retail fee schedules for Coinbase Advanced, Kraken or IBKR crypto spot, so the round-trip spot cost for a small US account is not sourced here.
- No peer-reviewed study reports break-even costs for intraday momentum or reversal on BTC, ETH and SOL using post-2022 data.
- Slippage and spread for SOL at retail size were not found in any source.

## Key Question 5: What are the tail risks of the strategies that do work (exchange failure, liquidation cascades such as October 10 2025, depegs)?

### Takeaway
The strategies with a positive record carry fat left tails that backtests miss: hedged carry positions can be forcibly unhedged by auto-deleveraging and collateral mark-downs, trend systems face deep drawdowns and gaps when liquidity disappears, and all venue-based strategies carry exchange-failure risk. The 10 October 2025 event is the reference stress case.

### Cited Findings
- **10 October 2025.** More than USD 19 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated in about a day, affecting about 1.6 million traders (Coinglass, described as conservative). The trigger was a US threat of 100% tariffs on China. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/heres-investors-know-weekends-massive-170219939.html)
- **Speed.** About 70% of liquidations (USD 6.93 billion) happened in 40 minutes (20:50-21:30 UTC), with USD 3.21 billion in the single minute at 21:15 UTC; USD 16.7 billion of USD 19.38 billion hit longs. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)
- **Liquidity.** Bitcoin top-of-book depth on key venues fell by more than 90%, spreads reached double-digit percentages, many market makers withdrew, and some exchange interfaces froze so traders could not manage positions or move collateral. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)
- **Auto-deleveraging hit hedged books.** Some well-hedged shorts were reduced or fully closed; "ADL can involuntarily close profitable shorts, turning a hedged portfolio into a naked one during periods of stress." — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)
- **Hyperliquid ADL.** ADL closed USD 2.1 billion of positions in 12 minutes. Chitra (arXiv 2512.01112) estimates the production mechanism over-cut winning traders by USD 45.0-51.7 million, corresponding to about USD 653.6 million of positions closed beyond what was needed; Hyperliquid's founder disputes that traders were net harmed. — [arXiv 2512.01112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01112); [Protos](https://protos.com/outdated-algorithm-caused-650m-excess-losses-on-hyperliquid-report)
- **Depeg and collateral.** USDe traded in the mid-USD 0.60s on Binance (about a 35% discount) while staying near USD 1 elsewhere. Binance's local price fed its margin system, marking down collateral and pushing thousands of unified-margin accounts into liquidation. Whether this counts as a true depeg is disputed. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity); [Cointelegraph Magazine](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/19b-crypto-market-crash-leverage-china-tariffs)
- **Pre-crash carry signal.** Perpetual funding rose from about 10% to nearly 30% annualised by 6 October 2025, days before the crash. Afterwards open interest fell more than 40% and several venues cut leverage caps and raised collateral haircuts. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)
- **Carry and crashes.** High carry predicts future price crashes, and cash-and-carry positions are exposed to margin spikes and liquidations in drawdowns. — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- **Negative funding.** Funding was negative on about 16-17.5% of days historically, with a longest streak of 13 days. — [Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/protocol-overview/risks/funding-risk)
- **Exchange failure.** FTX and Alameda failed in November 2022, months after Terra-Luna; Borri et al. cite them as the practical face of limits to arbitrage in crypto. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
- **Trend and momentum drawdowns.** Bitcoin's maximum drawdown on weekly data is -83.6% (-74.9% after 2020). Time-series momentum loses in falling markets (Han, Kang and Ryu), and the AdaptiveTrend system had a bear-regime Sharpe of -0.31. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435); [AUT working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf); [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11708)
- **Total liquidations.** Coinglass counted USD 154.64 billion of liquidations in 2025. Liquidation notional is not the same as losses. — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/crypto-futures-trading-mistakes-2025)
- **Weekend gaps.** With ETF and CME participants absent, weekend bitcoin liquidity is at record lows. — [The Block on Kaiko](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2024-06-30-bitcoin-sees-all-time-low-weekend-trading-levels-following-etf-approvals-kaiko-302705)
- **Token-launch risk.** 98.6% of Pump.fun tokens collapse and about 93% of Raydium pools studied showed soft-rug behaviour. — [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)

### Inferences
- A "delta-neutral" funding or basis position held on an offshore perpetual venue is not neutral in a cascade. The short leg can be auto-deleveraged at the worst moment, leaving unhedged spot, and cross-margined collateral can be marked down on a venue-local price. Mitigations suggested by the evidence: low leverage on the short leg, collateral in cash or T-bills, isolated margin, and a US-regulated, centrally cleared venue (CME or Coinbase Derivatives) even at lower yield.
- Carry yield and crash risk rise together: the trade paid most (about 30% funding) in the week before the largest liquidation event on record, so sizing carry up when funding is high is pro-cyclical.
- For trend following the tail is slower: long drawdowns in sideways or bear regimes and gap risk through stops when depth falls 90%. Backtests that assume fills at stop prices understate this.
- Any stop-loss or liquidation-price logic in a retail bot should be stress-tested against 10 October 2025 conditions: spreads of several percent, minutes-long venue outages and no ability to move collateral.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies total losses to basis or funding-arbitrage traders on 10 October 2025; the evidence is qualitative plus the contested Hyperliquid estimate.
- I found no documented account of how positions on US-regulated venues (CME, Coinbase Derivatives) behaved on 10 October 2025.
- The count "35,000 ADL events on 20,000 users" on Hyperliquid appears only in secondary coverage and was not traced to a primary source.
- No dataset quantifies retail losses from centralised-exchange insolvency after FTX.
