# Retail costs swallow intraday forex edges

No open-source intraday forex technical-analysis repository published or maintained between 2024 and November 2026 has a verifiable live track record after costs, and the published research gives no reason to expect one: the realistic prior for a self-built rule is a gross edge about the size of the spread and a net result near zero. The closest thing to live evidence is one project's self-published OANDA transaction exports, which show a loss of $1,637.71 on a $2,500 stake across 1,190 trades despite a 78% win rate. The repositories worth studying are worth studying for plumbing and risk controls (order handling, server-side stops, ledgers, cost-aware backtests), not for entries, and GitHub's forex topics are also seeded with star-inflated lure repositories that reading alone can screen out. The build toolkit is mature for every component except OANDA execution: TA-Lib 0.8 for indicators, NautilusTrader on Dukascopy bid and ask ticks for backtests, `arch` and skfolio for snooping control, and Interactive Brokers with ib_async for CME micro FX futures are all maintained, while the OANDA client, the financing model, session logic, the news filter, the kill switch and reconciliation must be written by hand. Costs are similar on the two lawful paths: about 1.4 pips (roughly 1.2 basis points) per EUR/USD round trip at OANDA US, and about $2.05 per Micro EUR/USD contract (roughly 1.4 basis points) at Interactive Brokers when crossing the spread, against an average 2026 daily range of only about 65 pips. That arithmetic rules out anything faster than roughly one-hour holds and one or two trades a session, and a realistic edge of 0.04R to 0.10R per trade needs about 600 to 3,900 trades to tell apart from zero. The defensible plan is to test three evidence-backed ideas (the EUR/USD session drift, hourly mean reversion and round-number conditioning) under a pre-registered protocol that expects a null result, then paper trade and trade at minimum size mainly to verify plumbing and costs. **Nothing in this report is legal, tax or investment advice.**

*Conventions.* All repository and package figures were collected on 23 November 2026. In the repository sections, stars, forks, licence and dates are exact GitHub Search API values, and "last push" (any branch) stands in for last commit; in the toolkit sections, stars are the rounded figures shown by shields.io badges, so the same project can appear as 1,277 in one place and 1.3k in another. Strategy and risk-control descriptions come from reading each README; nothing was downloaded, cloned or run, and commit histories, file trees and CI runs were not inspected. "Unverified" marks a figure that could not be confirmed against a primary source. "Researcher arithmetic" marks a calculation built on cited inputs, not a number published by the source. One basis point (bp) is 0.01%; with EUR/USD near 1.15, one pip is about 0.87 bp. "R" is the amount risked on a trade.

On venue fit, only the OANDA-based Python and JavaScript projects, the FOREX.com client and the LEAN bot target venues a US retail resident can use; almost every MetaTrader 5 Expert Advisor assumes an offshore CFD account, several need hedging accounts, and none of the READMEs read states US first-in-first-out compliance. The US rules are specific. The retail security deposit is 2% of notional on major currencies and 5% on others (50:1 and 20:1), a dealer "may not carry offsetting positions in a customer account", and positions are offset oldest first ([NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)). OANDA's own note says that under these rules an Expert Advisor's opposing order on the same pair is rejected or closes the older position, and that EAs which close by per-trade stop or target without regard to age "may error or stop working" ([OANDA](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trade-tap-blog/trading-knowledge/the-nfas-fifo-rule-challenges-solutions-forex-expert-advisors)). A US resident forking any of this code should therefore assume one net position per symbol, no opposite-side orders and exits that close the oldest ticket first. Grid, hedge-chain and multi-ticket scale-in designs do not survive that translation. No repository found targets CME micro FX futures with an intraday technical strategy, which makes the futures path an open niche, not a covered one.

## Lure repositories are planted across forex topics, and reading catches most of them

No security-vendor report was found that names a specific trojanised MetaTrader or forex-bot repository or PyPI package. The published reports cover the same playbook against crypto and prediction-market bots. What follows on forex is therefore first-hand observation from the research, unverified by any vendor, and none of the repositories named was opened beyond its README or analysed. Treat every one of them as hostile: do not download or run anything from them.

The clearest case is a repository tagged `forex-bot` with 75 stars, 0 forks and no licence, created on 8 November 2026, which advertises a "pre-activated" 1.9 GB Windows build and whose README points to a PowerShell script (`Fix.ps1`) on a Microsoft-lookalike domain ([TerminusPotter/quant-trading-bot](https://github.com/TerminusPotter/quant-trading-bot)). A second pattern is clustering: thirteen repositories were created on the same day, 28 June 2026, all with language "HTML", no licence, 115 to 132 stars and 0 to 2 forks, search-optimised titles under the MT5 and expert-advisor topics, and a single "download" link to the owner's github.io page ([example](https://github.com/AbdulWasay13/TradingView-Pipe-Executor)). A third cluster from 13 September 2026 promotes "non-repainting" MT4 strategies through a URL shortener, with no code language detected ([example](https://github.com/lightningfast66/forex-mpx-zones-strategy-mt4)). "ninjatrader-bot" lures show the same profile at 338 to 350 stars and zero forks ([example](https://github.com/colonykazekagedoor/ninjatrader-bot)), and two repositories described as MT5 tools are labelled C# and still contain "your-username" placeholder links. The June cluster's creation date falls two days after the start date Arctic Wolf gives for a fake-GitHub-page infostealer campaign that used the same landing-page mechanism, but no link between the two has been established ([Arctic Wolf](https://arcticwolf.com/resources/blog/fake-github-repositories-deliver-boryptgrab-lineage-infostealer/); the article was read only as a search summary).

The background research is solid even where the forex specifics are not. A Carnegie Mellon, Socket and NC State study estimated about 6 million suspected fake GitHub stars through December 2024, mostly promoting short-lived malware repositories "masquerading as pirating software, game cheats, or cryptocurrency bots" ([arXiv 2412.13459](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2)). Kaspersky's "GitVenom" campaign found more than 200 fake repositories, some with AI-written READMEs ([Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/about/press-releases/kaspersky-exposes-hidden-malware-on-github-stealing-personal-data-and-485000-in-bitcoin)). The package-registry precedents are crypto, not forex: a PyPI package that hijacked exchange orders and stole API keys ([JFrog](https://jfrog.com/blog/malicious-pypi-package-hijacks-mexc-orders-steals-crypto-tokens/)) and one that posed as a trading bot ([Checkmarx](https://checkmarx.com/blog/cryptocurrency-enthusiasts-targeted-in-multi-vector-supply-chain-attack/)). Cracked software is a separate route: Trend Micro documents infostealers delivered through fake installers and cracks in password-protected archives ([Trend Micro](https://trendmicro.com/en_us/research/25/a/how-cracks-and-installers-bring-malware-to-your-device.html)), which is relevant to "cracked" Expert Advisors.

Softer scam patterns sit beside the malware. They include compiled-only "open source" (a time-limited `.ex5` with a contact for the licensed build), backtest headlines used as marketing next to broker affiliate links and paid upsells, Telegram handles as the support channel, and repositories published by commercial signal products. A related pattern needs no bad intent: legitimate projects also ship risky conveniences, such as a prebuilt `.exe` with a `setup.ps1`, prebuilt `.ex5` files, or bridge binaries. Build from source and ignore the binaries.

Vetting by reading only follows directly from these observations, and the research behind this report is itself an example of it: READMEs were read as raw text and nothing was cloned or executed. Start with metadata. High stars with near-zero forks, a creation date shared with look-alike repositories, language "HTML" or no code language for a supposed Expert Advisor, and no licence are each a reason to stop. Then read the README for a single download button, a shortener link, an `.exe`, a `.ps1`, a password-protected archive, the words "pre-activated" or "cracked", or a request to put an MT5 login, account password or API key in a config file before any code can be reviewed. Then read the file list and source in the browser: the strategy logic must exist as source and not only as `.ex4`, `.ex5` or `.exe`; install scripts and dependency lists deserve a line-by-line read, because stealers run at install time through package lifecycle hooks before any code is imported ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/2025-blockchain-and-cryptocurrency-threat-report)); and any hard-coded URL, webhook or outbound call unrelated to the broker's API is disqualifying. Finally, look for honesty signals, which the best repositories have in common: stated losses or null results, an explicit cost model, caveats about tick quality, tests and CI, and a plain statement about whether martingale or grid logic is present. A repository that passes all of this is still only reading material. If code is ever run, it belongs in a dedicated machine or container with no browser profiles, wallets or SSH keys, with dependencies pinned to exact versions.

### Indicator libraries: one implementation for research and live removes the commonest mismatch

TA-Lib is now the clear first choice because it installs from binary wheels and, in 0.8.x, ships a handle-based streaming API whose values are documented as "identical to the one the Function API reports for that bar": `update` commits a closed bar and `peek` evaluates a forming bar "committing nothing" ([ta-lib-python README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TA-Lib/ta-lib-python/master/README.md)). talipp is the pure-Python alternative, with O(1) updates and support for revising the last value ([talipp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nardew/talipp/main/README.md)). NautilusTrader's and LEAN's built-in indicators are incremental by design, though that was inferred from their event-driven architecture and not confirmed from a quoted page. Batch-only libraries such as [pandas-ta-classic](https://github.com/xgboosted/pandas-ta-classic) (MIT, 224 indicators, active), `ta`, finta, polars-ta, freqtrade's `technical` and vectorbt's indicator factory are safe live only when recomputed on closed bars with a long enough warm-up. The original pandas-ta should be avoided in 2026: its repository could not be found, PyPI holds only two beta releases with no declared licence, and a fork's page carries the upstream notice that the library would be archived without additional support by 1 July 2026 ([PyPI pandas-ta](https://pypi.org/pypi/pandas-ta/json); [pandas-ta-kozmoai](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-ta-kozmoai/)).

The tools prone to look-ahead or repainting are the swing, zigzag, pivot and smart-money functions, which need future bars by construction. The smart-money-concepts library defines a swing high as "the highest high out of the swing_length amount of candles before and after", with an example length of 50, so that label is only knowable 50 bars later ([smart-money-concepts README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/README.md)). On TradingView, `request.security()` with look-ahead on and no offset leaks future data, and strategies that calculate on every tick behave differently on historical and real-time bars ([Pine Script repainting docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/)). The other standard causes of backtest-to-live mismatch are recursive indicators (EMA, RSI, ATR) run on a short live window when the backtest used long history, which TA-Lib exposes as an "unstable period" that defaults to zero; computing on the forming bar live but on closed bars in the test; multi-timeframe merges that stamp a higher-timeframe bar at its open; and volume or market-profile tools on spot forex, where "volume" is tick count or quoted size and not traded volume.

### Backtesters: none models forex costs correctly out of the box

NautilusTrader comes closest. It fills against best bid and ask quote ticks, offers netting accounts and margin models, and its documentation is blunt that bar data "cannot establish intrabar price order, spread, depth, or queue position" ([data and venues](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/data-and-venues.md)). Three defaults need attention. The fill model's slippage probability is 0.0 and a touched limit order fills with probability 1.0 ([fill models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/fill-models.md)). There is no native next-bar-open fill mode, and using the current bar's open inside the bar handler "introduces look-ahead" ([bar execution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/bar-execution.md)). And the built-in FX rollover module uses raw OECD short-rate differentials with no dealer mark-up, and its source comment says Wednesday and Friday rollovers are tripled ([fx_rollover.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/crates/backtest/src/modules/fx_rollover.rs)), whereas OANDA triples only Wednesday and charges nothing at weekends ([OANDA financing](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trading/financing-fees/)). Check a one-week run before trusting any swap total. Use the stable 1.231.0 line: the 2.0 release candidates "are intended for community testing" and are not recommended for production ([NautilusTrader README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/README.md)).

LEAN is second and has one unique advantage: its forex data is OANDA's own, as quote bars from January 2007, and it has an OANDA brokerage model. That model's defaults are an immediate fill, no slippage, zero fees because the data already embeds OANDA's spread, leverage up to 50 times and no margin interest, so slippage and financing must be added ([LEAN OANDA model](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/brokerages/supported-models/oanda)). Minute data for local use costs $9.36 per pair-year ([LEAN CLI forex datasets](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/lean-cli/datasets/quantconnect/forex)). backtesting.py (AGPL-3.0, version 0.6.6) takes a constant `spread=`, applies commission twice per trade since 0.6.0, closes trades first-in-first-out by default and has no swap or slippage model; it runs one instrument on one price series ([backtesting.py changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kernc/backtesting.py/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Open-source vectorbt applies fractional fees and slippage only, and its README places limit orders and leverage in the paid PRO edition ([vectorbt README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/README.md)); it is for fast screening. Backtrader has had no release since April 2023.

The two platform testers have the same weakness in different forms. MetaTrader 5's tester always trades at bid and ask, but only "real ticks" mode has a spread that changes within a minute bar; generated ticks use a spread fixed per bar ([MetaTrader 5 help](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/algotrading/tick_generation)). TradingView's broker emulator fills from chart bars with an assumed path inside each bar and "assumes that no gaps exist inside each chart bar"; the Bar Magnifier that improves on this is a Premium and Ultimate feature ([Pine Script strategies docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/strategies/)). Any engine that fills stops and targets inside a single bar on one price series is guessing the path, so results on one- to five-minute bars with tight exits should not be trusted from backtesting.py or TradingView. No engine enforces the US first-in-first-out rule by name; backtesting.py's default and NautilusTrader's netting mode are the closest. The design choice that removes the largest modelling gap is to be flat before 5 pm New York time, which makes swap irrelevant and leaves spread at entry and exit as the cost to get right.

### Historical data: Dukascopy is free and deep, and it is not your broker's feed

Dukascopy's free history, tick-level bid and ask over many years, is the cheapest multi-year source, and two maintained downloaders fetch it. Its defects are structural. In Dukascopy's own words, ticks "are neither orders nor trades. We reflect only best ask and bid available in our system", and volumes are quoted sizes ([Elite Trader thread quoting Dukascopy](https://elitetrader.com/et/threads/interpreting-dukascopy-tick-data.352792/)). Forum reports, dated 2012 to 2018, add timestamp confusion, missing ticks around weekends and daylight-saving changes, and a price-scaling divisor issue for yen crosses and gold ([Dukascopy forum](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/forex/jforex/forum/viewtopic.php?p=64464)). One practitioner found backtests on Dukascopy and another feed "drastically different" and was advised to test on the feed the live broker supplies ([MultiCharts forum](https://www.multicharts.com/discussion/viewtopic.php?p=101017&view=show)). HistData's one-minute files are bid-only and stamped in Eastern Standard Time without daylight saving, which shifts sessions by an hour for half the year ([FX-1-Minute-Data](https://github.com/philipperemy/FX-1-Minute-Data)); they suit coarse sanity checks only. OANDA's candle endpoint returns bid, ask or mid at granularities from five seconds upward, 5,000 candles per request, aligned by default to 5 pm New York ([OANDA pricing endpoints](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/pricing-ep/)); its history depth is not documented. Interactive Brokers bars are free for forex with an account, but bars of 30 seconds or less reach back only six months and pacing limits apply ([IBKR historical data limitations](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/tws-api/doc/market-data-historical/historical-data-limitations/introduction)). For CME futures there is no free tick history; Databento's CME plan renews at $199 a month ([Databento](https://databento.com/blog/updates-to-subscription-pricing)). FirstRate's forex bars are a single price series with no bid and ask ([FirstRate](https://firstratedata.com/ai/datasets/fx-intraday)).

The practical consequence is a two-feed rule: research on Dukascopy, then re-run the final candidate on the execution venue's own data (OANDA bid and ask candles, or futures bars for the futures path) and require the result to hold on both. Before trusting any set, confirm timestamps are UTC, flag quotes inside the weekend window, detect gaps in liquid hours, reject ticks where ask is at or below bid, check yen and metal scaling against a known price, and build bars from bid and ask separately. Spreads by session were not obtainable from any primary source in this research, so hourly spread statistics should be computed from OANDA bid and ask one-minute candles before any cost assumption is fixed.

### Execution and connectivity for venues a US resident can use

**OANDA US spot.** The v20 API allows 120 REST requests per second, and its price stream delivers "at most 4 prices per second" per instrument, so it is a throttled quote stream and not a tick feed ([OANDA development guide](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/development-guide/); [pricing endpoints](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/pricing-ep/)). OANDA's recommended pattern is to request account details once at startup, store the snapshot and transaction ID, and then poll account changes from that ID ([OANDA best practices](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/best-practices/)). The API definition has not changed since version 3.0.25 in September 2018 ([OANDA release notes](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/release-notes/)), which is why stale wrappers still function: oandapyV20 was last released in August 2021 and the official `v20` bindings in September 2018. NautilusTrader's integration list contains Interactive Brokers but no OANDA adapter ([NautilusTrader README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/README.md)). Because the API is frozen, a compact client covering candles, the pricing stream, orders and account changes is a modest job; the researchers' estimate of 200 to 300 lines is a judgement, not a measurement. OANDA US lists TradingView, MT4 and its own platform, and MT5 is not listed for the US entity ([OANDA US platforms](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trading/platforms/)).

**CME micro FX futures.** ib_async, the successor to ib_insync, is at 2.1.0 (December 2025, BSD) and needs IB Gateway or TWS running ([ib_async README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ib-api-reloaded/ib_async/main/README.md)). pysystemtrade (GPL-3.0, 3.6k stars, active) is "a fully automated system for futures trading (for interactive brokers)" built on it, and is the best open reference for a production futures bot ([pysystemtrade README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robcarver17/pysystemtrade/master/README.md)). At Tradovate, API access is a $25 a month add-on that requires a funded account of at least $1,000, and streaming market data through the API additionally requires a CME licence agreement ([CrossTrade blog](https://crosstrade.io/blog/tradovate-api-add-on-fee-cme-license-webhook/)); a community post puts that licence at $500 a month, which is unverified ([Tradovate community](https://community.tradovate.com/t/whats-the-deal-with-market-subscriptions-and-api-access-to-charts-do-we-need-another-payment/11039.md)). Tooling that keeps IB Gateway logged in and restarts it, and the cost of IBKR's CME FX market-data subscription, were not researched.

**Interactive Brokers spot forex is not a path.** Earlier research in this project found IBKR stating that US clients "generally must be Eligible Contract Participants", broadly assets above $10 million, to trade leveraged forex ([IBKR FAQ](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/support/faq/content/am-resident-u-s-trade-67416550)). That page could only be read through a search extract and was not re-verified, so confirm it with the broker; if it holds, IBKR is a futures venue for this project and a currency-conversion venue otherwise.

**MetaTrader at US dealers.** FOREX.com US markets MT5 to US clients and offers a REST API on request to account holders ([FOREX.com MT5](https://www.forex.com/en-us/lp/metatrader-5/); [FOREX.com API trading](https://forex.com/en-us/premium-trader-tools/api-trading)); Trading.com US offers MT5 ([MetaQuotes listing](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/find-broker/trading-com-us)); tastyfx offers MT4 ([tastyfx](https://tastyfx.com/platforms/metatrader-4)), and its API offering could not be confirmed because the help page returned an error. The official MetaTrader5 Python package (5.0.6231, September 2026) drives a running terminal and is Windows-only; [mt5linux](https://github.com/lucas-campagna/mt5linux) runs it under Wine, and MetaApi is a proprietary cloud bridge from $30 a month ([metaapi.cloud](https://metaapi.cloud/)). cTrader is effectively closed to US residents after Spotware restricted US onboarding in 2026 ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/ctrader-restricts-us-prop-firm-access-following-internal-regulatory-assessment/)).

**TradingView webhook bridges.** TradersPost lists OANDA, Interactive Brokers, Tradovate and NinjaTrader; its free tier auto-submits paper trades only, and paid plans are $49, $99 and $199 a month ([TradersPost pricing](https://traderspost.io/pricing)). PineConnector is MetaTrader-only at $59 to $199 a month and claims "typical end-to-end latency under 1 second" ([PineConnector FAQ](https://www.pineconnector.com/pages/faq)). The open-source tradingview-webhooks-bot (GPL-3.0, last commit February 2024) has no broker built in. These bridges suit bar-close logic on 15-minute bars and above, they put strategy logic in Pine where repainting applies, and they add a paid intermediary to every order. Whether TradingView webhooks require a paid TradingView plan was not verified.

### Calendar filters, overfitting controls and monitoring

pandas_market_calendars (MIT, version 5.6.1) ships a forex calendar and several CME calendars, and exchange_calendars includes a CME calendar, but both answer only "open or closed" ([pandas_market_calendars README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsheftel/pandas_market_calendars/master/README.rst)). No maintained library for forex sessions, opening ranges or the rollover hour was found; these are small enough to write and test with the standard library's time-zone support. For news, the Forex Factory weekly calendar export is free but limited to two requests per five minutes per file type according to a forum post ([Forex Factory thread](https://www.forexfactory.com/thread/post/15041460)), so fetch it once and cache it. FRED's release-dates endpoint needs only a free key and must be told to include future dates ([FRED API docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/releases_dates.html)). Trading Economics is quoted by third parties at $149 a month, which is unverified, and investpy is dead.

For overfitting, `arch` 9.0.0 implements Hansen's SPA test, White's Reality Check, the Romano-Wolf StepM procedure and the model confidence set, with stationary and block bootstraps ([arch source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bashtage/arch/main/arch/bootstrap/multiple_comparison.py)). skfolio provides `CombinatorialPurgedCV` and `WalkForward` splitters ([skfolio docs](https://skfolio.org/generated/skfolio.model_selection.CombinatorialPurgedCV.html)). Optuna searches parameters but has no walk-forward logic of its own, and every trial it runs counts toward the multiple-testing denominator. pypbo covers the probability of backtest overfitting and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio. QuantStats has a built-in Monte Carlo on returns. mlfinlab has been closed-source since 2021 and should not be planned around.

For operations, Healthchecks is the dead-man's-switch (a monitor that alerts when the bot stops checking in), Apprise sends alerts to Telegram, email or Pushover through one interface, and Supervisor or systemd restarts the process. freqtrade is crypto-only but remains the best open example of a dry-run mode and Telegram control to copy ([freqtrade README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/README.md)). OANDA's partner pages reference Equinix NY4 but do not tie the v20 endpoint to a data centre, so measure latency from any candidate server; for hour-scale holds it will not matter.

The hand-written list is therefore: the OANDA client with reconnecting streams and transaction-ID reconciliation; an OANDA financing model with the 1% administration fee and the Wednesday triple; session, opening-range and rollover-hour helpers; the join between a news calendar and the strategy clock; the kill switch with daily-loss, position, stale-data and heartbeat checks, plus startup reconciliation against broker state; a bid/ask-aware bar builder if NautilusTrader's aggregation is not used; first-in-first-out and no-hedging position logic; and, on the futures path, contract-roll handling. This matches what the repository survey found reusable: plumbing and controls, not entry logic.

## Published evidence finds predictability, not profit after retail costs

The evidence for intraday technical trading in major forex pairs is weak, and the weakness has a consistent shape. **No intraday, price-only rule family in major pairs was found with all three of a post-2010 sample, a formal data-snooping correction and positive net-of-cost out-of-sample returns.** The rigorous studies are almost all on daily data, and the intraday studies repeatedly find real predictability at zero cost whose break-even cost sits at or below what a trader pays.

### Daily rules set the base rate: nothing significant in developed currencies since the early 1990s

The largest test, Hsu, Taylor and Wang, ran 21,195 rules on 30 currencies over up to 45 years with a stepwise snooping test and costs deducted. Over the full sample the best developed-currency rules earned more than 6% a year net with Sharpe ratios near 0.6, but by sub-period there were "only a few predictive rules found in the 1980s, and none since the 1992-1996 period". In the true out-of-sample test, rules chosen on data to 2011 and traded from 2012 to September 2015 gave the developed-currency portfolio **1.35% a year at a Sharpe ratio of 0.24, not statistically different from zero**, while emerging currencies earned 7.3% a year ([Hsu, Taylor and Wang, SSRN copy](https://technicalanalyst-cdn-1.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/13144511/SSRN-id2765673.pdf)). The only snooping-corrected study whose whole sample is post-2010 covers daily EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY from 2010 to 2016 with 7,846 rules and a 3 bp cost: 5% to 15% of rules pass the test in sample, and portfolios of those rules earn "small annualized returns (<= 1% per annum)" out of sample ([Hassanniakalager, Sermpinis and Stasinakis](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/246954/2/246954.pdf)). A false-discovery-rate study of 7,650 rules over 1994 to 2014 found selected rules persist for one to three months and "no rules are consistently profitable over the full sample period" ([Zarrabi, Snaith and Coakley](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v49y2017icp113-127.html); abstract only). A study of 113,148 rules reports returns up to 30% a year before correction, "a sharp fall" in robust rules after it, and Bollinger and RSI rules as the ones still robust in its later sub-sample ([Coakley, Marzano and Nankervis](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v45y2016icp273-282.html); abstract only, net figures not retrieved).

### Intraday rule tests: the break-even cost is at or below the cost actually paid

Neely and Weller evolved rules on half-hourly data for four dollar pairs in 1996 with a strict train, select and test split. At zero cost the rules returned 67% to 132% a year from about one trade an hour, yet the **break-even one-way cost was only 0.40 to 1.01 bp**; rules trained with costs in the objective traded far less and broke even at 2.1 to 3.3 bp, and the authors found "no evidence of excess returns" once realistic costs and hours were imposed ([Neely and Weller, St. Louis Fed WP](https://s3.amazonaws.com/real.stlouisfed.org/wp/1999/1999-016.pdf)). Dempster and co-authors fed popular indicators to four learning methods on 1994 to 1998 GBP/USD intraday bars: all made significant profits at zero cost and "none of the methods produce significant profits at realistic transaction costs" ([Dempster et al.](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/wp0030.pdf)). Analysts' filter rules "would not be profitable" on intraday quotes ([Curcio et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ijf:ijfiec:v:2:y:1997:i:4:p:267-80); abstract only). Twelve chart patterns on intraday EUR/USD showed predictability in more than half of cases and profitability in two, "too small to cover the transaction costs" ([Ben Omrane and Van Oppens](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cor/louvco/2004035.html)). Moving-average and momentum models on 30-minute dollar-euro data did worse than the same models on daily data ([Schulmeister, WIFO](https://www.wifo.ac.at/?p=113125)). An hourly rule added nothing beyond a seasonal volatility model in a bootstrap test ([Sokolovski, UCT thesis](https://open.uct.ac.za/bitstreams/1ca4b008-7ebc-4b87-8804-c8674358c228/download)).

The positive intraday claims deserve a fair hearing and do not change the picture. A genetic-programming study on one-minute data for six majors reports excess returns "even when appropriate transaction costs are taken into account" ([Manahov, Hudson and Gebka](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfin:v:28:y:2014:i:c:p:131-157)), but its cost level, dates and out-of-sample design could not be checked, so it stands as an unverified abstract. An hourly cointegration pairs thesis earned 16.48% cumulative net over about 6.6 out-of-sample years, roughly 2.3% a year by researcher arithmetic ([Hoang, Tilburg thesis](http://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=158611)). These justify testing those families. They do not justify trusting them.

### Real effects that are too small: session drift, fixing reversals and round numbers

Time-of-day effects are the best-documented intraday regularities in forex. Breedon and Ranaldo, using interdealer firm quotes from 1997 to 2007, found currencies depreciate in their own trading hours: EUR/USD returned an annualised minus 8.4% in the European session and plus 10.0% in the US session at mid-quotes. Trading at firm bid and ask, EUR/USD still earned 6% and 7% a year, and **every other pair was negative**, so "most of these simple time-of-day trading strategies are not profitable when trading costs are included" ([Breedon and Ranaldo, SNB WP](https://www.snb.ch/public/asset/en/www-snb-ch/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04/publications0_en/working_paper_2011_04.n.pdf)). Krohn, Mueller and Whelan extended this to nine currencies over 1999 to 2019: the dollar appreciates about 2.5 bp before each of the Tokyo, Frankfurt and London fixes and reverses by about the same amount afterwards. Their cost table is the clearest statement of the retail problem. For the euro around the European fixes, the combined trade earned 15.75% a year at zero spread, 8.64% (Sharpe 1.03) at half the quoted spread and **1.52% (Sharpe 0.05) at the full quoted spread**; for sterling the full-spread result was negative, and the yen lost at every cost level. The authors conclude "it is not obvious that this can be exploited by the average trader", and the effect has decayed: Tokyo-fix reversal returns have been "around 5% or below since 2013", and European-fix returns "dropped to below 5%" in 2018 and 2019 ([Krohn, Mueller and Whelan, Bank of Canada WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). The same paper finds no discernible daily pattern in CME FX futures order flow, a caution against assuming the effect transfers cleanly to the futures path. No study measures these effects after 2019.

The month-end London fix shows reversals too: a strategy that fades the pre-fix move and holds for up to 15 minutes produced Sharpe ratios above 1 at one horizon or another in 15 of 21 pairs over 2004 to 2013, with half the interbank spread assumed at exit. That is twelve trades a year per pair, executed at a fix price retail clients generally cannot obtain, on a sample that ends before the 2015 change in fix methodology; the same paper notes that a retail aggregator's spread was "roughly twice as large as the inside spreads" on the interbank platforms ([Evans](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/81583/1/MPRA_paper_81583.pdf)).

Round numbers are the one microstructure fact that supports a pure price rule mechanically. Published support and resistance levels predicted intraday trend interruptions better than arbitrary levels, with bounce frequencies of roughly 57% to 66% against 55% to 57% for artificial levels, and 96% of published levels ended in 0 or 5 ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). In a bank's order book, take-profit orders cluster at round numbers and stop-loss orders just beyond them, so trends tend to pause at a round number and accelerate once it breaks ([Osler 2003](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)). Those are 1996 to 2000 samples and predictability tests, not profit tests; the measured effect is 2 to 9 percentage points of bounce frequency, which makes it a filter on entries and stop placement, not a standalone signal.

### Popular retail methods with no formal evidence

For ICT and smart-money concepts (order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity sweeps) no peer-reviewed or working-paper test was found, and a broker explainer says as much itself ([XS.com](https://www.xs.com/en/test-blog/smc-smart-money-concepts-explained/)). Practitioner and vendor backtests disagree and cannot be verified: one mechanical test across 738 assets reports negative average alpha for both order blocks and fair value gaps ([IndicatorEdge](https://indicatoredge.io/smart-money)), while a broker article reports a 58.4% win rate with no dates, net profit or code ([FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/smart-money-concepts-work-backtest-evidence)). Harmonic patterns, supply and demand zones and Elliott wave have no formal evidence. Fibonacci retracements "cannot meaningfully predict" short-term rates in one five-pair thesis ([Macalester thesis](https://digitalcommons.macalester.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1041&context=economics_honors_projects)). Ichimoku with default settings "failed to create value consistently" ([Deng et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:ijfiec:v:26:y:2021:i:4:p:5321-5336); abstract only). Single-candlestick patterns on EUR/USD gave "no net positive average returns ... after taking into account transaction costs" ([Orquín-Serrano](https://mdpi.com/2227-7390/8/5/802)).

Two popular ideas are borrowed from other markets. Opening-range breakout evidence is for US equities ([Zarattini and Aziz](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)); no peer-reviewed or working-paper test of a London-open or Asian-range breakout on spot forex was found, and the one disciplined practitioner test, pre-registered on crypto over two windows, found a gross edge of about 0.04% per trade and 41 of 42 net cells negative ([SimpleMarkets](https://simplemarkets.io/strategies/research-log/opening-range-breakout-costs)). Intraday momentum of the "first half hour predicts last half hour" type has forex support only for the rouble on the Moscow exchange, a market with a defined open and close ([Elaut, Frömmel and Lampaert](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:37:y:2018:i:c:p:35-51)). No rigorous study of intraday technical rules on major pairs with a sample ending after 2021 was located, so anything tested today on 2022 to 2026 data is out of sample relative to the literature, with no published benchmark to check a positive result against.

### A 65-pip day and a 1.4-pip spread cap the feasible frequency

EUR/USD's mean daily range in 2026 through 20 November was about 65 pips (56 bp), with a median of 58 pips and realised volatility near 5.7%; the second half of the year averaged 57 pips, about the same as 2019, the quietest year in the series. For comparison the mean was 82 pips in 2025 and 104 in 2022. These are researcher arithmetic on a vendor's daily bars, cross-checked against a second vendor at 63 pips for 2026 ([Alpha Vantage](https://www.alphavantage.co/query?function=FX_DAILY&from_symbol=EUR&to_symbol=USD&outputsize=full&apikey=demo); [Twelve Data](https://api.twelvedata.com/time_series?symbol=EUR/USD&interval=1day&outputsize=1000&apikey=demo)). Scaling the daily standard deviation by the square root of time gives a typical one-minute move of about 0.9 bp and a typical one-hour move of about 7 bp. If a rule is right about direction with probability p and wins or loses the typical move, the accuracy needed to break even follows directly from the cost. The table is researcher arithmetic under a uniform-volatility approximation that was not checked against one-minute data.

| Holding period | Typical move, 2026 volatility | Break-even accuracy at 0.5 bp round trip | At 1 bp | At 2 bp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 minute | 0.9 bp | 83% | Impossible (above 100%) | Impossible |
| 5 minutes | 2.1 bp | 65% | 80% | Impossible |
| 15 minutes | 3.6 bp | 59% | 67% | 84% |
| 1 hour | 7.3 bp | 54% | 59% | 67% |
| 4 hours | 14.6 bp | 52% | 54% | 59% |
| 8 hours (one session) | 20.6 bp | 51.5% | 53% | 56% |

Nothing in the literature supports sustained directional accuracy of 65% to 80% on five- to fifteen-minute holds. **One-minute and five-minute scalping is ruled out at retail costs by arithmetic**, fifteen-minute holds are marginal even at half a basis point, and holds of one hour to one session are the shortest horizon at which the required accuracy, 53% to 59%, is within sight of any documented effect. The same logic applies to stops and targets: cost adds c / (S + T) to the break-even win rate for a stop of S pips, a target of T and a cost of c, so a 10/10-pip trade at a 1.2-pip cost needs 56% wins instead of 50%, while a 20/40 trade needs 35.3% instead of 33.3% but asks for two thirds of a typical day's range in one direction. In a 57-pip regime the feasible design space is roughly 10- to 25-pip stops with 15- to 40-pip targets, one or two trades a session. As a budget, two round trips a day at 1 bp cost about 5% of notional a year, which is about the entire gross session effect last measured in 2018 and 2019; ten a day cost about 25%, which no documented effect can fund.

### Telling a small edge from zero takes hundreds to thousands of trades

The table below is a standard power calculation, not a literature result (researcher arithmetic; trades are assumed independent, and serial correlation raises the counts). The middle column is a single pre-specified rule at one-sided 5% significance with 80% power; the last raises the hurdle to a t-statistic of 3, the common allowance when many variants were tried.

| Win rate | Payoff ratio | Net expectancy | Trades for 5% one-sided, 80% power | Trades for t = 3, 80% power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 52% | 1.0 | +0.04R | 3,860 | 9,211 |
| 50% | 1.1 | +0.05R | 2,728 | 6,510 |
| 35% | 2.0 | +0.05R | 5,066 | 12,089 |
| 55% | 1.0 | +0.10R | 612 | 1,461 |
| 45% | 1.5 | +0.125R | 612 | 1,461 |
| 40% | 2.0 | +0.20R | 334 | 797 |
| 60% | 1.0 | +0.20R | 148 | 354 |

A 52% win rate at 1:1 after costs, which is the realistic upper range for a mechanical intraday rule on this evidence, needs about 3,900 trades: 8 to 15 years at one or two trades a day on one pair. A 55% rate needs about 600. Low-win-rate, high-payoff designs need more trades for the same expectancy. An apparent 60% on 150 trades is exactly what the best of a few dozen variants of a zero-edge rule produces. This is consistent with Hsu, Taylor and Wang's own arithmetic that a rule with a true Sharpe ratio of 0.6 needs about 7.5 years of data, and with the population outcome: about two in three US retail forex accounts lose in any given quarter, with OANDA reporting 34.33% of accounts profitable in the third quarter of 2026 ([OANDA disclosures](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/legal/regulatory-public-disclosures)), and national studies cited by ESMA found 74% to 89% of retail CFD accounts lose ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)).

**Recommended stack, path B (CME micro FX futures).** Keep the same research tools, then test on futures bars from IBKR or Databento, and trade through NautilusTrader's Interactive Brokers adapter or raw ib_async, with pysystemtrade as the reference for production structure. This path has a fully open, maintained research-to-live chain, exchange clearing, no financing mark-up and the possibility of sub-1 bp costs with resting limit orders. Its costs are a gateway session that must be kept logged in, a wrapper with no commits in about eleven months, a contract size that is coarse for a small account, and quarterly rolls. The MetaTrader and TradingView routes are alternatives to both. MT5's real-tick tester is accurate for that broker's own feed and the EA runs inside the dealer's platform, but the work is tied to MQL5, a Windows terminal and a dealer that offers MT5 in the US. A TradingView bridge is the least engineering and the least control: logic lives in Pine, fills depend on a paid intermediary, and bar-close strategies on 15-minute bars or longer are the only sensible use. ## A staged plan that expects a null result

Everything in this section is a synthesis and a judgement, not a finding from any single source, and it is not investment advice. The evidence says the most likely outcome of honest testing is that every candidate fails, and the plan is built so that this outcome is cheap and unambiguous.

### Three rule ideas and one calibration test

The first idea is the **EUR/USD session drift**, traded as the rule itself with one or two trades a day. The windows come straight from the literature, so there are no parameters to fit: be short EUR/USD from about 2:00 to 8:15 am New York time (the dollar strengthens into the European fixes) and long from the London fix at 11:00 am toward the New York close, exiting before 5 pm. It is the only pair and session combination where the effect exceeded interbank costs in the original sample, and the only intraday effect replicated across twenty years and several data sets. The honest expectation is poor. At OANDA's 1.4-pip spread two round trips a day cost about 6% of notional a year (researcher arithmetic), the gross effect was last measured below 5% a year, the euro trade earned only 1.52% at the full quoted spread even over 1999 to 2019, and nothing is known about 2020 to 2026. The test is worth running because it answers two questions at once: whether the effect still exists, and whether limit-order entries or the futures path can reach the half-spread cost at which it was once worth having.

The second idea is **hourly mean reversion**: fading stretched moves on one-hour bars with Bollinger- or RSI-type rules, a hard stop, a time stop and no position across 5 pm. The support is indirect. Bollinger and RSI rules were the ones reported as still robust in the largest daily rule study, mean-reversion rules improve and trend rules deteriorate as sampling frequency rises in high-frequency commodity data ([Hudson, McGroarty and Urquhart](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/404223/)), and the fix literature documents reversals. Test it on EUR/USD first and then on a range-prone cross such as EUR/GBP or AUD/NZD, where spreads are wider in basis-point terms and where no intraday study exists. Policy-anchored crosses carry a tail that stops cannot control: when the Swiss National Bank removed the EUR/CHF floor on 15 January 2015, one US dealer's clients were left with about $225 million of negative balances ([Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/fxcm-faces-losses-as-swiss-shock-leaves-alpari-uk-insolvent/41219974)).

The third idea is **round-number conditioning**, used as a filter on the first two and not as a signal: prefer fades as price approaches a level ending in 00 or 50, place stops beyond the level instead of at it, and treat a clean break as the end of the fade. The test must compare real round numbers against arbitrary levels, as the original study did.

The fourth item is a **calibration test, not a candidate**: one pre-registered version of the London-open breakout of the Asian range, the most common session idea in the repositories. One open-source backtest on forex found weak results and the best practitioner test in another market found the gross edge smaller than costs. Running it once verifies that the pipeline reproduces a known-weak result; a strongly positive outcome should be treated first as a sign of a bug or a leak. Hourly cointegration pairs are a possible fifth idea for later, on the strength of one thesis.

Leave everything else alone: anything under a 15-minute hold, ICT and smart-money patterns, Fibonacci, harmonics, Elliott wave, candlestick patterns, Ichimoku, and any grid, martingale or averaging-down recovery, which the US first-in-first-out rule also makes impractical in a single account.

### The test protocol, fixed before any backtest is run

**Data.** Use bid and ask series, never mid, at the bar frequency traded, including the rollover hour and news minutes. Research on Dukascopy ticks after the quality checks described above, then repeat on the venue's own data. The literature shows why: the six-pair session effect flips from significant everywhere at mid-quotes to profitable for EUR/USD alone at firm bid and ask.

**Costs.** The base case is the measured spread by hour of day from OANDA bid and ask candles (not the 1.4-pip headline), plus commission where it applies, plus a slippage allowance on stop orders. Stress at 1.5 and 2 times the base. Report the break-even cost of every rule and reject any rule whose break-even is under twice the base cost. A rule with a gross edge of 0.05R and a cost of 0.03R is one spread-widening away from zero whatever its t-statistic.

**Snooping correction.** Keep a written log of every rule family, parameter value, pair, session filter and exit variant ever evaluated; that count is the multiple-testing denominator. Apply White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA test to the best rule, and the Romano-Wolf step procedure if a set is kept, all available in `arch`. If none is implemented, require a t-statistic of at least 3 on net per-trade returns as a blunt substitute. The session-drift rule has essentially one variant, which is its main statistical virtue; cap the mean-reversion grid at a small pre-declared number of variants.

**Splits, walk-forward and hold-out.** Use a training period, a selection period, and a final hold-out of the most recent 18 to 24 months that is opened once. Because the published record stops around 2019 to 2021, a 2025 to 2026 hold-out is unseen by the literature and covers both a higher-volatility year and the current quiet regime. For any rule with parameters, re-select on a rolling two- to three-year window and trade the next one to three months, since that is the documented persistence of selected rules.

**Minimum trade counts.** Fix the required count from the power table before running anything. Plan around a net edge of 0.04R to 0.10R: at least about 1,500 trades in the selection sample to clear a t-statistic of 3 for a 0.10R edge, and accept that a two-year hold-out at two trades a day (roughly 750 to 1,000 trades) can confirm a 0.10R edge but cannot confirm a 0.04R edge. If the design cannot generate the trades, the question cannot be answered and the idea should be dropped, not loosened.

**Robustness and null comparison.** Report results by year and by volatility regime, with and without the best 1% of trades, at neighbouring parameter values, on a second pair not used in selection, and with the minutes around scheduled news and the rollover hour excluded. Compare against randomised entry times with the same holding period and exits, to check that the result is not just the session drift or the stop geometry.

**Stop conditions for research.** An idea is abandoned, with no re-tuning, if net expectancy at base cost is not positive in the selection sample; if break-even cost is under twice base cost; if it fails the snooping test; if it is net negative in either of two independent windows; if it does not beat the random-entry null; if it fails the hold-out; or if the pre-declared variant budget is used up. A failed hold-out is final for that idea, because a hold-out consulted twice is no longer a hold-out.

### Research, paper, small live, then scale

| Stage | Purpose | What happens | Gate to move on (suggested) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | Decide whether any candidate deserves capital | Run the protocol above; write down the hypothesis, costs, trade count and stop conditions first | Survives costs at 2 times base, the snooping test, the null comparison and the single hold-out |
| 2. Paper trading | Prove the plumbing and measure real spreads | Run on the broker's practice or paper environment for several weeks; deliberately exercise restarts, disconnects, the news blackout, the rollover blackout and the kill switch | Signals match a backtest of the same period bar for bar; simulated fills sit within the modelled cost; reconciliation and alerts work when tested on purpose |
| 3. Small live | Measure real fills, slippage and records | Minimum size (a few thousand units at OANDA, or one micro contract), no additional leverage, server-side stop on every position; export transactions daily | Realised cost per trade within a pre-set tolerance of the model; no unexplained order or position discrepancies |
| 4. Scale | Increase size slowly | Step up in increments with the account balance capped | Pre-set stops: a drawdown deeper than the backtest's worst, realised costs above the stress case, or sustained divergence from the backtest returns the bot to stage 2 |

Stages 2 and 3 cannot confirm an edge. At one or two trades a day, three to six months of paper or live trading yields roughly 60 to 250 trades, against the 600 to 3,900 needed. What these stages can do is confirm that live signals equal backtest signals and that realised costs equal modelled costs, and cost converges much faster than edge. The mr-scrooge-v6 record is the cautionary example of what a live stage shows when costs and payoff asymmetry were underestimated.

### Safety controls for an unattended forex bot

No regulator or standards body publishes a retail checklist, so these controls are assembled from the broker documentation, the repository survey and the market-structure evidence. Risk limits belong outside the strategy code: a maximum position per pair and in total, a maximum order size, a cap on orders per hour, a daily loss limit that flattens and halts, and leverage far below the 50:1 cap. Research on the 2010 US leverage limit found it cut high-leverage traders' losses by 40% ([Heimer and Simsek](https://nber.org/papers/w24176)). Every position needs a server-side stop held at the broker, with a periodic check that it still exists, and position logic must be netting and first-in-first-out by construction.

Time-based guards matter more in forex than in most markets. The bot should not enter, and should not leave unprotected stops resting, across the 5 pm New York roll, which is "the time when bid-ask spreads in the FX market are the highest and trading volume is the lowest" ([Krohn, Mueller and Whelan, conference draft](https://benny.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/2dedYfDH)); being flat by then also avoids financing. It should stand aside around scheduled news from the cached calendar, refuse to trade when the live spread exceeds a per-pair cap, and be flat before the weekend. Daylight-saving transitions deserve a dedicated test, because session logic keyed to the wrong clock fails silently.

Operationally, the bot needs startup and periodic reconciliation of local state against the broker (at OANDA, the snapshot and transaction-ID pattern); idempotent client order identifiers so a restart cannot duplicate an order; a stale-data guard that halts when quotes stop or timestamps lag; a heartbeat to an independent monitor that alerts when the bot goes silent; alerts routed to a phone; and a tested kill switch that cancels orders, flattens positions and stops the process. Credentials should sit in a dedicated sub-account funded with risk capital only, on a machine that runs nothing else, with dependencies pinned. Knight Capital's $460 million loss in 45 minutes, with 97 warning emails unread, is the standing reminder of what stale code and unrouted alerts cost ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)). Stops do not cap losses in a gap, as January 2015 showed, and US dealers may not describe customer funds as segregated or bankruptcy-protected ([NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)), so the balance left at the dealer is the final control.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the question a builder should ask. Reference code, indicator libraries, bid and ask history and snooping tests are all free and adequate; what does not exist, in any repository or any paper found, is an intraday technical rule on a major pair that has been shown to clear a retail spread out of sample. The two most rigorous open-source projects reached that conclusion about their own work, and one of them paid $1,637 to learn it live. The valuable asset in this project is therefore the test harness, not the strategy: a pipeline that uses tradable prices, counts every variant, holds back the last two years and reports break-even cost will return an honest answer quickly, and on this evidence the answer will usually be no.

Two things could change that. The first is cost, not cleverness: the documented session effects were worth having at half the quoted spread and worthless at the full spread, so the only lever with evidence behind it is execution, which means resting orders on the futures path or a pricing tier that a small account does not yet qualify for. The second is that the literature goes dark after about 2021, so a careful test on 2022 to 2026 data produces information nobody has published. A result near zero there is the expected outcome and a useful one; a clearly positive result should be distrusted until it survives a second feed, a doubled cost and a few hundred live trades at minimum size. Nothing in this report is legal, tax or investment advice, and the eligibility, tax-election and account-protection questions flagged above belong with the broker and a qualified professional before real money is committed.
