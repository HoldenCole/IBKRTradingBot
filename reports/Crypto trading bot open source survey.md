# Open-source bots automate trading, not profits

The open-source tooling for a fully automated crypto or forex bot is mature, free and well maintained in November 2026, but nothing in the code or in the published research supplies an edge, and the best population data say a retail short-horizon trader should plan on losing. Five frameworks carry almost all the serious work: freqtrade, Hummingbot, NautilusTrader, QuantConnect LEAN and the CCXT connectivity library, with Jesse, Lumibot and OctoBot as smaller live options. The six repositories in the viral "cheating" post are ordinary plumbing (a Solana signing library, a hobby-grade backtester, a stale Jupiter client, a Meteora liquidity SDK, a dataframe engine and a charting library) and confer no advantage. Regulator-grade data put the share of losing retail short-term traders at roughly 70 to 97% depending on horizon, about two in three US retail forex accounts lose in any given quarter, and measured intraday crypto edges are worth roughly 1 to 9 basis points per round trip against entry-tier US spot taker fees of 160 to 180 basis points per round trip. The only strategy family with repeated support for a positive net return at retail scale is slow, volatility-sized trend following on the most liquid coins, and even that evidence comes mostly from working papers, is concentrated in bull regimes, and had its strongest peer-reviewed cousin challenged by a re-audit this month. The commonest way retail bot users lose everything is not strategy failure but stolen keys, usually through a trojanised "trading bot" repository. A sensible build therefore treats the project as an engineering and risk-control exercise first: research with honest cost assumptions, weeks of paper trading, a very small live stage on a US-licensed venue with a trade-only key, and scaling only if live results track the backtest. **Nothing in this report is legal, tax or investment advice**; items that need a professional are flagged.

*Conventions.* GitHub star counts are the rounded figures displayed on 23 November 2026 and were not cross-checked against the GitHub API; two rounding sources were used, so the same project can appear as, for example, 31.8k in one table and 32k in another. "Unverified" marks a figure the underlying research could not confirm against a primary source. "Researcher arithmetic" marks a calculation built on cited inputs, not a number published by the source. A "fully hands-off" bot is also a slight fiction: exchange APIs, fee schedules and libraries changed repeatedly during 2026, so even a correct bot needs periodic maintenance, monitoring and tax record-keeping.

### Licences do not restrict a private bot; venue law does

No licence in the survey restricts running a modified bot for your own account. GPL-3.0 (freqtrade, OctoBot, Lumibot) and AGPL-3.0 (backtesting.py) bite only if the bot is distributed or offered as a network service; Apache, MIT and LGPL bases leave proprietary distribution open ([freqtrade LICENSE](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/blob/develop/LICENSE); [nautilus_trader repo](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader)). The practical restriction is commercial: Jesse's live trading runs through a pre-built plugin whose "access is limited to those with an active license" ([Jesse live trading docs](https://docs.jesse.trade/docs/livetrade)). Whether a strategy file is a derivative work of a GPL framework is a legal question the research did not address.

The binding constraint for a US person is the venue list. freqtrade's own documentation lists the United States among countries blocked by Binance and tells US users to use `binanceus` ([freqtrade exchange notes](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/exchanges/)). Hyperliquid's terms classify US residents as "Restricted Persons" enforced by geofencing, according to secondary sources; the primary terms were not retrieved ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/is-hyperliquid-available-in-the-usa)). That the global platforms of Bybit, OKX, Bitget, Gate, KuCoin and MEXC exclude US persons is widely understood but was not verified per exchange in this research. The upshot is that **a US freqtrade user is realistically limited to long-only spot on Kraken or Binance.US**, and popular community strategies tuned on Binance futures cannot be replicated lawfully.

US-regulated leverage now exists but framework support lags. Coinbase launched CFTC-regulated perpetual-style nano bitcoin and ether futures on 21 July 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/363796/coinbase-launches-perpetual-futures)), Kraken launched CFTC-regulated perpetuals for US clients on 15 June 2026, listed on Bitnomial ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-06-15-kraken-launches-crypto-perps-trading-in-us-on-kraken-pro-404751)), and CME crypto futures moved to 24/7 trading in mid-2026 ([CME press release](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2026/6/01/cme_group_announceslaunchof247cryptocurrencyfuturesandoptionstra.html)). The research found no confirmation that any open-source framework or CCXT connects to the Coinbase or Kraken US perpetuals, and the `krakenfutures` connectors in freqtrade and Jesse point at Kraken's non-US platform. The proven route to lawful short or leveraged crypto exposure inside an open-source framework is therefore CME futures through Interactive Brokers using NautilusTrader, LEAN or Lumibot. State-level availability, New York in particular, was not researched.

### Community strategies have no verified live record

NostalgiaForInfinity, the best-known freqtrade strategy (GPL-3.0, 3.5k stars, 28,903 commits), publishes only backtests in commit comments and makes no performance claims ([NFI repo](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity)). No independent, audited live track record was found for it or for any other popular community strategy. One user report from August 2026 shows the mechanism of disappointment: a backtested entry at 1.4889 for a 4.19% gain filled live at 1.5386 for 0.54% ([NFI issue #1234](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity/issues/1234)). That is a single anecdote, but it matches freqtrade's own warning that "many strategies, without the programmer knowing, have fallen prey to lookahead bias" ([freqtrade lookahead-analysis docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/)). A strategy edited several times a day is being continuously refitted to its recent losses, so backtests of the current version are in-sample by construction.

The list also mixes languages without comment: two libraries are TypeScript and four are Python, which reads more like a collage of repository names than a description of a running system. The Jupiter client is likely pointing at legacy endpoints, because Jupiter unified its APIs into Swap API V2 in March 2026 and describes the earlier Ultra API as superseded ([Jupiter developer docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/llms.txt); [Jupiter changelog 2026-03](https://developers.jup.ag/changelog/2026-03)); the client's source was not inspected to confirm this. "Cheating" is a misnomer: thousands of competitors, including professional sandwich bots, use the same or better tools.

### Connectivity libraries: one generalist and many thin wrappers

CCXT is the only actively maintained library that covers every relevant US spot venue with websocket support bundled in the free package, though none of those US venues is among its twelve "certified" exchanges and Robinhood is absent ([CCXT README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/ccxt@latest/README.md)). Official single-venue clients exist for Coinbase ([coinbase-advanced-py](https://github.com/coinbase/coinbase-advanced-py), Apache-2.0) and Alpaca ([alpaca-py](https://github.com/alpacahq/alpaca-py), Apache-2.0). For Interactive Brokers, [ib_insync](https://github.com/erdewit/ib_insync) is unmaintained (last release July 2023) and its successor [ib_async](https://github.com/ib-api-reloaded/ib_async) has had no release since December 2025, so it should be treated as slow-moving. For forex, OANDA's v20 REST API allows 120 requests per second ([OANDA development guide](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/development-guide)), but the Python wrappers around it are stale: [oandapyV20](https://github.com/hootnot/oanda-api-v20) was last released in 2021 and the official `v20` package in 2018 ([PyPI v20](https://pypi.org/project/v20/)).

On Solana the Python stack is solders plus [solana-py](https://github.com/michaelhly/solana-py) (MIT, v0.42.0 in November 2026), while the exchange SDKs for Jupiter, Meteora, Raydium, Orca and Pump.fun are TypeScript-first. Several widely recommended packages are stale: [anchorpy](https://github.com/kevinheavey/anchorpy) has had no commit since March 2025, Jito's Python client [jito-py-rpc](https://github.com/jito-labs/jito-py-rpc) no release since June 2025, and the Flashbots Python library [web3-flashbots](https://github.com/flashbots/web3-flashbots) no commit since July 2024. Staleness matters more on-chain than on centralised exchanges because the APIs move faster.

For data, free sources go a long way: exchange candles through CCXT, [Binance public data dumps](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) as a deep-liquidity reference series, and Tardis.dev's free first-of-month tick files ([Tardis billing FAQ](https://docs.tardis.dev/faq/billing-and-subscriptions)). Full tick and order-book history is a paid product, and [cryptofeed](https://github.com/bmoscon/cryptofeed) can record your own going forward. DuckDB and polars are sensible for storing and querying archives on a laptop. For forex there is no consolidated tape, so free histories from Dukascopy or HistData are one venue's quotes and do not reproduce your dealer's spreads ([Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/historical/)).

### On-chain Solana trading is adversarial and legally unsettled for US users

Network fees are small; the real costs are other participants. Bitquery counted **6.85 million sandwich attacks by 2,545 bot wallets against 2.15 million wallets** between 30 October 2025 and 7 October 2026, with 96% on Pump.fun venues, and trades set with 20 to 50% slippage tolerance were sandwiched 14 times as often as those under 3% ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/)). A July 2026 preprint found that only 0.198% of 832,941 Pump.fun launches "graduated" ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823)), and Solidus Labs classified about 99% of Pump.fun launches as pump-and-dumps or rug pulls under a flag-based definition ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)). Free infrastructure is thin: Jito's public endpoint allows one request per second ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)), Jupiter's keyless tier half a request per second ([Jupiter developer docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/llms.txt)), and Helius's streaming data starts at $499 a month on a pricing page whose own "last updated" stamp is January 2025 ([Helius pricing](https://www.helius.dev/pricing)). Jupiter's help material says its mobile product is not accessible in the United States, and a 2026 review states Jupiter "is not available to U.S. residents under its published terms"; the current full web-app terms were not retrieved, so this is unsettled rather than established ([Jupiter help centre](https://jupiverse.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/22632755720732-Why-is-Jupiter-Mobile-not-available-in-my-country)). dYdX opened to US traders in December 2025 for spot only, with perpetuals still unavailable ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/382180/dydx-spot-trading-solana-us-traders)).

## Backtests flatter by default; only order-book engines model fills

Every bar-based research library fills orders at the requested price inside the candle's range with zero slippage unless told otherwise, and resolves a stop and a target inside the same bar by convention, not by data. freqtrade says so directly: "All orders are filled at the requested price (no slippage) as long as the price is within the candle's high/low range", and backtesting "will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode" ([freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). The ranking below is an inference from documentation and source code; no independent head-to-head comparison against live fills was found.

Two details deserve attention. NautilusTrader's default path through a bar visits the high before the low, which is the optimistic ordering for a long position holding both a stop and a target; its adaptive alternative is described as "a deterministic heuristic, not a reconstruction of the actual trade sequence" ([Nautilus bar-execution docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/develop/docs/concepts/backtesting/bar-execution.md)). And strategies using passive orders or scalping are the most overstated everywhere, because queue priority and adverse selection are absent from candle data.

### The standard errors each have a tool, none switched on by default

Look-ahead bias is the commonest driver of too-good results. freqtrade computes indicators on the whole history at once, ships a `lookahead-analysis` command to catch strategies that peek, and a `recursive-analysis` command for indicators that take different values live than in backtest ([lookahead-analysis docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/lookahead-analysis.md); [recursive-analysis docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/recursive-analysis.md)). FreqAI documents its own leakage surface: features are computed once over the entire training range ([FreqAI running docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/freqai-running.md)). Survivorship has no automatic detector for crypto pair universes; freqtrade warns that dynamic pairlists apply today's list to the past.

Data-snooping is the deepest problem. Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new factor needs a t-ratio above 3.0 and that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). A Quantopian study of 888 algorithms found that the more backtests a user ran, the bigger the gap between in-sample and out-of-sample performance; secondary summaries report backtest Sharpe explaining only about 1 to 2% of out-of-sample variation, a figure not verified against the original paper ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). freqtrade's hyperopt documentation warns that over-precise parameters "will usually result in overfitted results" and its validation step reuses the optimisation window ([freqtrade hyperopt docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/hyperopt/)). Open implementations of the corrections exist: [skfolio](https://github.com/skfolio/skfolio) (BSD-3-Clause) for combinatorial purged cross-validation and walk-forward, [pypbo](https://github.com/esvhd/pypbo) (AGPL-3.0) for the probability of backtest overfitting and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ([Bailey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)), and the young [purgedcv](https://github.com/eslazarev/purged-cross-validation) (MIT). mlfinlab is proprietary despite its public repository ([mlfinlab LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hudson-and-thames/mlfinlab/master/LICENSE.txt)), and the original pandas-ta now carries an unclear licence with its GitHub repository not found, so [pandas-ta-classic](https://github.com/xgboosted/pandas-ta-classic) (MIT) or TA-Lib are the safer indicator dependencies ([PyPI pandas-ta](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-ta/)).

## AI trading repositories have stars but no replicated profit

No independent replication was found showing that any popular machine-learning, reinforcement-learning or LLM trading repository produces profit after costs out of sample. Every one carries an educational or research disclaimer.

The reinforcement-learning paper most often cited as showing these agents work in crypto shows the opposite when read closely. Gort and co-authors tested agents on ten coins over 58 days in 2022 with a 0.3% cost and slippage ignored: **every agent lost money**, from 35% for the best to 59% for the worst, against a 51% fall in the index ([arXiv 2209.05559](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05559)). Its durable contribution is the method of rejecting overfit agents, not the returns. On simulated prices where the optimal policy is known, Lu found the best algorithms needed more than 2 million steps to approach it, which the author equates to roughly 8,000 years of daily data ([arXiv 2307.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694)). For LLM strategies, the FINSABER study re-ran published agents over 2004 to 2024 on S&P 500 constituents including delisted names and found buy-and-hold significantly outperformed them, with no statistically significant alpha ([FINSABER paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v4)); that test covers equities only. In professionally run crypto forecasting contests, gradient-boosted trees and small neural networks with heavy feature engineering won, and the winning single model in the 2025 DRW contest scored a correlation of about 0.13, a figure taken from a search summary of the write-up ([Kaggle write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/drw-crypto-market-prediction/writeups/drw-solution-1st)). Neither contest scored after-cost profit.

### Live AI contests: the median model lost money

In Alpha Arena Season 1, six LLMs each traded $10,000 of real money in crypto perpetuals on Hyperliquid from 18 October to 3 November 2025. Final balances reported by ForkLog sum to $42,298 from $60,000, a field-wide loss of about 29.5% in 17 days by researcher arithmetic, with two of six models ahead ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)). The organiser does not publish a final ranking, third-party recaps disagree on the exact figures, and all published per-model fee, trade-count and Sharpe numbers conflict across sources and should be treated as unverified ([Moby guide](https://moby.win/learn/alpha-arena/)). In Season 1.5 on US equities, only 6 of 32 result sets finished positive and the combined book fell about one-third; under an identical prompt the winning model made 158 trades while another made 1,418 ([Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)). The organiser's founder said LLMs "can't really make money by themselves". An independent review of 11 arenas found only 2 with a profitable median model ([Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)), and three academic live benchmarks reach the same conclusion that general intelligence does not translate into trading ability ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209); [AI-Trader](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10971); [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)). No Season 2 results were public as of 23 November 2026. These windows are far too short to rank models, but the failure mode is consistent and behavioural: overtrading, poor sizing and leverage. Any LLM in the loop needs hard frequency, size and leverage limits enforced outside the model.

## What survives costs at retail scale: slow trend, and little else

### Most retail short-horizon traders lose, and automation does not change that

Loss rates rise with time in the market: in Brazil the profitable share fell from 29.8% after one day to 3.0% after 300 days. Short reporting windows flatter the picture, which is why one-quarter US forex disclosures show about one-third profitable (OANDA 34.33% in Q3 2026, FOREX.com 32.56% in Q2 2026, tastyfx 38.53% in Q3 2026) while a four-year French study found 89% losing. Interactive Brokers reports a higher 46.50% profitable for Q3 2026, a figure taken from a search extract of its page and probably reflecting a different client mix ([IBKR performance page](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/general/about/performanceCustomerForex.php)).

No credible dataset isolates "bot users". The nearest evidence is a peer-reviewed study of Hyperliquid's complete on-chain record: valuing each trade against the mid-price two hours later, net of fees, manual front-end traders lost $1.11 billion, algorithmic market makers gained $208 million and **algorithmic takers roughly broke even** ([Jia et al., AFT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36)). The sample window is reported inconsistently between sources and should be checked before quoting. Automation removes some behavioural errors; it does not make a retail taker a winner, and the makers who profit are professional firms.

### The cost level that kills intraday signals is a few basis points

Measured intraday edges in bitcoin are tiny. A live experiment with more than 400,000 maker orders on the Binance bitcoin perpetual found an order-book-imbalance taker signal worth about +1 basis point per round trip before fees and −1.96 after a best-tier 1.5 basis-point taker fee; maker versions lost 0.43 to 0.49 basis points per trade even with a rebate, and a naive strategy quoting at the touch lost almost 60% in about three days ([Albers et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)). The best published hour-of-day rule returned 33% a year gross from one round trip per day, which is about 8 to 9 basis points per trade by researcher arithmetic, with no costs deducted in the source ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)). Triangular arbitrage opportunities on Binance are mostly 0 to 2.5 basis points and vanish after standard fees ([Muck, Schmidl and Wolf](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/bitstreams/8b9ae900-017a-4bed-94b9-609c16e89945/download)). A plain grid has an expected return of essentially zero before fees ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)).

The rule of thumb that follows: sub-hour signals are worth 0 to 2 basis points and are untradeable for anyone paying taker fees; hourly-to-daily seasonal signals are worth under 10; daily trend signals were tested at 10 to 50 basis points ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)); and the weekly cross-sectional trend factor reported a break-even cost of 141 basis points per trade ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0022109024000747)). **Holding period, not signal cleverness, decides whether retail costs are bearable.** With entry-tier spot round trips at 160 to 180 basis points on Coinbase and Kraken, and a practical floor of roughly 4 basis points plus spread and funding on US perpetual-style futures, every measured sub-hour edge is already underwater for a US retail account.

### Crypto: one family with support, and it is contested

**Daily trend following** has the best support. Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon test an ensemble of Donchian breakouts on a survivorship-free universe from 2015 to March 2025, long-only on the top 20 liquid coins with a 25% volatility target; the author page reports a Sharpe ratio above 1.5 net of fees and a secondary summary reports 18% net annual growth at 10 basis points of cost ([Barbon paper page](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)). This is a working paper, one author works at a trading firm, and the detailed figures could not be verified against the paper itself. Peer-reviewed support for time-series momentum exists but uses pre-2019 data ([Liu and Tsyvinski](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/risks-and-returns-cryptocurrencies)). Man Group finds risk-adjusted trend returns peak at about 10 to 15 coins because liquidity falls sharply beyond the top 15 ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)). The caveats are heavy. An earlier study that fitted parameters on one year and tested on the next found no predictable, attractive Sharpe ratios for bitcoin over 2011 to 2019 ([arXiv 2009.12155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155)). Samples that include 2015 to 2021 flatter the result: a low-credibility 2026 preprint's own benchmark puts plain one-month momentum at a Sharpe of 0.65 with a 34.8% drawdown over 2022 to 2024 ([arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11708)). Trend following in bitcoin and ether is largely timed market exposure, and its profits concentrate in rising markets. No coin-specific evidence was found for SOL.

**Cross-sectional momentum** at a two-week horizon earned 2.6% a week gross in a 2013 to 2025 sample and 2.1% after 2020, before any costs ([Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)), but it needs a broad universe and a short leg that a US retail account cannot practically run. The related peer-reviewed "CTREND" factor reported 2.90% a week net of costs, and then a preprint dated 1 November 2026 audited its code and reported that after correcting look-ahead and accounting problems the gross weekly return falls to 0.05% ([arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01951)). That re-audit is new and unrefereed and the original authors' response is not known, but it is a live demonstration of how fragile even journal-published backtests are.

**Carry** was the best risk-adjusted trade of 2020 to 2024 and has been competed away. The long-spot, short-perpetual bitcoin trade had a gross Sharpe of 6.45 from August 2020 to May 2025 and was negative in 2025 ([Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)). Bitcoin funding fell from 11.0% annualised in 2024 to 2.2% for 2026 through 11 August, on an issuer's own data ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/)), and by April 2026 the CME basis was about 5% against a risk-free rate of about 4.5% ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/396722/cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand)). High carry also predicts crashes ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)).

Everything else lacks credible support for a non-professional. **Market making** suffers adverse selection in every queue state studied, and the only Hummingbot profitability material found is the project's own, which says it does not claim liquidity mining will be profitable ([Hummingbot whitepaper](https://docs.hummingbot.org/liquidity-mining.pdf)). **DEX liquidity provision** is dominated by sophisticated participants, with retail providers capturing about 10 to 25% of fees on Uniswap v3 ([BIS WP 1227](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.htm)). **Technical rules** tested with multiple-hypothesis corrections showed no out-of-sample predictability for bitcoin ([Hudson and Urquhart](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1)). **Mean reversion on majors** trades against the documented sign: daily reversal lives in small illiquid coins, while the largest coins show daily momentum ([IRFA 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)). **MEV and arbitrage** are concentrated in a handful of integrated players: three searchers took about three-quarters of CEX-DEX arbitrage value ([arXiv 2507.13023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13023)), and roughly 30% of Ethereum sandwich bots ran at a net loss ([Cointelegraph Research](https://cointelegraph.com/research/exclusive-data-from-eigenphi-reveals-that-sandwich-attacks-on-ethereum-have-waned)). **Memecoin sniping** is an insider's game: more than half of Pump.fun tokens are sniped in their creation block, and in over 15,000 launches the sniper wallet was funded directly by the token's deployer ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)).

### Forex: no family with clean net-of-cost evidence in the last decade

No strategy family has clean, peer-reviewed evidence of a positive return net of retail costs in major-currency spot forex over roughly 2016 to 2026. Simple filter and moving-average rules stopped working by the early 1990s ([Neely, Weller and Ulrich](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:44:y:2009:i:02:p:467-488_09)). A broad test of more than 21,000 rules did find predictability across 30 currencies over 45 years, varying by subperiod and country group; its net-of-cost figures were not retrieved ([Hsu, Taylor and Wang](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/inecon/v102y2016icp188-208.html)). Currency anomalies remain profitable before publication, but "trading profits decrease substantially after the publication of the underlying academic research" ([Bartram et al., JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/mispricing-and-risk-premia-in-currency-markets/CB12427D463F25A14B5A4F20A1BB3FB4)). Carry is compensation for crash risk, as the August 2024 yen unwind showed ([BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.htm)). Diversified trend following, measured by a multi-asset index that is not forex-only, gained 27.4% in 2022 and then suffered a 20.4% drawdown from May 2024 to May 2025, its second largest since 2000 ([Cambridge Associates](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=19140)). Intraday patterns around local trading hours and fixings are real in academic data ([Breedon and Ranaldo](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [Krohn, Mueller and Whelan](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:79:y:2024:i:1:p:541-578)), but nobody has shown them surviving retail spreads. Grid and martingale "expert advisors" have no peer-reviewed support at all, and the 2015 Swiss franc move left one US dealer's clients with roughly $225 million of negative equity ([Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/fxcm-faces-losses-as-swiss-shock-leaves-alpari-uk-insolvent/41219974)).

### Tail risks that backtests miss

The 10 October 2025 crash is the reference stress case. More than $19 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated in about a day; bitcoin top-of-book depth fell more than 90%, spreads reached double-digit percentages, some exchange interfaces froze, and auto-deleveraging closed profitable hedged shorts, "turning a hedged portfolio into a naked one" ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)). About 70% of liquidations happened within 40 minutes ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)). Funding had risen to nearly 30% annualised days before, so carry paid most just before it hurt most. Any stop-loss logic in a retail bot should be tested against those conditions, not against fills at the stop price.

## Stolen keys, not bad strategies, cause most total losses

### Malicious "trading bot" repositories are a documented, recurring attack

The dominant total-loss path for a small account is credential theft, and fake bot repositories are a standard delivery vehicle. SlowMist analysed a GitHub repository called `solana-pumpfun-bot` with inflated stars and forks whose dependency read the wallet key and sent it to an attacker; a victim lost funds the day after running it ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam)). Kaspersky's "GitVenom" campaign found more than 200 fake repositories, some with AI-written READMEs ([Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/about/press-releases/kaspersky-exposes-hidden-malware-on-github-stealing-personal-data-and-485000-in-bitcoin)). A Carnegie Mellon study found about 6 million suspected fake GitHub stars across 18,617 repositories, most promoting short-lived malware posing as pirated software, game cheats or crypto bots ([arXiv 2412.13459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459)). Package registries are no safer: stealers run at install time through npm and PyPI lifecycle hooks before any code is imported ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/2025-blockchain-and-cryptocurrency-threat-report)), legitimate dYdX client packages were compromised in February 2026 ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/compromised-dydx-npm-and-pypi-packages.html?m=1)), and a May 2026 campaign spread more than 34 packages targeting Solana wallet data across npm, PyPI and Crates.io ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/05/29/solana-sui-and-aptos-wallet-data-targeted-in-trapdoor-package-attack)). YouTube tutorials for "AI MEV bots" that have viewers deploy a contract which simply sends their funds to the scammer stole over $900,000 ([SentinelLABS](https://www.sentinelone.com/labs/smart-contract-scams-ethereum-drainers-pose-as-trading-bots-to-steal-crypto/)). The CFTC's own advisory is titled "AI Won't Turn Trading Bots into Money Machines" ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnandProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)). The viral post's framing fits this pattern closely enough that any repository reached through social media should be treated as hostile until proven otherwise: stars, forks and polished READMEs are not evidence of legitimacy.

Two further lessons shape the design. First, any bot that needs a raw private key in a file, which means every on-chain sniper or DEX bot, has a strictly worse threat model than an exchange bot with a scoped API key, because one bad dependency means total loss with no exchange-side control. Key-custodying Telegram bots have been breached roughly yearly, most recently Bloom in October 2026 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bloom-bot-hackers-953k-faster-popcorn)). Second, "withdrawals disabled" is necessary but not sufficient. In the 3Commas leak attackers used trade-only keys to pump illiquid tokens against their own orders ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/3commas-ceo-confirms-api-key-leak-following-warning-from-cz)), and in November 2026 more than $10 million was drained from the Lighter exchange through compromised API keys, prompting its post-mortem line: "A key that can trade can still lose you money if someone else holds it" ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/lighter-incident-exposes-risks-beyond-withdrawal-controls)).

### Custody: nothing on a crypto venue is insured like a bank deposit

FDIC insurance does not apply to crypto assets ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/fact-sheets/crypto-fact-sheet-7-28-22.html)) and SIPC does not protect digital assets that are not registered securities ([SIPC](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/investor-faqs)). Coinbase's crime insurance does not cover losses from unauthorised access to a customer's own account ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/insurance)). Venue failures are rare but large: Bybit lost about $1.4 billion in February 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/380992/biggest-crypto-hacks-2025)), and FTX customers were repaid in cash at petition-date values, not at later crypto prices ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/05/08/ftx-customers-recovery-repay-bankruptcy)). US forex dealers may not describe customer funds as segregated or bankruptcy-protected either ([NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)). The balance left on the venue is the final control.

### US regulation: your own account is simple, anything else is not

Under the definitions found, commodity trading advisor and pool operator status attaches to advising or pooling other people's money for compensation, so running a bot on your own account appears to need no federal registration; no primary source states this negative directly, and trading for friends, selling signals or pooling funds is where a lawyer is needed ([Bitcoin.com legal explainer](https://news.bitcoin.com/navigating-cftc-regulations-for-crypto-funds); a CFTC proposal of August 2026 to adjust these exemptions is not final, per the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/21/2026-17079/commodity-pool-operators-and-commodity-trading-advisors-reduction-of-duplicative-regulation-through)). Federal market-structure legislation stalled when the CLARITY Act failed a Senate cloture vote 49 to 50 on 15 September 2026, leaving spot trading governed by state licensing ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation)); New York requires a BitLicense or trust charter ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/virtual_currency_businesses)). Using a VPN to reach an offshore exchange breaches venue terms and misrepresents location. Enforcement has targeted venues, as in OKX's guilty plea with more than $504 million in penalties ([US DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/okx-pleads-guilty-violating-us-anti-money-laundering-laws-and-agrees-pay-penalties)); the research found no case of a retail individual prosecuted solely for VPN access and none establishing that it is lawful. The practical risks are frozen funds on a location re-check, no US recourse and no tax reporting. For forex, the CFTC warns that unregistered offshore dealers are a leading source of complaints, typically deposits that cannot be withdrawn ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8566-22)). US retail forex also carries specific design constraints: leverage capped at 50:1 on majors and a first-in-first-out, no-hedging rule that rules out most grid and hedge-recovery systems in a single account ([NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)).

### US tax: the product choice sets the paperwork and the rate

The following is general information; consult a CPA before acting on it. Spot crypto is property, so every fill is a reportable disposal and gains on positions held a year or less are taxed as ordinary income ([IRS digital assets page](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)). Brokers report on Form 1099-DA, with basis reporting for assets acquired and sold in the same custodial account from 1 January 2026; assets transferred in are "noncovered" ([IRS Form 1099-DA instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da)). Broker-held units default to first-in-first-out, and the relief allowing a different method in your own records runs only through 31 December 2026 ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0751-temporary-relief-for-identification-of-digital-assets-extended-through-2026)). The wash-sale rule does not yet apply to crypto, but a bill extending it cleared the House Ways and Means Committee 38 to 5 on 16 September 2026 and has not been enacted ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1972-ways-and-means-approves-digital-assets-tax-bill)); if it becomes law, a bot that re-enters the same asset within 30 days would see most realised losses deferred. Net capital losses offset only $3,000 of other income a year ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)), which creates an asymmetry: a bot that makes money one year and gives it back the next has paid tax on the first year and holds a slow-to-use carryforward.

Regulated futures are simpler. Section 1256 contracts are marked to market at year end and taxed 60% long-term and 40% short-term on one net figure reported on Form 6781 ([26 USC 1256](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1256)). This is settled for dated CME-style futures. For the new US perpetuals it is not: brokers market 60/40 treatment, but there is no IRS guidance, the character of funding payments is unclear, and CME is arguing in a pending challenge that the product is a swap ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/tax-management-memo/are-bitcoin-perpetuals-valid-futures-contracts-for-tax-reporting)). Spot forex defaults to ordinary gain or loss under Section 988, with an election out that must be documented on the day a transaction is entered ([26 CFR 1.988-3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.988-3)). On-chain trading carries the heaviest burden because the DeFi broker reporting rule was repealed in April 2025, so nothing is reported for you ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-04-21-congress-repeals-digital-asset-regulations-applicable-to-decentralized-finance-platforms)). By researcher arithmetic on cited rates, 60/40 treatment in the 24% bracket gives a blended federal rate of 18.6% against 24% for short-term spot gains, a saving that leverage, funding and fees can easily outweigh.

## Crypto versus forex for a bot: more movement against better structure

| Dimension | Crypto (BTC, ETH, SOL) | Forex (majors) |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | Bitcoin 30-day implied volatility about 36 to 38 in Aug-Sep 2026 after a spike near 100 in Feb 2026 ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-volatility-is-in-meltdown-but-downside-protection-still-commands-a-premium-202608101104)) | EUR/USD 3-month realised volatility about 4.5%, near a five-year low ([FXStreet/Commerzbank](https://fxstreet.com/news/euro-policy-risks-could-revive-swings-against-us-dollar-commerzbank-202611020632)) |
| Retail round-trip cost | 160-180 bp taker on entry-tier Coinbase or Kraken spot; a few bp on regulated futures (exact figure unverified) | About 1.2 bp on OANDA EUR/USD at a 1.4-pip spread; about 1.4 bp on CME micro futures as a taker (researcher arithmetic; exchange fee unverified) ([OANDA US pricing](https://oanda.com/us-en/trading/our-pricing)) |
| Cost relative to a day's move | Cheap only if all-in round-trip cost is under roughly 7-8 bp (researcher arithmetic) | About 4% of one day's standard deviation (researcher arithmetic) |
| Evidence of a retail-accessible edge | Daily trend following: positive but contested, bull-regime dependent | None with clean net-of-cost support since about 2016 |
| Market depth and competition | Maturing fast; carry and arbitrage already competed away | $9.6 trillion a day, the most professionally arbitraged market ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/statistics/rpfx25_fx.htm)) |
| Hours | 24/7; weekends thin since spot ETFs ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2024-06-30-bitcoin-sees-all-time-low-weekend-trading-levels-following-etf-approvals-kaiko-302705)) | Sunday to Friday with weekend gap risk |
| Shorting and leverage for a US person | Spot is mostly long-only; shorting needs CME futures or the new US perpetuals | 50:1 cap on majors; first-in-first-out, no hedging in one account |
| Counterparty protection | No FDIC or SIPC cover; venue failure history | Registered dealers with $20 million minimum capital and public disclosures, but no segregation claim; cleared CME alternative |
| Default tax treatment | Spot: property, short-term ordinary rates, per-fill reporting. Dated futures: Section 1256 | Spot: Section 988 ordinary. CME futures: Section 1256 by default |
| Simplest unattended API | CCXT or official exchange SDK with a trade-only key | OANDA v20 (HTTPS token, no desktop terminal) |
| Historical data | Free exchange candles per venue; paid tick data | No consolidated tape; free data is one venue's quotes |
| Scam ecosystem | Trojanised bot repos, drainer contracts, custodial Telegram bots | Unverifiable "expert advisors", signal sellers, prop-firm challenges where about 7% of accounts ever receive a payout ([Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/exclusive-only-7-of-300000-prop-trading-accounts-achieved-payouts)) |

One venue point changes the forex picture materially. Interactive Brokers quotes the tightest spot forex pricing, but states that US clients "generally must be Eligible Contract Participants", broadly meaning assets over $10 million, to trade leveraged forex, so for a small US account it is a currency-conversion venue and not a place to run a long-short forex bot ([IBKR FAQ](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/support/faq/content/am-resident-u-s-trade-67416550)). The page could only be read through a search extract and sits in tension with IBKR's own reported 23,000 retail forex accounts, so confirm with the broker. The workable US routes are OANDA for very small size and the easiest automation, or CME micro futures (the Micro EUR/USD contract is 12,500 euros) through a futures broker for exchange clearing and default Section 1256 treatment ([CME M6E specs](https://cmegroup.com/trading/fx/e-micros/e-micro-euro_contractSpecs_futures.html)).

The judgement: crypto is the more promising of two unpromising fields for a small systematic bot, because it moves enough for a slow signal to clear costs and because one strategy family has some net-of-cost support. Forex offers better counterparties, a cleared exchange alternative and cleaner tax treatment through futures, but less evidence of exploitable inefficiency in the pairs retail can trade, and its small moves only become meaningful returns through leverage, which is what turns tail events into account-ending losses. Research on the 2010 US leverage cap found it cut high-leverage traders' losses by 40% ([Heimer and Simsek](https://nber.org/papers/w24176)). No study directly compares net-of-cost retail systematic results in the two markets.

## A recommended stack and four gated stages

The recommendations in this section are a synthesis and a judgement, not findings from any single source, and they are not investment advice.

### The stack

For research, use Python with polars or pandas and DuckDB for data, CCXT for exchange candles, vectorbt for fast parameter sweeps (its Commons Clause licence does not restrict private research), and skfolio or pypbo for purged cross-validation, probability of backtest overfitting and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio. Because fast sweeps multiply the number of trials, every parameter set tried should be logged and the overfitting statistics computed on the full set. Survivors should be re-tested in an event-driven engine with realistic fills: NautilusTrader's stable version 1 line, or freqtrade with `--timeframe-detail` and its look-ahead and recursive checks.

For live trading there are two defensible paths. The simplest is **freqtrade on Kraken spot**, long or flat, at daily frequency: it has the largest community, a mature dry-run mode, exchange-resident stop-losses on Kraken, and drawdown and stop-loss guard protections, and at a few trades a month Kraken's 0.40% / 0.80% entry tier is bearable. The structurally better path is **regulated futures through Interactive Brokers using NautilusTrader or LEAN**: Micro Bitcoin futures (0.1 bitcoin per contract) and micro forex futures give lawful shorting, per-contract fees, central clearing and settled Section 1256 treatment ([CME MBT overview](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/lessons/micro-bitcoin-futures-product-overview.html)). Its costs are complexity, a locally running gateway session, a slow-moving Python wrapper, and a contract size that a small account must check against its capital before anything else. The Coinbase nano perpetual-style contract (0.01 bitcoin) is smaller ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/363796/coinbase-launches-perpetual-futures)), and Coinbase exposes its US futures through the same Advanced Trade API its official SDK wraps ([Coinbase CDP docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/advanced-trade-api/rest-api/futures/list-futures-positions)), but framework support is unconfirmed and its tax treatment is unresolved. For forex at very small size, OANDA through LEAN or a direct REST client is the practical choice. Avoid building on Backtrader, ib_insync, Blankly or any Solana sniper code.

### Strategy families worth testing first, and those to avoid

Test first, in this order. Daily time-series trend following on bitcoin and ether, long or flat, with volatility-targeted sizing and an ensemble of breakout lookbacks so no single parameter carries the result; SOL can be added with the caveat that no coin-specific evidence exists. Next, a long-only momentum tilt across the 10 to 15 most liquid coins a US venue lists, rebalanced no more often than weekly, understanding that the published effect relied on a short leg and that its strongest supporting paper is now disputed. Third, and only on the futures path, low-leverage trend following on micro forex futures as a diversifier with an expected return near zero after costs. Carry or basis is worth monitoring but not running continuously, since the yield is currently near the Treasury-bill rate and rises together with crash risk.

Avoid: anything sub-hour, including scalping and order-book signals; market making on US spot venues; grid, martingale and averaging-down "recovery" bots in either market; mean reversion on the major coins; cross-venue and triangular arbitrage; MEV, sniping and memecoin trading; Meteora or other concentrated-liquidity provision on volatile pairs; reinforcement-learning agents and LLM decision loops as the source of signals; copy trading; community strategies such as NostalgiaForInfinity run as-is; forex expert advisors bought from marketplaces; and prop-firm challenges.

### The stages

| Stage | Purpose | What happens | Gate to move on (suggested) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | Decide whether any candidate deserves capital | State the hypothesis before testing; use next-bar fills, non-zero slippage and the actual fee tier you will pay; run look-ahead checks; log every trial; hold back a final untouched period | The strategy survives pessimistic costs, the overfitting statistics on all trials, and the hold-out, and its drawdown in the worst historical regime is one you would tolerate |
| 2. Paper trading | Prove the plumbing, not the edge | Run dry-run on live data for weeks on the real venue; exercise restarts, outages and the kill switch | Paper fills and signals match a backtest of the same period; reconciliation, alerts and kill switch all work when tested deliberately |
| 3. Small live | Measure real fills, fees and tax records | Minimum size, no leverage, dedicated sub-account, trade-only key; export fills daily | Live results track the backtest of the same period within a tolerance set in advance; no unexplained position or order discrepancies |
| 4. Scale | Increase size slowly | Step up capital in increments while keeping venue balance capped | Pre-set stop conditions: a drawdown deeper than the worst in the backtest, or sustained divergence from it, sends the bot back to stage 2 |

A candid limit applies to stages 2 and 3: a daily trend strategy trades a few times a month, so weeks of paper or small-live trading can validate operations but cannot statistically confirm an edge. Confirming a modest Sharpe ratio takes years of data, which is why the research stage and the pre-set stop conditions carry most of the weight.

### Safety controls an unattended bot needs

The controls below are assembled from incident reports, exchange guidance and practitioner sources; no regulator or standards body publishes an authoritative retail checklist. On the code side, use only the canonical repository linked from a project's own documentation, read install scripts before running them, pin exact dependency versions with hashes, install with lifecycle scripts disabled, and run the bot in a dedicated machine or container with no browser, personal wallets or SSH keys and with outbound traffic limited to the exchange's API hosts. CCXT ships almost daily, and four of the five US spot venues changed fees during 2026, so version pinning and a calendar check of fee schedules both matter.

On the account side, fund a dedicated sub-account with risk capital only and keep the rest off the venue; scope the key to trade and read, with withdrawals, internal transfers and earn or pool permissions off and an IP allow-list ([Binance security blog](https://www.binance.com/cs/blog/security/5461902802472279002)); restrict the tradable universe to liquid majors, since the 3Commas drain depended on illiquid pairs; and do not hold collateral in yield-bearing or synthetic stablecoins, given that one traded near $0.65 on a single venue during the October 2025 crash ([Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/cryptos-flash-crash-liquidation-binance-adl-auto-deleveraging)).

On the operational side, enforce limits outside the strategy code: maximum position, order size, orders per hour, daily loss and leverage (none for a first deployment). Add a tested kill switch that cancels orders, flattens positions and disables the key; a heartbeat on an independent host that alerts if the bot goes silent; reconciliation of local state against exchange positions at startup and periodically; idempotent order identifiers so a restart cannot duplicate orders; a stale-data guard; and exchange-resident stop orders with a periodic check that they still exist, since one developer's post-mortem describes a stop that silently vanished ([dev.to](https://dev.to/xqliu/two-losses-two-systemic-bugs-automated-trading-post-mortem-from-okx-and-hyperliquid-427l)). The Knight Capital failure, a $460 million loss in 45 minutes from stale code with 97 warning emails ignored, remains the template for what missing deployment controls and unrouted alerts cost ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the premise of the viral post. Tools are the abundant, commoditised part of this problem, and the scarce parts are a signal that survives a few basis points of cost, a venue a US person can lawfully use at a bearable fee, and the discipline to keep keys and limits tight. For a small US account the fee schedule quietly makes the strategic decision: at entry-tier spot fees only strategies that trade a few times a month are even eligible, and those are also the only ones with supporting evidence. The frontier worth watching is the onshoring of perpetuals during 2025 and 2026, which could bring intraday costs down by an order of magnitude; today that frontier lacks framework connectors, a settled tax answer and a completed legal challenge, so it is a reason to design for portability, not a reason to build on it yet.

The honest expected value of this project, on the evidence, is a modest loss or a result close to holding the underlying asset with lower drawdowns, plus a real education in systems engineering. The November 2026 re-audit of a journal-published crypto factor is the most useful single data point for calibrating trust: if a refereed result can shrink to nothing under a code review, every backtest produced at home deserves the same suspicion. A bot built to survive, with small size, no leverage, trade-only keys and pre-committed stop conditions, keeps the cost of finding out low. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice, and the registration, state-availability and tax-election questions flagged above belong with a qualified professional before real money is committed.
